On leaving the Club in 2006 Brendan Allen provided the Club with a summary of some of his thoughts for the future of the course. He was strongly in favour of the Windsor Green introduction, gave pointers as to the management techniques he had found most successful with it, and felt that the fairways would continue to improve over several years as the Windsor Green dominates and weed invasion by Poa annua and other species is reduced. He was also supportive of the strategic development plan provided earlier by Alex Glasgow and adopted by the Club in General Meeting as a blueprint for future development. Brendan stated that the plan was sensibly aimed at meeting the needs of the mid to longer handicapped player, the emphasis of the plan being on modernisation rather than development.

The message was to make the most of what we have, not aim for something we cannot be. In the context of Brendan’s comments it is interesting to note that George Kinghorn, one of our ‘young guns’, wrote to the Board in 2004 to suggest he felt the Board should concentrate on providing better course conditions rather than attempting to provide additional length and difficulty. George felt that the present course design provided a great environment and a good test of golfing prowess for Club members and visitors alike. Many a Club member would endorse those sentiments with enthusiasm.

Bunkers on the course were a topic of much official debate at this time. They have probably been the topic of many an unofficial debate throughout the history of the Club. Brendan was of the opinion that the feature bunker on the Knoll should be eliminated for a number of reasons, most important of which was that it was a danger to staff when operating machines. Eventually the bunker was remodelled and now appears to be destined to survive as an important visual feature on this signature hole even though it is the destroyer of many a promising round. One of the justifiable criticisms of this bunker is that it severely penalises the poorer player amongst us while having virtually no impact at all upon the play of the more talented.

This is in conflict with the valid theory that hazards should be placed so as to penalise the good player who strays rather than the poorer player who is well capable of penalising himself without the need for course intervention. The visual nature and the strategic position of this particular bunker probably determine that it remain as ‘the exception that proves the rule’.

Brendan’s parting shot was to the effect that the bunker upgrade program formulated by the Course Development Group should be put into action immediately as “further member input will only unnecessarily delay the process”. Most of the program was proceeded with though not to the satisfaction of all.

While bunkers do add considerably to the workload of the Greens staff they also serve a number of important roles. Commander Harris claimed they could be placed so as to act as lighthouses guiding the safe route to the fairway or green. A number of those he designated to serve that purpose have been removed, presumably because they were considered to serve no useful purpose and added to the cost of course upkeep. Not all would believe their removal was justified or beneficial. The role of bunkers as hazards is often poorly understood, perhaps because of the pristine condition of most seen on Television, and because the extremely talented players seen there have so little trouble recovering from them.
