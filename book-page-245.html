Unfortunately Alan was destined to strike disaster in the third round and thus drop from contention as more favoured contenders such as Charles, Marsh, Wolstenholme and Lister made their way up the leader board. This year the tournament had drawn the usual contingent of Australian and New Zealand Professionals but was made more International by the presence of a strong group of Americans headed by PGA Tour winner Larry Ziegler who was at that time 37th on their money list. Included in the American contingent were three young men who arrived in the belief that they were entered in this tournament and in the PGA Championship at the Mount.

It transpired they had been let down by their New Zealand contact and organisers here had not heard of them. Fortunately the NZPGA and local organisers were flexible enough to allow their late entries and they were included in both fields. One member of this group, Jim Albus, responded by heading the first day leader board with a grand 64, six under par, a score he maintained to the end of the tournament to finish in eighth place. Years later Albus was to feature prominently on the Seniors Tour in America over a number of seasons. Fellow American Ziegler may well have won the tournament but for a poor round of 74 sandwiched between a 67 and a final pair of 65s.

Bob Charles and Graham Marsh, brother of Australian wicket keeper Rodney, were paired in a Trans Tasman battle that enthralled the large gallery that accompanied them throughout the afternoon in spite of rain that fell midway through the round. The contest became a virtual singles match between the pair

until Wolstenholme, playing three holes ahead, looked likely to upset that scenario as he rose to the top of the leader board with just five holes to play. His bad hole came at the par three 14th but he held his game together over the closing holes to post a twelve under par total of 268. At that stage Charles and Marsh were also twelve under and playing the 16th. Marsh birdied that hole to move into the lead. Uncharacteristically Charles missed a putt of little more than a metre on the 17th to yield second place to Wolstenholme while Marsh gave the gallery a final thrill by playing a great iron shot to the 18th green.

He made no mistake with the resultant two metre putt to round out a convincing victory. His total of 266 was made up of two 67s followed by two 66s, consistent excellence by a grand sportsman.

While the long 13th hole, “The Crescent”, proves the undoing of many a player Graham Marsh rated it his favourite hole. He commented: “I had two eagles, a birdie and a par there. It seems a simple hole but it does not allow you to get away with much”. Most club players would agree with him, at least in regard to the second sentiment.

An interesting incident occurred on the fourth hole during this round when Marsh’s tee shot struck the lip of the front bunker and fell back in. On reaching the green Marsh walked along the green above the bunker and pointed his club at the spot where the ball had struck. In doing so he disturbed a grain or two of sand. On reporting this to Charles the pair became concerned that a rule may have been infringed so the Rules Committee were called upon. After consulting with eye witnesses the committee ruled no advantage had been gained by the inadvertent disturbance of a few grains of sand and no testing of the condition of the hazard had resulted so no penalty would be imposed.

Both Charles, and obviously Marsh, were happy with this decision. Later Larry Ziegler, on hearing of the incident commented: “The committee made the sensible and wise decision. However had it happened on the PGA Tour at home a penalty would have been imposed”.
