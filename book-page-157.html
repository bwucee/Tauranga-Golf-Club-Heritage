This was reduced to $400 000 when the Trust indicated $230 000 was available through them. 2001: Turf advisory consultant David Ormsby met with the Board to discuss the establishment of Cynodon Fairways. 2002: Resolved that the Board Chairman, rather than the President, should chair General meetings of the Club. 2003: Plans were initiated to align the financial year with the membership subscription year. This would confer a number of advantages in budgeting and general administration of the Club. This was ratified at the AGM that year together with a comprehensive revamp of the Club Rules.

Doubts were expressed in relation to the establishment of the experimental Windsor Green fairways. Climatic conditions had not been conducive to a rapid spread of stolons and some Board members were questioning the decision to proceed. Consequently the Board resolved to delay further development until more positive results were seen. 2004: David Ormsby recommended Browntop be encouraged on the greens by facilitating deeper rooting through coring and improving aeration. Greens should be over sown with Brown top at each renovation. The pH level should be low (4.9-5.3), Nitrogen levels moderate, and Potash levels low (less than 3%). Greens Superintendent Brendan Allan presented a comprehensive report on the Windsor Green fairways in which he expressed great confidence in their future under our conditions.

Fairway improvement would be a continual process over several years as the Windsor Green becomes dominant over Poa annua and invasive weeds. In the same paper Brendan praised the Alex Glasgow development plan as being sensibly aimed at the mid to longer handicapped player with an emphasis on modernisation versus development. Brendan’s advice was that we should make the most of what we have, not aim to be something we cannot be. A paper on the upgrade of the Pro Shop was presented. 2005: Golf Autoscore demonstrated their computerised scoring system following a number of improvements they had made following an earlier demonstration in 2002 when several deficiencies had been evident.

The Board decided to purchase the improved system at a cost of $8500 plus service fees of $200 per month. Golf Autoscore was installed in Nov. 2005. and met with immediate acceptance by members and officials alike. 2006: Brendan Allan prepared the course for the first Carrus Professional Tournament in May before

leaving for Auckland Golf Club. His parting comment was that the Club should proceed with the proposed upgrade of bunkers as further member input would only delay the process unnecessarily. His report also recommended the removal of the bunker on the Knoll as it was dangerous to staff, served no purpose other than to penalise poorer players, and was difficult to maintain. The father and son team of Jack and Jay Carter began operations from the renovated Professional Shop. 2009: The Board adopted a new Course Management Policy Document. The degree of success these strategic plans achieve will be revealed as time goes by. Judgement must be reserved at this time.
