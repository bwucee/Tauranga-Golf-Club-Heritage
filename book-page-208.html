At the time I seriously considered throwing the job in but I didn’t. Not long after leaving Noel fell from a ladder while painting his house and died shortly after.

Curly Peters replaced Noel. Gradually my pay increased but not, I think, to the full award rate, and certainly not to reflect the work that I did. The Club membership was always growing in that period. There was talk of further additions to the clubhouse, plans were made and there was another upheaval in the offing. Curly did a good job as secretary though his assistant, Henry Becker a retired post master, did a lot more than his fair share and, upon his retirement I think that Pat Massey did the lion’s share of the work. I carried on cleaning, serving in the bar, and doing the flowers while avoiding getting involved though I could sense Pat was not too happy with her lot at times.

I would sometimes have a few holes of golf during the summer months when things were quiet. I would play round to the 6th on my own, or sometimes with an occasional lone visitor. I missed playing very much. I used to stand at the window overlooking the 6th green or watch the players coming up the slope to the 18th green and wonder if I would have the chance to play again.

Eventually another round of clubhouse extensions was started. The whole upstairs area was doubled in area and a basement area excavated underneath to provide a storage room with lock up facilities for spirits, bottled beer, and the like. This meant a lot more work for me. Vacuuming the lounge and rearranging and cleaning the tables could take all morning. The committee did employ window cleaners to clean the windows both inside and out where previously I had done the inside and occasionally make a special trip to do the outside. A new social committee decided to run dinner dances, a sit down dinner and a band for dancing. That meant another big rise in the work required of me.

We would borrow long tables and extra chairs from the Racing Club while all our small tables would be carried out and stacked on the veranda. I would get people to give a hand but on Sunday morning everything would have to be put back in order to be ready for the players coming in to the bar in the afternoon. Those were very long weekends, especially as some party lovers would stretch their evening out well into the small hours of Sunday morning. It was often 2 am before I got home and then it was back to clean up in the morning. The final result of the extensions was very successful.

While the work was going ahead I would take home off cuts for firewood and there were often other items piled to go to the dump that were too good to waste so I would salvage those too.

Kitchen staff came and went, committees changed year-to-year, new members arrived and older ones

departed but I carried on as usual. The same Vets came to their Annual Tournament, the same players to the Labour Weekend Tournament. It was nice to see their faces year after year. The bar usually ran smoothly. I changed from the old tin box for the cash to a hand operated cash register, then to a fancy one that had me tied in knots for a time. That slowed down service until I got used to it. Getting the big gas cylinder up to the bar was a major effort. Occasionally I would manage to get one of the boys who played to help me up the stairs with it but they would dodge me if they could.

I would then have to back the van up to where the suppliers had delivered the cylinder, manhandle the cylinder into the van, drive the van round to the porch end of the lounge under the Moreton Bay, get the thing out and roll it along the lounge floor and into the bar.
