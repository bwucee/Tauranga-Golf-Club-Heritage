The advantages of such a progression were obvious. As the trailblazer Colin proved well versed and efficient in all that he did for the Club over a lengthy period.

R G (Roy) Bathgate followed Colin through the ranks. Roy was an accountant by profession and brought much of the meticulous nature of that discipline to his work on committee. Serving as Vice Captain in 1958 Roy progressed to the Presidency in 1965. Through no fault of his own Roy served just one year as President before being defeated in a ‘bloodless coup’. Other members of the committee went down with him, some voluntarily withdrawing in protest at what they considered to be an unjustifiable vendetta. For some reason that 1966 Annual Meeting was held at the ‘Down Under’ Coffee Lounge in Central Tauranga. Those present will long remember the meeting for the currents that stirred beneath the surface.

In retrospect the break in committee continuity that followed that evening did not make for a happy and settled Club over the next two or three years.

Jim Reid and his wife Jean were very well known members who began their golf at the old Te Puna Golf Club. It is believed that some members of Te Puna formed the nucleus of what became the Otumoetai Golf Club. The details appear lost in the mists of time. Both Jim and Jean were very supportive of Tauranga Golf Club through the fifties, sixties, and into the seventies. Jean took on the catering role during the fifties and was admired for the quality service she provided. A very keen Intermediate level golfer Jim was known for his advocacy of the ‘corkscrew grip and the K address’. Jim survived the 1966 coup to continue in his role as Captain but it was not until 1970 that he was finally elected President, an honour he had probably earned some years previously.

Jim’s golfing wisdom was recognised on a wider level through his services to Bay of Plenty Golf. There he rose through the ranks to assume the Presidency from 1971-73. Jim was elected to Life Membership of that organisation in 1985 in deserved recognition of his service over many years.

Dr R S (Rod) Rowe was a member of the committee when Bruce Cunningham, at that time Town Clerk to the Mt Maunganui Borough Council, initiated a move to establish a new Golf Club on land adjacent to the Tauranga Airport. There were restrictions on heights and buildings on the area owing to Airport activities. These caused Bruce to believe a golf course might well be the ideal solution. Coincidentally this was at a time in the early Seventies when membership of the two existing courses was at a premium and another course was seen to be a virtual necessity. In 1971 a committee made up of representatives

of the Tauranga and Mt Maunganui clubs was formed to promote the concept. Rod Rowe took a special interest in this and selflessly ignored the prospect of almost certain progression through the committee ranks at Tauranga in order to see the Omanu Golf Club successfully established. He was ultimately to be rewarded with election to the Presidency of the new club.

D M (Des) Wilson joined the committee as Vice Captain to Sol Kanapu in 1969. It was a period when committee posts were turning over rapidly. Des was to move up to Captaincy the following year and to Presidency one year later. Des was not one to be very happy as a ‘Back Bencher’ in any organisation
