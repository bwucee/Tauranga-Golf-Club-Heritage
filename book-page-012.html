Dr. Brown had been a member of the Racecourse Domain Board and had given that role great and successful attention. On the School Committee he had proved himself a tower of strength and had helped them along in a way that no one else could have done. He had taken great interest in Golf and this had been the salvation of many of them. He hoped interest would be kept up. The Mayor then presented the Doctor with a small token of their esteem in the form of a case of pipes, cigar and cigarette holders.

In reply Dr. Brown said it took his breath away to hear the nice things that had been said of him. In presenting him with pipes they were simply pandering to his weakness. They must have noticed his pipes were very shabby. He expressed regret at leaving Tauranga. He and his wife both liked the town and he had hoped to spend the rest of his days here but stress of circumstances was too much for them. He commented on the grand climate of Tauranga and the beautiful scenery and stressed the need for municipal enterprise. Tauranga needed a water supply and other facilities and Mr. Commons, after viewing some municipalities at home in Britain, had come back full of enterprise and go.

With an enterprising Mayor and a live Council Tauranga was bound to go ahead. He said Tauranga was a town without peer in New Zealand. It had a splendid harbour that should be the Port for the Bay and for the Waikato. When he returned he hoped to find a railway station, the town lighted with gas or electricity, and a library built. On the medical side he was leaving in his place Dr. Gault, an excellent man of high professional attainments, high character, and a good fellow. He hoped they would extend the same right-handed fellowship to him as they had done to the speaker during the two or three years he had been here.

Mr Semadini then read a farewell ode written by a ‘Bunkered Poet’.

The poem, while perhaps not of any great literary merit, bears reprinting here as a graphic commentary on those times and the influence Dr. Brown had on the developing community during his short residence in Tauranga. From the poem it becomes apparent that the game of Bridge, as with Golf, owes its origins in the town to the initiative and enthusiasm of Dr Brown.

FAREWELL ODE

By a “Bunkered Poet” We are gathered here this evening,

For a farewell Korero, With our guest the good old Doctor Brown,

But ‘white’ as we all know.

Since he came into our midst,

About two years ago, He’s taught us Bridge, He’s taught us Golf,

And Things we didn’t know. He showed to us the strokes of Golf,
