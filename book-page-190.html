Trees

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 190 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

Bert Goulding planted the Moreton Bay Fig beside the clubhouse around 1920. It is as fine a specimen as can be seen anywhere, including its native Australia, and, though it creates problems for the clubhouse situated alongside, few would be prepared to advocate its removal. Mr Goulding was also responsible for supplying and nurturing the two Canadian Redwoods behind the Knoll.

Joyce Carnachan donated the Jacaranda by the fourth green while Keith Lusk planted the three Kauris on the second tee during the late thirties. The latter three showed little growth until released to the light some twenty years later. The date palms that were once more prevalent on the course originated through the children of one member sneaking dates from the kitchen cupboard and throwing the stones out the window to destroy the evidence. Eventually the stones germinated, the misdemeanour was forgiven, and the young palms transplanted to the course where they flourished unless becoming victim to a misdirected sweep of the mower. Over the years many people have contributed to the planting, nurture, and ongoing care of the trees on the course.

Harry and Maurice Bradmore, Fred Washer, Frank and Maisie Warnes, and Willis Faulds were especially involved during mid-century years when many strategic fairway trees were introduced. Bill Kennedy of the Tree Society, though not a golfer, donated many fine specimens. He was also the driving force behind the development of McLarens Falls Park and has been responsible for much

enhancement of the City and surrounds through the donation of trees and the encouragement given to others to become involved in planting specimen trees. Many other people have donated trees over the years and the tradition continues. Around the turn of the new century members were given the opportunity to donate $100 to purchase suitably selected trees which were then planted, each with a small plaque recognising the donor. This project was very well supported.

Bob Burstall, an authority on trees, was based in Rotorua with the Forest Service. In 1979 he and Club

identity Willis Faulds did a thorough survey of the trees on the course during which they identified 102 different species and estimated there were over 7000 individual trees and shrubs. For some years Mr. Burstall was regularly consulted on the program for the planting, care, removal and replacement of trees. His expertise in that role was invaluable and added much to the development of the property. In recent years the “Firewood Gang”, recognised elsewhere in this publication have undertaken the task of controlling and encouraging the proper growth of trees on the course, an essential task that was sometimes neglected in the past as evidenced by the large number of multi-trunk and poorly shaped trees that we do have.

The work of Jim Swainson has been particularly valuable in this context. “Arbor Care Ltd” has provided annual tree maintenance under contract funded by the Racecourse Reserve Committee. COMMEMORATIVE TREES In addition to being honoured with Life Membership of the Club a commemorative Deodar (Cedrus deodar) was planted between the sixth green and seventh tee as a permanent reminder of the contribution Mr Mirrielees made to the Club. This was the first of a number of commemorative plantings that have been made either by the Club itself, by groups within it, or by family members.
