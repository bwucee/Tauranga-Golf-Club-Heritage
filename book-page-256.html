In 1967 Tauranga staged a match between four of the International Professionals competing in the PGA at Mount Maunganui. A large gallery saw Martin Roesink (Holland) and Tony Jacklin (England) defeat Billy Dunk (Australia) and Resident Professional Jim Paterson two up. The three overseas stars all scored 66s while Paterson was also under par. While Roesink thrilled the crowd with the power of his driving it was Jacklin who particularly impressed for the all round quality of his game. His pure swing and clean struck iron shots had many an astute judge claiming we would hear much more of him in the future. This proved to be accurate as Jacklin went on to win the NZPGA at Mt.

Maunganui that week in an 18-hole play off with Roesink. Far more importantly Tony Jacklin was to restore British golfing pride with victories in both the British and US Opens the following year. He then went on to enjoy a successful career that culminated in appointment to Captaincy of the European Ryder Cup Team.

The exhibition that afternoon began with a clinic on the first tee. Bob MacDonald, a popular Professional from Wellington acted as Master of Ceremonies in between amusing the large audience with his comic imitations of well known golfing identities. His show, in itself, would have made the afternoon worthwhile.

Martin Roesink demonstrated his driving ability by playing a number of drives. His first two were slightly faded and finished at the base of the second tee. The next was pin high to the left of the first green, the last three were all through the back of the green. It was a spectacular demonstration of power hitting. This was with a wooden headed, steel shafted driver, and using the rubber-wound ball. It is difficult to imagine where these drives would have reached had he been using equipment equivalent to that available today.

THE BILLY CASPER SHOW

When Billy Casper visited New Zealand in 1969 Tauranga had the opportunity to invite one of the World’s very best players to demonstrate his talents to our members and to interested members of the public. Casper, along with Bob Charles, was recognised as one of the great putters of all time, yet he was very much more than that. During his career he registered 51 Professional victories including three majors, the 1970 Masters at Augusta complementing his two United States Opens. He appeared in eight Ryder Cup teams registering more points than any other player and was described by Johnny Miller as being the “most underrated golfer of all time, hands down”.

It is difficult to see why he was so underrated as he competed extremely successfully in an era during which Ben Hogan was still active while stars such as Arnold Palmer and Gary Player were enjoying their best years.

For his visit to Tauranga Casper was scheduled to put on an exhibition clinic before Lunch then play an 18-hole match partnering the newly crowned Club Champion Jim Lancaster against Club professional Jim Paterson and Noel Hayden of Rotorua. Lancaster, at that stage the youngest winner of our championship aged 19, was given the honour of partnering Casper against the two club Professionals. Defeated championship finalist John McLeod was invited to caddy for the American and advise him on features of the golf course, as Casper had no prior knowledge of any of the holes.
