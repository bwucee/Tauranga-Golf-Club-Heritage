Women’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 91 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

1947: A recommendation from the AGM that three starting tees be used in turn by the three grades was discussed. (At the time the Ladies were always drawn in their own handicap grades). A secret ballot was held to decide which day was favoured as Match day. The size of the Ladies’ Committee was increased to thirteen. (How did they ever reach an agreed decision?) 1948: A new Local Rule was adopted for the Fourth Hole: ‘if a player from the tee plays her first ball from the gully she has the option of playing it or picking it up. If she decides to play the first ball, and takes a stroke, she must hole out with that ball’.

(Was this a product of a committee of thirteen? Thankfully we now have better worded local rules - comprehending this one must have been more difficult than playing the hole).

1950: Competition fees were raised to 15/- for the season. Locker fees were set at 10/- pa. Another Local Rule was adopted for the troublesome Fourth Hole: “a ball played from the tee failing to reach cut fairway is Out of Bounds. Play second shot from the tee and, in the event of the first ball passing through the rough grass at the top bank, first ball is in play”. (Was this really considered to be an improvement?).

1952: There was a Special General Meeting called to discuss the notice of motion that starting times on Club day (Wednesday) be from 12.00 to 12.45. This was not agreed to. A Ways and Means Committee was to be established to raise funds for the Building program planned to alter and enlarge the clubhouse. The hole-in-one fund fee was 1/- pa reducing to 6d the following year if not collected. (Anyone successful was awarded the entire pool with which to ‘shout’. On occasion someone would be unfortunate enough to hole out soon after another had done so only to find the ‘cupboard was bare’).

1953: It was determined that the results of Ladies’ competitions and other Club news should be published in the BOP Times.

1954: The hole-in-one fund fee was increased to 1/6d. A new Local Rule was adopted for play of the Eighth Hole. It read: “should the tee shot hit the second power pole the tee shot may be replayed. In addition if any pole interfere with the line to the green the ball may be moved away to a safe position no nearer the hole”. A vote established Club day as being Wednesday. 1955: Dr. Jean Reading established a competition for Girls of school age in order to encourage wider participation by them. The BOP/TV Ladies’ Association split from the Waikato/King Country Association. It was suggested that the social side of competition day was being lost with the system of morning and afternoon start times.

A three-tee start at 10.30 was promoted as a way of rectifying the problem.

1956: The Saturday Ladies’ start time had been agreed to from 9.30 to 10.30am. There had not been enough interest to continue with Jean Reading’s School Age competitions.
