More distant fairways

As soon as the initial work involved in developing the rudiments of a golf course was underway Mr Fred Rutter, an Auckland Professional, was engaged to lay out 18 holes utilising much of the present land together with some land lying within the racecourse oval. A description of that first course will be given in a later chapter.

Golf in New Zealand was essentially a winter game and in summer the fairways at Tauranga were devoted to the production of hay crops. In March the greens would be mown down in preparation for the new season and a single-reel, horse drawn mower would occasionally be run over the fairways to complement the work of the grazing stock.

Secretary/Treasurer Mr H B Mountfort would go around town in January/February to ask the members to pay their subscriptions, £4 at that time, so that he could engage Mr McKinlay to mow the greens in early March and have the funds to pay him.

The first ‘clubhouse’ was a corrugated iron shed located where the eleventh tee now sits. It served as the jockey’s room on race days and must have been a miserable location on wet winter race days.3

Forms for seating and a long table took up almost all the available space in the ‘Tin Shed’ while a

smaller table accommodated the primuses and kettles. Someone donated a tatty looking basket style armchair that was squeezed into the corner and the first player in for lunch would stake his claim and sit in isolated splendour while the rest sat around the table eating their paper-wrapped sandwiches and sipping their tea. Sometime later a cookhouse was built at the end of the building and an Orion stove installed. Members would collect firewood off the course and fire up the new stove so the days of the primus ended and a new age of convenience began!

At first few members owned cars and many cycled to the course with their clubs on their backs. This was quite a trip as the course was some distance from their downtown residences. A lucky few sometimes succeeded in scrounging a ride in a car or gig. Until as late as 1957 the entry road from Cameron Road was a metal surface prone to severe potholing. The first improvement was made in 1958 when sealing was laid to 1.5 car widths over the first 20 chains of road (about 400m). The final 250m was not done until 1960. The Domain Board, as it was then known, financed this roadwork from the rental received from the two tenants.

Such expenditures had to be well planned and budgeted in advance. The Club owes a very real debt of gratitude to the members of the Racecourse Domain Board and its successor, the Racecourse Reserve Committee, for their insight and astute financial management over the history of our time on the Gate Pa course.

There was no clash between racegoers and golfers for no one would have dreamed of playing golf on a race day. For one thing the races were a major social occasion when the men wore their finest suits and no self-respecting lady would attend without having first visited Amy Hogg’s establishment in Devonport Road to acquire a fashionable ensemble. In any event the Racing Club had the right to park cars on the golf course and it was not until the Sixties that this ceased to be the situation.
