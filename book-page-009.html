Setting the scene

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 9 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

recognised as the father of New Zealand Golf Charles Howden founded The New Zealand Distilling Company to produce whisky in Dunedin. Howden left New Zealand in 1881 and, with his departure, the Dunedin Golf Club appears to have expired after its short ten-year life. Eight years later Howden returned to Dunedin and, in 1889, was instrumental in setting up the Otago Golf Club at Balmacewan. That club continues as a major club today. Notable in the list of inaugural members of the original Dunedin Golf Club was a Dr W. M. Brown who later served on the committee of the club that succeeded it, the Otago Golf Club.

Development of the game in New Zealand was very rapid and by the outbreak of World War Two Tauranga was just one of some eighty clubs affiliated to the New Zealand Golf Association that had been established in 1910. Even more rapid expansion followed the cessation of war in 1918 and by 1929 the number of clubs had swelled to 159. Today it is claimed we boast more clubs per head of population than any other country with the possible exception of Scotland where it all began. Certainly the game here is available and affordable to almost all who wish to play, something not true in most parts of the World.

On the subject of costs a 1928 edition of New Zealand Golf Illustrated published an average annual golfing budget for the guidance of those contemplating taking up the game. The total cost per year was estimated at £16/2/6 made up of club fees (£6/10/0), balls (£5), repairs (£1), and refreshments (£3/12/6). This annual cost would perhaps be less than some would now spend on a single afternoon at the club. While no comment was made on the cost of equipment to get you started we can be confident that £10 would have done you proud. Based on these figures refreshments represented about 22% of a player’s annual expenditure. I wonder what proportion of the average member’s annual expenditure is now represented by the cost of refreshments.

Over the years there have been many holes in one, some more unusual than others. Perhaps Harry Jeffreys began it all in the early days when the fifth tee was located inside the Racecourse oval. The green was where the grandstand now stands. Harry made a mighty heave at the ball only succeeding in topping it so that it never became truly airborne. In spite of this it scrambled across the racetrack, hit a tree trunk, straightened up after striking a fence post, trickled up the hill onto the green, and finally dribbled exhausted into the cup. The perfect shot!

Perhaps a more notable hole in one was that scored by Miss Rachell Baker in October 1940 when she holed her tee shot at the notorious Knoll. Apart from the personal thrill associated with the perfect stroke Miss Baker had the distinction of being the first woman to achieve the feat on the course. To commemorate her achievement the Ladies of the Club presented Miss Baker with an engraved silver goblet. I wonder where that goblet is today
