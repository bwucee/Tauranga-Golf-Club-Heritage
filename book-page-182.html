In spite of these and other objections Michael, then Chairman of the Greens Committee, finally got the OK from the committee to proceed with a sanding program. Surprisingly this was not to be a trial using the putting green, or some other area, for a year or two to prove the worth or otherwise of the program. The risky approach of committing all the greens to the program was instead embarked upon from the beginning. Fortunately the doubters were soon proven to be wrong and the program was here to stay with obvious on-going advantage to the course and its members.

Sports Turf preparation is both an Art and a Science. Those charged with that preparation make decisions that prove advantageous at some times and not at others. Tauranga Golf Club has been fortunate that the long-term trend resulting from the totality of decisions reached has been one of steady improvement to the general benefit of both present and future members. The decision to sand greens, the installation of an adequate irrigation system, the establishment of Windsor Green fairways, and the instigation of daily mowing of greens have been amongst the most significant decisions made by the Club during its first one hundred years. While each has been made in the face of opposition, each has proven to be of enormous benefit.

In March 1984 it was reported that the greens were being mown four days per week. This was a clear improvement on the twice a week schedule that had prevailed for many years. It was considered that Saturday morning mowing would be beneficial but the cost at $60 per time, or $3000 per year, was prohibitive. Sunday morning mowing would be even more costly. It was not until the 1980s that daily mowing, including weekends, was instituted. The availability of better machinery allowed this to be completed quickly and efficiently. No one would have contemplated such a program in the days of the Over-green mowers when the operator walked behind the machine.

Throughout our history there have been those who disagree with the way our course has been managed. It may be difficult to appreciate that fine turf greens cannot be kept in ‘tournament condition’ 52 weeks of the year. The courses the Professionals play on the USA and European PGA Tours are brought to a peak for those events. This process may take place over several months. It is the same for the major events we stage. Turf grasses do not respond well to constant close mowing and rolling but this is what is required to produce tournament greens that are fast and true. The Greens Supervisor must temper the desire of members to experience ‘tournament conditions’ on a regular basis with the need to protect the health of the greens throughout the year.

Sadly some Club officials over the years have not appreciated this and have agitated for conditions that are simply unsustainable. Should such views ever prevail the long term consequences would be serious indeed.
