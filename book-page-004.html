INTRODUCTION

The gestation period of this Centennial history has been a long one. The early history of the Club was first compiled by the late Miss Joan Mirrielees in preparation for the Golden Jubilee of the club in 1961 but was not published owing to a lack of funding. Miss Mirrielees was a long time Club member, a teacher and an historian. Following that earlier compilation Miss Mirrielees wrote to the 1974 committee offering to prepare a ‘complete history of the club’. This offer was accepted with enthusiasm, especially in view of the fact that the club was believed to be approaching its Diamond Jubilee year. The celebration of that milestone in 1986 seemed to cement the year 1911 as the foundation year of the club though there was evidence of a Golf Club in the town somewhat earlier.

The move to the present course did not take place until 1914 when the existing club decided to take up an available lease on a portion of the Racecourse Reserve. 1911 appears to have been chosen from a perusal of the original championship honours boards, as this was the first year in which a club champion was officially recognised. Perhaps this represented a sensible compromise although it has now become clear that the club does indeed date back to 1904. Incidentally the honour of winning that first championship belonged to Mr A Matheson who repeated the feat the following year, and again in 1921 and 1927.

The major difficulty facing Miss Mirrielees and subsequent researchers has been a serious lack of documented and minuted history. At some time in the decade of the Forties the club secretary also acted as secretary to a local authority and rumour has it that there was a problem related to the funds of that authority and the said gentleman left town in undue haste, though not before taking the time to destroy significant records of both organisations. As it is impossible to substantiate these rumours so long after the event it may be best to leave the suspect unnamed. Whatever the truth of the matter no minute books recording the first thirty years or so of our history have been discovered.

Fortunately Miss

Mirrielees was able to contact a number of older residents and members who were able to recall some of the earlier history. Reference material gleaned from copies of the BOP Times held by the Tauranga City Library yielded further vital information.

As an indication of the difficulties faced in researching relevant material it was not until 1985 that the President was able to report to committee that Miss Mirrielees had made significant progress on the task. This enabled the President, Dr Eric Hutcheson, to give an interesting after dinner address on the early history of the club during the Diamond Jubilee celebrations held in 1986.

Miss Mirrielees died in 1989 without the satisfaction of seeing her work go to print .She did however leave a considerable amount of valuable material she had collected from a variety of sources. In time this material was passed on to Mrs Deidre Hartley who, in 1992, expressed the desire to produce a booklet in time for the 90th anniversary planned for June 2001. Over a period Deidre added both to the research and to the developing written text. In this endeavour members of the Ladies’ committee assisted Deidre by preparing and distributing questionnaires to members, and by checking through old documents.
