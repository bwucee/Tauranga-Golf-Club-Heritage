Four down in almost as many holes Ashley suddenly came alive to win comfortably with holes to spare. Elsewhere the standard of golf was very high for Intermediate players, most having to shoot close to, or under, 80 to succeed. As the final match approached the 18th green Peter Chadwick’s approach finished to the right of the green with a set of gang mowers intervening on his line of play. When no relief was offered the rest of his team dragged the offending mowers aside and Peter chipped successfully to remain square.

With two wins, two halves, and two losses the teams finished tied on three points each so a sudden death play off was required. Team members looked at one another, most none too keen to carry the responsibility. Finally someone said: ‘Norm’s the man for the job’. And so he was. There would be few more competitive players at that level than was Norman Robson. His nerve held as he calmly parred the first extra hole while Johnny Tai, of Opotiki, three putted from well away from the pin. The final chapter in the story came when Norm, arriving at the Tauranga Clubhouse to proudly present the hard earned Pennant, found he did not have it.

Eventually it transpired he had placed it on top of his car while he loaded his clubs at Omanu before driving off. It was never found and another had to be obtained from BOP administrators.

It was to be another 18 years before an Intermediate team was again successful. A team for which Ray Pittams, Mike Sherratt, Colin Gray, Ralph Bills, Steve Stephens, Gary Baker, Brent Hickisey, Chris Best,

Alan Bush, Nigel Rose, and Andrew Reid all appeared at some time throughout the pennant season won the final played at Te Puke in 1998. The team was player-managed by Bruce Simister, a man who gave much to the encouragement and management of pennant teams over many years. Further success followed in 2003 when Tauranga scored the trifecta with success at Senior Championship, Intermediate, and Junior level, an epic achievement in our history. Members of the Intermediate team were Robbie Aitken, Ralph Bills, Jim Costello, Colin Gray, Ian Humble, Chris McAlpine, Klaus Niermann, Mike Sherratt, and Paul Smith. The Junior team that year included Alan Brown, Roger Craw, Graham

Fowler, Ian Given, David Hermitage, Roy MacGregor, Ernie Pratt, and Bernie Priestley.

Junior teams were successful four times in the old Inter-Club Shield years, bringing home the trophy the first year it was held in 1950 and again in 1955, 56, and 1965. Since the Pennant format was introduced in 1971 they have been victorious in 1983, 87, and 2003. The 1983 team were successful in the final at Whakatane in the last year before the Zone system began. The Tauranga team included a number of ‘grey haired gentleman’ including Bill Good and Jack Hill. At the presentation someone made the observation that Tauranga had at last won their first Junior Pennant since the series began. A wag at the back called out: ‘it looks as though the same team has been trying all those years’.

Junior golfers do not get many opportunities to compete in a team contest over a full weekend so the BOP Pennant finals provide a memorable occasion for them. This was certainly the case for the 1987 Junior team who competed against the other Zone winners in the final at Lake View. Tauranga team members were Peter Brown, Bob Eaton, Kevin Moynahan, John Robinson, Neville Robinson, and David Mowat. Gary Hardaker had been an important member earlier in the season before his handicap moved down a division.
