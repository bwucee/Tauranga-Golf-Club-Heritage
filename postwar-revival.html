1946–1951

A course restored, a membership growing

Returning players and increasing membership brought new energy—and new demands on the club.

After the war, members worked to restore the full eighteen-hole course. The history associates that effort with returning servicemen and women eager to resume peacetime pursuits.

By 1951 the book records 416 members, an unprecedented total for the club. The wooden clubhouse that had opened in 1938 was becoming too small for the number of people using it.

The next phase included plans for a new building and continued improvement in course care. Grant Silvester took over as head greenkeeper in 1951, beginning a long association with the course.

This was more than a recovery of the prewar routine. It set up the clubhouse project, the Veterans’ Society and the expanded tournament programme that would make the 1950s such an active decade. The accompanying image shows the clubhouse built in 1954, after this period of growth.

Access improved as well: the book records sealing the first section of the entrance road in 1958, with the remaining section completed in 1960. The latter improvement falls just beyond this chapter.
