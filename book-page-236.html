Don Stevenson one morning bought a new ball at the Pro Shop when Jim Paterson was the incumbent. Don teed up and promptly toed his new ball back into the shop, breaking a large window on the way. Many will remember Don’s wife Sylvie who was an active and very popular member over at least forty years commencing in the early sixties.

Good as it was Godfrey’s record was to last only as long as it took Terry Leech of Taupo to complete his afternoon round. Leech (son of legendary Taupo Green Keeper Charley Leech) had played the first two rounds very well in 70 and 72 to trail Ted McDougall by four strokes in the Amateur section of the tournament. Heavy overnight rain resulted in the greens playing slow and the fairways long on the Sunday. To add to these conditions drizzly rain fell throughout the afternoon. In spite of this Terry Leech played some of the greatest golf ever seen on the course during his round of 65.

His outward nine of 34, thanks to a birdie at the long seventh, did not suggest what was to follow on the final, more difficult, second nine. Birdies at the 10th, 11th, 13th, and 15th, together with an eagle three on the short par five 17th were offset by a shot dropped at the par three 14th. To annex the Amateur prize Leech needed this score or better as McDougall continued his good form with a one- under-par round of 70 to finish just one stroke back.

Jane Butler, now representing the North Shore Club, won the Women’s section on count back from Miss P Harrison on the strength of a final round of 77, three better than her rival. Incidentally this result would have been reversed had the committee adopted an alternative count back method that gives priority to the lowest round of the tournament. It is obviously important to have all tournament conditions published before the event. An indication of the strength of women’s golf at our own Club was given by the placing of both Olga Johnson and Pat Kidd in the top ten, while Una Wickham was not a competitor that year.

WALTER WINS AGAIN IN 1965

Although he was not able to reproduce the brilliance of the previous year Walter Godfrey again won the Professional section. Commencing with a 72 he improved a stroke in each round to defeat evergreen Ernie Southerden by three strokes. Interestingly the pair showed the same scoring pattern with Ernie starting one stroke higher on 73. In similar vein Terry Leech played steadily without the extra inspiration of the previous year and his four-over-par total of 217 was not good enough to hold out rising star Brian Boys of Hamilton who sandwiched a 75 between two 69s. Tauranga’s Keith Dawkins claimed fourth place, the best performance by a local man in the history of the event, still nine strokes off the pace.

Thanks to an opening round of 71 Miss Natalie White of Auckland, a New Zealand representative, headed Miss Wickham by 10 strokes after the first 18 holes. Using all her experience and wealth of

local knowledge to close the gap Una failed to do so by three strokes. Mrs Olga Johnson, another

local, was two strokes back in third place.
