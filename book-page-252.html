The Spalding Masters and the NZPGA Championship

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 252 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

Eamon Darcy (68, 64), Terry Gale (66, 67), Peter Croker (65, 68) and Ian Stanley (65, 68) were in close attendance. New Zealand’s Bob Charles looked to have his fourth NZPGA Championship safely under control when he scored another 65 on the third day. This left him a comfortable three strokes clear of Ted Ball and Roger Davis, and five clear of Terry Gale and Kel Nagle. Canadian Jerry Anderson was one stroke ahead of the latter two and thus four behind the leader. The only question in the mind of most spectators seemed to be by how many strokes would Charles win. Alas the putter that had served Charles so well throughout his career decided to go cold that day.

Three putts were required at the fifth, a hole that had yielded an ace the day before. Another three-putt followed at the ninth. In spite of these blemishes Charles teed off on the penultimate hole with a lead of two strokes over Gale who was then in the clubhouse and resigned to finishing a stroke or two short of the win. Surprisingly Charles missed from less than two metres at the seventeenth for his third three-putt, and third bogey, of the day leaving him just one stroke clear playing the final hole. There his tee shot was hooked to finish just short of a tree on the right of the fairway.

A good recovery was drawn around the tree to finish in the mouth of the green from where he failed to get down with a chip and putt so a play off was required.

The sudden death play off commenced at the sixteenth where both drives were well placed in the fairway. Charles made the green with his second but was at least 15 metres away while Gale failed to make the green at all. His chip was weak finishing three metres short. Uncharacteristically Charles putted some 1.5 metres beyond the hole only to see Gale roll his longish putt home. The crowd who had braved miserable weather featuring rain and a blustery wind throughout the day were stunned to see Charles pressure putt slide well wide of the hole for his third successive bogey. Gentleman and sportsman as always Charles was quick to congratulate Gale, his sincerity obvious to all in spite of the disappointment he must have felt.

At the start of the day many players had an opportunity to challenge for the title but all, other than the ultimate champion, fell away. Perhaps the weather conditions, so much in contrast to those earlier in the week, may have accounted for the fact that only seven players bettered par. Round of the day went to Peter Crocker whose 66 included four birdies and an eagle. No one else managed an eagle on the day. Amongst those expected to challenge for the title Ball blew his chance by taking three to find the green at the knoll, Davis lost interest rather early dropping shots at the second, fourth and fifth, while Lister, like Davis, shot 69.

Nagle played well enough to be a serious threat but for once the Tauranga course failed to yield much in the way of birdies to the popular Australian. The Canadian Anderson launched the most threatening challenge to Charles and Gale when he birdied the ninth and tenth but bogeys at the 16th and 17th ended his run.

Tauranga Club professional Rick Barker performed well in the Tournament making the cut comfortably with rounds of 68 and 70. Rick finished mid field with a three over par total of 283.

Sadly this 1981 staging of the NZPGA Championship was to prove to be the last held at the Tauranga course. The Championship went into recess for a lengthy period before being resurrected at the Clearwater course in Canterbury in the new Century. It was to be another 25 years before Tauranga again saw a quality field of Amateurs and Professionals contest an Open Championship.
