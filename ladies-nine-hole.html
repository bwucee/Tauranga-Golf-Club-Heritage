1977–2006

A shorter round, a wider welcome

Tauranga played a pioneering role in nine-hole golf, with Phyll Sandlant helping turn an invitation to try the game into a substantial club group.

The book describes a 1977 move to provide nine-hole handicaps for women who found a full round difficult. It presents that local provision as a forerunner of the later organised movement.

In Phyll Sandlant’s account reproduced by Mowat, an advertisement in February 1998 invited women to try nine-hole golf at Tauranga. Ninety-six women registered. Professional Rick Barker provided coaching, with volunteers accompanying beginners and introducing the game’s etiquette and competition formats.

The inaugural BOPTV Honda Nine Hole Tournament followed at Tauranga in 1999. The book credits Phyll and the volunteers who supported her, as well as Alison Holdem and Lynn Goodhew who carried the work forward. Phyll became a life member in 2006.

A separate 2014 centenary report in SunLive identifies Patsy Hankins as instrumental in the national initiative and says she approached Phyll in 1998 to help implement it. That report calls Tauranga the first New Zealand club; Phyll’s account in the book specifically describes the first group in Bay of Plenty. Both sources show Tauranga’s pioneering contribution, while describing the scope differently.

The Ladies 9 Hole collection links this history to the club group’s own records. Present-day membership, programmes and achievements can be added as new, dated contributions rather than assumed from the 2014 account.
