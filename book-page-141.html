Administration

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 141 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

At the fifth hole one summer morning Noel Hockin, then Secretary Manager, holed in one during a round that started before 6 am. This was the day after the Races and punters, as was normal in earlier years, had been parked on the 8th and 9th fairways the previous day. First of his Sunday morning four to climb up to the elevated tee Noel discovered that someone had been “caught short” the previous day and deposited human waste in the centre of the tee. Feeling duty bound to clean up this disgusting mess Noel did so before teeing up and playing the perfect shot which duly reached the bottom of the hole for Noel’s first and only hole in one in 45 years of playing the game.

That day must have proven to be an awfully long one for Noel who was never one to shy away from a celebratory drink or three.

The advent of the Board of Management in 1994 inevitably resulted in the committee becoming redundant in the form under which it had existed over the previous ninety years. This made little difference to the administration of specific groups such as the Women, the Veterans, or the Thursday players. It did have quite a profound influence on the administration of Men’s competition golf. Where previously a Match Committee, the members of which were drawn from the Club Committee, had supported the Captain and his Deputy they now became part of a stand-alone group with restricted responsibilities. Further these officers had once been part of a strong committee responsible for all aspects of Club management.

Now they had responsibilities essentially limited to managing Men’s competition golf including representative golf. This appeared to make the job less attractive and it became difficult to find people willing to stand for positions on the Men’s Committee. In addition life styles had changed and many members, through work and family commitments, could not find the time to make themselves available for committee work. Those who did step forward often stayed for only a year or two with the result that continuity was tenuous at best. This was an unfortunate and unexpected disadvantage arising from the introduction of the Board system of Club governance. Gradually the problem resolved itself as the Saturday men came to realise the importance of consistent leadership and a level of continuity began to flow through their administrative structure once more.

The concept of a Board of Management to control the financial aspects of the Club began to be discussed seriously in the early nineties as other Golf Clubs around New Zealand began to adopt this form of management. An informal investigative group consisting of Ted Bold, Rex Edmonds, Barry Jensen, and Bruce Simister reported to Committee in August 1994. They recommended that a Board of Management be established to handle the business aspects of Club management with a separate committee to control the playing and social aspects of club life. Maurice White and David Mowat were appointed to join that group which would then prepare a final document to present to a Special General Meeting of the Club.

Honorary Solicitor Bruce Gordon was of immense value in assisting the group to draft the appropriate wording that would be needed both to present the concept to Club members, and to amend Club rules appropriately in the event the proposals should be adopted.
