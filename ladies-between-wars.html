1937–1956

The work behind the Wednesday game

Committee records show women shaping competition, fundraising and opportunities to play.

The book’s extracts from the Ladies’ minutes record a 1937 discussion about separating the men’s and ladies’ sections. The proposal was not adopted at that time. Later entries show the women organising competitions and undertaking essential work around the clubhouse.

In 1946 businesswomen asked to play their competitions on Saturday afternoons. The source records the request without giving the response. It is a small but telling example of how access to playing time mattered.

The 1952 minutes describe a Ways and Means Committee for the building programme. In 1953, the committee resolved that competition results and club news should be published in the Bay of Plenty Times; in 1954, a vote established Wednesday as club day.

Dr Jean Reading introduced a competition for school-age girls in 1955. The following year the minutes recorded insufficient interest to continue it. This short-lived initiative belongs in the junior story as well as the women’s record: encouragement did not always produce an enduring programme on its first attempt.

Source note

Adapted from Pars from the Past, edited by David G. S. Mowat (2014), pp. 90–91, 94. Newspaper reports are described as cited in the book; original issues have not yet been checked for this preview.
