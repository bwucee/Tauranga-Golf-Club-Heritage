Women’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 93 / 305Next page →

Open original PDF at page 93 →

1974: Nen Sim became the first woman ever to be elected to the Club Executive.

1977: It was proposed to provide for a nine-hole handicap for Veteran Lady members who found it difficult to complete full rounds. Play was to be from the Eleventh to the First hole inclusive. This was seen as a means of encouraging these members to continue their membership rather than retire, or move away to less hilly courses as some had done in the past.

In this regard Tauranga led the way when introducing this abbreviated form of the game prior to its widespread adoption by NZ Golf. Later we were to become one of the first Clubs to promote Nine Hole

Golf to younger players in addition to this group of older women.

The power poles on the left hand side of the 8th fairway caused many a player to lose a stroke or two and a collective sigh of relief went up when, in the Fifties, these unwelcome obstructions were removed. Local rules did not normally allow for a replay should you be unfortunate enough to strike a pole. Why the 1954 rule referred to in the minutes quoted above only applied to the second pole is a mystery. Balls were as likely as not to ricochet out of bounds after striking a pole and Murphy’s Law ensured that seldom was a ricochet favourable to the player. Perhaps the most spectacular happening was when one player’s drive struck the pole nearest the tee, flew back over his head, and disappeared into the bunker that then bordered the 7th green.

Though his playing partners were amused the player was not. A bunker shot over trees to the fairway was certainly daunting. He would have been better to have declared it unplayable and gone forward to play again from the tee though some ‘expert’ would probably have told him he couldn’t do that. (Incidentally a correctly worded local rule would not allow an optional replay. If a replay was to be allowed it would need to be compulsory.)
