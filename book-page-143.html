Trust and make the funds available specifically for the clubhouse project was in the best interests of the long term future of the club. Without Trust funds of $230 000 being made available it is doubtful the project would have been completed.

In October 1998 Grant Scoones presented the Board with a Business Plan for the years 1999 to 2004. In this plan the Mission Statement read: “To provide a quality golf experience for all players in surroundings of excellence”. A month later he presented a second paper entitled: “Club Leadership Guide”. In this he states: “Board members must provide leadership. They must be as good at their job as they expect the General Manager to be. The GM must be the Chief Executive Officer, and seen to be that. The Board must give the GM and the Club strategic direction. The Board must formulate policy and empower and enable the GM to implement that policy.

The Board’s mission is to achieve the objectives of the membership. The Players Committee is to be responsible for the playing and social aspects of Club life and is to report to the Board via the General Manager”.

Current and future Boards might do well to study these two papers and note the concepts they outline. Together they provide much of what might best serve the needs of an evolving membership.

Barbara Glynn completed her term as Chairperson of the Board following the 1999 AGM of the Club. Barbara could be well proud of the progress made during her term. She was chiefly responsible for guiding the Board through the difficult period of uncertainty that precedes the wisdom of maturity. The dramatically remodelled and modernised clubhouse stands as a monument to her endeavours. Though building did not commence on the project during Barbara’s term in office the initial concept was developed, and the funding put in place, during those years.

The new Board elected David Mowat to the Chair and invited Jim Verran and Alison Holdem to serve as invited members. President Gary Baker, Player Representative Peg Copestake, and elected member Tony Gallagher completed the Board of six. Two major projects occupied the Board at the time. These were ensuring that the best possible result was obtained from the clubhouse redevelopment and that the condition of the fairways be improved. The story of clubhouse development is told elsewhere in this History.

Two options appeared to be available to improve the playing quality of our fairways. The first would involve a major commitment to developing a comprehensive fairway watering system, the second to

introduce fairway grasses that would thrive during our driest summers without the need for additional watering. Estimates at the time suggested it would cost $300 000 to $500 000 to set up full fairway watering, plus another $50 000 per annum in running costs. There was no guarantee that the required volume of water would be available in perpetuity.

David Ormsby, our advisor from Turf Research, was invited to meet with Club representatives to present the case for establishing a suitable Cynodon cultivar on our fairways. Windsor Green was considered to be the most suitable of those available. This grass had been successfully introduced to a number of Northern Clubs, notably Gulf Harbour where the World Cup had been staged within twelve months
