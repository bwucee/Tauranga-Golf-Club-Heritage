The women veterans’ golf club

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 137 / 305Next page →

By 1999 Nine Hole members had joined the monthly competitions and were integrated into the field. Play was now organised in fours and it was reported the caterer was unhappy with this format, as the pace of play was slow in comparison to the earlier play in threes. This caused a delay in completing the round and players coming in for lunch. The committee resolved to continue with play in fours. The Players’ Committee advised that the Lady Vets should accept players on handicaps up to 54 as this was now an official handicap under the new Slope system.

By the turn of the century membership had grown to include 102 Eighteen-hole members and 23 Nine-holers, though the average attendance was seldom much over forty-five. BOP Zone tournaments catered for fields of 120 and more to show that Veteran Golf was strong throughout the District. A team from Tauranga travelled to the Tauhara course in Taupo to claim the first ever victory for the Club in the Home Links Competition. The successful team included Hazel Casey, Sybil Grant, and Fay Horan. Perhaps inspired by this success the Club voted to have more competition days with official draws being made for the first and third Monday of each month. Kath Simmonds was seen to be doing an excellent job of organising the nine-hole players on these days.

Carmel Country Estate sponsored a tournament in October 2002 that attracted a field of 102 from a wide range of clubs. While the tournament proved an unqualified success the sponsorship sadly was not continued beyond that year. Bay Radiology continued to sponsor the popular Grandmothers’ Tournament for several years until 2005 when Palmer’s Garden Centre became involved for the first time.

In 2003 it was proposed that the Lady Vets no longer be represented on the Players’ Committee. While the committee resolved to formally object to this their objection was not successful and there has been no restoration of that representation.

It appears that Veteran Lady Golfers all became Veteran Women in 2005 in keeping with other female golfers throughout the Country. In that year the President’s report noted that research had determined that the first AGM was held in 1988 with Marg Calver elected President. Prior to that it would appear that Phyl Carter had been recognised as presiding over an unofficial organization the year prior. Notable amongst the women elected to office in 1988 was Shirley Crawford who served on the committee almost continuously until 2005. Shirley was elected President for the 2002/3 years and represented the Club at District level for several years. For the record the following statistics taken during the 2006 season showed the average attendance to be 25.6 Eighteen-holers and 10.7 Nine-holers.

The largest field hosted 34 of the former and 18 of the latter. Understandably most members are ‘fair weather players’ and attendance falls dramatically when rain threatens.

By 2007 accumulated funds had reached such a level that the committee voted to donate $400 to the Club with the suggestion this be used to provide decorative Planter Boxes. Total sponsorship through the season amounted to $515 with Gate Pa New World, Aussie Butcher, PaknSave, Fresh Fruit of Chapel Street, and BB’s Coffee Shop of Fraser Cove being willing supporters. The Grandmothers’ tournament attracted 92 entries, an indication of the strength of Veteran Women’s golf.
