Twilight golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 195 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

There are few pleasures to match that of wandering over a golf course on a balmy summer evening. Should the drives be booming down the centres of the fairways and the putts nestling into the bottoms of the holes so much the better, though something less on the scale of golfing excellence will not significantly dampen our enjoyment. For this reason vast numbers of golfers throughout the Country, from raw beginner to seasoned expert, look forward to the onset of Daylight Saving and the start of Twilight Golf. Residents of Tauranga are no exception though interest has not been consistent through the years.

Twilight tournaments were introduced to Tauranga in the summer of 1974/75 when willing volunteers would organise roll up starts, simple prizes, and a light meal to follow the golf. These weekly events were rotated over different week nights in an attempt to cater for those who might have commitments on particular nights. On account of the lay out of the course, the ninth hole being well away from the clubhouse, these events were played over ten holes simply because the tenth hole finished right by the car park. The first year realised a profit of $186 thanks mainly to the splendid catering provided by a large team of volunteers led by Nen Sim.

Nen kept meticulous records where she listed the income and expenditure each night together with many other relevant details including the menu, recipes, and suggestions for improvement. From these records we learn that on one night in January 1975 seventy people were served a meal. To cater for that number the menu included nine roast chickens served cold, four pounds of corned beef, and fourteen pounds of tomatoes. A potato salad included five pounds of potatoes plus a range of complementary ingredients. Cole Slaw and a bean, corn, carrot and onion salad were also available. For this sumptuous feast the princely sum of $1 was charged and yet the profit shown was more than $26.

The price of a meal increased to $1.50 the following year with the objective being to build up a pool of funds from which to purchase a number of items that it was felt the kitchen lacked. This objective was successfully achieved under Nen’s efficient management. At the end of the 1976/77 season items such as a Zip Water Heater, an extra refrigerator, an electric fan, and a selection of kitchen utensils were purchased from accumulated profit. By this time the catering for Twilight Golf was a joint effort involving Nen and her volunteers together with Mrs Anderson, the Club Caterer. It was a most amenable arrangement. Though a large number of women volunteered to assist Nen those who carried most of the burden were Nen herself, Joan Frith, Mavis Ward, and Joyce Gallagher.

What a wonderful job they did so willingly.

While these early events were popular with a group of loyal devotees, mainly regular club members, the concept gained additional impetus when Noel Copestake became involved. Under Noel’s leadership a small team gradually evolved a streamlined system that allowed a large field to play their ten holes, enjoy a social hour, a pleasant meal, and a generous prize table each Friday evening. All this was provided at very moderate cost and attracted Club members and visitors alike. Certain key personnel including treasurer, card checkers, bar persons, and barbecue cooks arrived each Friday to play their parts, usually sacrificing their own chance to play so as to ensure that all ran smoothly for the participants.

Noel himself started the field, having developed a starting program that ensured that the field flowed freely. He was never known to play in the field himself in all the years he was involved. In addition to acting as starter Noel filled the roles of sponsorship organiser, Master of Ceremonies, and occasional bar tender or barbecue cook as required.
