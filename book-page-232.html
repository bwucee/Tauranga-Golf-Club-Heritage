One who witnessed the round remarked that Murray had seldom been on his own fairway but the quality of his iron play was such that he almost always reached the green in regulation. At the time the trees were 50 years younger and did not present quite the difficulties experienced today but, given the equipment of the time, this round compares very favourably with any that have followed since. Alex Murray, born in Scotland, was the son of a professional golfer and, so steeped in golf was the family, that no fewer than eleven uncles were also professionals.

Murray showed his quality in winning three New Zealand Opens and would have won another but for his famous error in idly tapping his ball with his putter while waiting to putt during the final round at St. Andrews in Hamilton in 1937.

Despite shooting 69s in the first and final rounds Ernie Southerden of Napier finished two strokes behind Murray while Jim Paterson, now of Tauranga, repeated his third placing of the year before. Further down the field could be seen the name A E (Alf) Guy, a player renowned for his long hitting though his best days were now behind him. The BOP Times recorded that Alf thrilled the gallery by almost driving the 299 yard second hole. As Alf Guy was still one of the longest drivers in the game this gives some indication of the advancement made in ball and club technology as such drives as he achieved are now well within the range of a good number of club players

BUCKLER PREVAILS AGAIN IN 1955

Playing the most consistent golf seen to this time at Tauranga Frank Buckler left a quality field far in his wake following rounds of 72, 67, and 67. While not faultless his play was such as to leave the large gallery greatly impressed with the accuracy of his iron play and the quality of his putting. Regular place getters Ernie Southerden and Jim Paterson followed Buckler thanks to each scoring a 69 in one of their three rounds. The Akarana amateur Neville Dowden also scored a 69 in annexing the amateur section.

1956 THE EMPEROR PAYS A VISIT

In 1956 the reigning amateur champion of New Zealand, S G (Stuart) Jones of Hastings, was attracted to the tournament and he did not let the gallery or his reputation down. With rounds of 70, 73, and a superb final round of 66 “The Emperor”, as he became widely known during his long and illustrious career, outplayed the professional and amateur fields alike by many strokes. This was not to be the last time that Jones demonstrated to the professionals that it was perhaps fortuitous for them that he preferred to remain within the amateur ranks.

Popular winner amongst the professional division was local man J A (Jimmy) Paterson. A true gentleman of the links Jim learnt his trade in England and was one of the finest iron players one could hope to emulate. His well-struck iron shots seemed to fly quite low off the club then, as they slowed, they would rise so as to drop gently onto the green. It was a great pity that Jimmy’s nervous system did not allow him to consistently play the brand of golf of which he was so obviously capable. On this occasion he held his nerve well enough to finish on 217, four over par and two clear of Jack Kelly of Auckland.
