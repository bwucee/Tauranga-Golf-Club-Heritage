Resident professionals

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 168 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

After learning his trade in England Jim came to New Zealand to take up the resident Professional’s position with the Wanganui Golf Club at their Belmont course. A few years later some of our members made a trip to Wanganui where they met Jim and, being impressed with his personality and teaching skills, encouraged him to apply for the position at Tauranga. This Jim did and soon he and Barbara and their two sons were on their way North. Some years later twin daughters were born to the couple.

When Jim settled into his new role at Tauranga he found the available facilities to be rather less adequate than those that prevail today. The Pro Shop was located downstairs in the Clubhouse adjacent to the Ladies’ locker room. When the new Pro Shop was opened beside the first tee the Ladies Locker Room was expanded to fill the space it now occupies. Both the Professional and the Lady members were relieved to leave behind the cramped accommodation they had been forced to share for so long. While some members found that Jim’s quiet manner and distinctive accent made it hard for them to grasp the skill concepts he was endeavouring to pass on to them others reacted very positively to his teaching.

There is little doubt that his own swing was one that would serve as an ideal model for any aspiring player. One of the best lessons an observant player can have is to watch a really good golfing stylist practice. The easy rhythm, the perfect timing and the understated power tend to find their way into the observer’s own practice sessions. Jim Paterson was a very good example of a player to study in that context. His iron shots in particular had a special quality about them. The ball would start out low then climb higher towards the end of its flight before dropping quietly to the green. Unfortunately Jim’s tournament play was plagued by a nervous system that frequently did not allow him to demonstrate his true ability.

In spite of that he was competitive, often finishing near the head of the field amongst those Professionals competing in New Zealand at the time. On the basis of those performances he was selected to represent New Zealand in the two-man Canada Cup team. This was the forerunner of the present day World Cup.

Employment as a Club Professional has seldom been a lucrative career in New Zealand. When Jim took over the post in Tauranga the vast majority of players were of a generation that had lived through the depression years as well as the Second World War. As a result they were cautious in their spending habits and few people in the town would have been considered wealthy in any case. To help the family income Barbara worked at Hartleys on the Strand. When Barbara died at a relatively early age some of Jim’s motivation seemed to desert him and it perhaps came as some relief to him when the committee decided it was time to seek another to fill his position.

Throughout his twenty years at the Club Jim served loyally and with cheerful dignity. He assisted with work on the course when required over the summer months and was ever willing to assist in other areas when the need arose.

GREG HOLST

G T (Greg) Holst took up the position of Professional to the Tauranga Club in November 1972. Greg made a strong impression on the subcommittee that interviewed candidates on that occasion.
