Men’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

A W (Warwick) Nicol was the Accountant of the BNZ in Tauranga. He was a committee member and Vice President before being elected President in 1958-59. From 1954-56 he was Vice President of the BOPDGA and President in 1957-58. For many of those years he held the post of Men’s handicapper at Tauranga. He was made a Life Member of the Tauranga Golf Club in 1958 before retiring to Tirau.

H J W (Harry) Bradmore who was honoured with Life Membership in 1958 was not one to seek the limelight. He, along with his son Maurice, was very active behind the scenes. They followed on from the work of Mr Mirrielees in planting trees and nurturing their early growth. By doing so they made a valuable contribution to the development of the excellent conditions we now enjoy. Mr Bradmore presented the Alan Bradmore Memorial Trophy in remembrance of another son, Alan, who was killed in the Second World War. That trophy is contested annually over two Stableford rounds as part of the regular Saturday programme.

R J (Dick) Phelan was elected to Life Membership in 1963. Details of Dick’s contribution to Veterans’ Golf and of his long competitive career have been documented elsewhere in this publication. That he was able to feature in the final of the Junior Championship 47 years after his Senior Championship victory in 1930 bears testimony to his unprecedented longevity in the game he so obviously loved. It is unfortunate that complete records of committee personnel prior to 1953 have not been uncovered

so it is not possible to document the service records of men of that era. We can be confident that Dick Phelan did more than his share to see that the Club advanced in the right direction.

A L (Alan) Davidson was elected to Life Membership in 1964. There could be no more worthy recipient than ‘Zuke’ as he became known for reasons lost in time. Much of Alan’s contribution to the Club he loved has been well documented elsewhere in this History. For the record Alan was Vice President in 1953 when our full records began and he progressed to the Presidency in 1956-58. There is no doubt his committee work began earlier than that. He returned again to the committee in 1966 serving a second term of Presidency from 1968-70. During this phase Alan did much of his most significant work particularly in relation to course development and the incorporation of the ‘Harris Plan’ into the design of specific holes.

Many a match committee member of today would be amazed at the amount of work that was once done to achieve the same result that now happens automatically as computer data are projected onto the clubhouse screen to show individual scores. For example prior to the Tauranga Open 28 hole event all competitor names would have been meticulously hand printed on cardboard strips that were then slotted into metal runners on wooden score boards. In the course of the day all the tournament details would be appended by hand following manual checking of all cards. For years Alan, Des Wilson, Jim Reid, Keith Frith and others would forgo the opportunity to participate in the day in order to ensure that all this documentation was completed quickly and efficiently so that prize giving could be held as soon as possible following play.

For some years the first two mentioned above sponsored that particular tournament.
