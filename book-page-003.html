FOREWORD

I take great pleasure in writing this foreword to “Pars from the Past”, a comprehensive History of Golf in Tauranga over the past 110 years.

In terms of European settlement Tauranga is a very young city in a young country. Early British settlers brought much of their heritage to New Zealand, and the game of golf was established in Dunedin as early as 1871. This was almost 20 years earlier than the first American Golf Club was established. Golf in Tauranga owed it’s early beginnings to the arrival of Dr Brown from Dunedin who was instrumental in developing our first course on the Domain /Sulphur Point area of the town in 1904. That course proved to be less than ideal and, in 1913, the first overtures were made to the Trustees of the Racecourse Reserve to see if it was feasible to establish a course on the reserve at Greerton.

To those people who had the vision to realise that an ideal golf course could be established on what may not at first sight have appeared the best available site, we owe an enormous debt of gratitude. The course established so early in the twentieth century has been continually developed and improved over the succeeding 100 years so as to provide an enjoyable, yet challenging, course for hundreds of players through the past, the present and, we can be confident, many years into the future.

Miss Joan Mirrielees, a daughter of one of the true pioneers of golf in Tauranga, AJ Mirrielees, was the early historian to the club and her work was continued by Mrs Deidre Hartley and finally completed by long time member David Mowat. This history contains a record of the development of the original course in central Tauranga, the first course on the Racecourse Reserve that extended into the Racecourse oval, and the course as it is played today. While that record is important, this history is most notably a history of the people who have made the club what it is today. The stories of their selfless work over the course of the century, the personal anecdotes that show the human side of their personalities, these are the features of this work that will stand out for many readers.

While club members have contributed richly to our History, so too have the many visitors who have graced our course over the years. In this context the memorable visits of Professional players from around the world who featured in the Spalding Masters and the PGA Championships staged on the course in earlier years added much to the rich tapestry of our History.

David Mowat has captured the spirit of the people who make up our Club’s History in a manner that only a person with a true love of the game and its participants could have done. A member for over sixty years David was an ideal choice to take on the task of compiling this comprehensive, entertaining, and invaluable history of our Club.

On behalf of the Board of Management and of all past, present and future members, I thank David for this contribution to us all.

Graham Cathie – Board Chairman
