The Spalding Masters and the NZPGA Championship

Scoring in the 1971 event was remarkably good throughout the field with 64 rounds under the par of 70 being recorded while five players were under par in every round. Fifteen players bettered the 72-hole par of 280 including Amateur winner Ted McDougall.

1972 - TO CHARLES THE GLORY, TO MACKLIN THE SPOILS

The 1972 staging of the Spalding Masters, which also proved to be the last, featured a $3000 prize for a hole in one on the fourteenth hole. Murray Macklin, born in Tauranga but resident of Masterton, took the prize with a four iron shot during his second round. Under PGA rules Macklin, as a Probationary Professional, was unable to receive prize money from the tournament but special prizes such as those for holes in one were exempt from this policy so he was free to take the $3000. Later Bob Charles commented tongue in cheek: “I get only $1000 for playing 54 holes. Murray plays one shot and pockets $3000.

Is that fair”? In reality of course Bob was only too happy for Murray, especially as he came from Bob’s hometown of Masterton.

While the field included some interesting newcomers, plus the usual representation from New Zealand and Australia, the leader board featured old rivals Bob Charles and Guy Wolstenholme from the first day. These two matched one another stroke for stroke with identical totals of 67 and 68 before Charles pulled clear with 65 against Wolstenholme’s 67 in the final round. Terry Kendall was a further four

strokes back in third place while locals were delighted with the performance of their star junior, 18 year old Michael Nicholson, who annexed Amateur honours with grand rounds of 72, 69, and 69. An excellent photographic study of the two winners can be seen hanging in the clubhouse. Top honours to Michael in this tournament contributed to a great month for him which included a tie for leading Amateur in the City of Auckland Open at the Grange and a win in the New Zealand under 23 Championship played at Bridge Pa, Hastings. A closing 66 in that event emphasised the quality of Michael’s play.

A deluge during play of the third round of the 1972 Spalding which flooded many of the greens eventually forced the organisers to abandon that round and reduce the tournament to 54 holes. Though some players who were in the last stages of their rounds, including one who was reputedly seven under after the 14th, were very upset at the decision the committee had no choice. While greens on the final few holes were playable and the complaining players could have comfortably completed their rounds those on the front of the course had no chance of doing so. With the NZPGA Tournament scheduled to commence at The Mount there were no spare days available to complete a fourth round.

Following his victory at Tauranga Bob Charles was poised to become the first player to win $10 000 in a New Zealand season. Finishing in the top four places at Mount Maunganui would achieve that milestone.

TAURANGA STAGES 1974 NZPGA CHAMPIONSHIP

Sadly the 1972 staging brought down the curtain on the five year show that had seen the Spalding Masters attract quality golfers to Tauranga from many parts of the world.
