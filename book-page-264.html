They were not disappointed as Richard took up the challenge of attempting to drive this short par four only to see the ball roll into the bunker alongside the green. A solid bunker shot left the ball within two metres of the hole from where Richard appeared to hole it calmly enough though he later confessed his hands were not as steady as may have appeared. In spite of this remarkable round Richard still trailed the leader by three strokes. Wellington Amateur Nick Gillespie matched Green’s 63 to lie level with Lee at 12 under.

Unnoticed in all the excitement Masterton Amateur Ben Campbell quietly went about his business to compile rounds of 66 and 65 to trail by six strokes together with Cody Harper. The third day was something of a comedy of errors, the leaders appearing to vie with one another to see who would self-destruct next. First Nick Gillespie (65, 63) appeared to destroy his round by firing three balls into the orchard beyond the Norris residence to the left of the eighth fairway. Playing partner Andrew Green followed by recording a 9 at the par three ninth. He followed an out of bounds tee shot by firing his second tee shot into the bunker alongside the green.

Andrew then incurred a two-stroke penalty when his caddie (his father) raked the bunker while Andrew’s ball lay within it.

Meanwhile Campbell took advantage of the situation as he added a 64 to establish a three-stroke lead over Green (73), with a further two strokes back to Hendry (66) and Lee (72). Ryan Fox, son of legendary All Black Grant, produced his best Tauranga performance to date, three successive 67s leaving him a further stroke back.

Ben Campbell gave no one else a chance on the final day, shooting a 66 to win by four from Hendry who shot 65. Fox improved on his 67s with a 66 to edge Jared Pender, who closed with a 64 to add to his third round 63. This enabled Jared to collect his best cheque as a Professional with his second placing behind Hendry. Richard Lee, perhaps still somewhat shell-shocked from his 59, self-destructed at the 13th by firing a succession of drives into the racecourse to record a double-digit hole score and a final round 77. It may be hard to believe that a Professional could show a spread of 18 strokes between any two rounds of a tournament but it did happen.
