Personal experiences

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 205 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

In time the membership outgrew the rather primitive clubhouse and a bigger and brighter one was built on the present site. The facilities in the new building were good. The bar instead of being a cubby-hole was well planned and a decent size. The crates of beer were kept in a locked cupboard under the stairs. This meant they had to be carried up manually. The bar was run by Arthur Towsey, a commercial traveller, and a real personality around the club. The job was a labour of love carrying no salary. In the bar Arthur ruled. Early players would be caught as they arrived and made to do their share by carrying up crates of beer.

He opened and closed when he felt like it as no one else imposed restrictions or, if they did, no one bothered with them. In earlier times each case of beer or bottle of spirits was labelled with the name of a member for “personal delivery” at the club before leaving Guiness Brothers, the suppliers. This was the method used to get around the law that had no way of licensing clubs such as ours.

As the bar was quite small there was no room for two to serve behind it so there was often a crowd waiting. The dining room was to the left and the lounge to the right. The latter was well furnished and carpeted with landscape windows giving excellent views of the 6th and 18th greens.

With better facilities inside and out the Club boomed and it was not long before the new clubhouse was too small to meet the demands placed upon it. Arthur Towsey, a bachelor up till then, was fading in his barman role and he was soon to marry his Landlady and disappear from the scene. A new man took over. He had an English name which I forget. He seemed to spend a lot of time cleaning his ear!

Noel Hockin was our secretary, a job he had done for some time on a small honorarium. He then became full time Secretary Manager on a salary. One day, talking to Noel, I said to him, mostly as a joke that had I known the Barman’s job was up for application I would have applied. Noel just laughed. It really was a joke as, not being a drinker, I did not patronise the bar and had no idea of its inner workings. By 1964 there was talk of enlarging the clubhouse again. The changing rooms were too small, Jim Paterson’s shop was much too small, and the trundler shed was only a small building rescued from the Mowat property when it became the Girls’ College.

Consequently a new trundler shed was built incorporating a good-size Pro Shop and plans prepared for clubhouse extensions. The enlarged changing rooms expanded into the area previously occupied by the old Pro Shop beneath the dining room that, along with the lounge, was extended out towards the 18th fairway. The building plan was a major project for the Club and caused much discussion and controversy amongst the members with many strongly in favour and many against. With the assistance of the Club’s Bankers and a good take up of debentures by members the work was eventually completed.

There was one small hitch. The chap who was steward and barman decided it would be too much upheaval for him to cope with during the extensions and he handed in his notice. Noel Hockin informed the committee he had a solution to that problem. He rang me and said did I remember telling him I would have been interested in a job at the Club. I’d quite forgotten all about it but he said the job would only be a temporary one until the alterations were complete and everything had settled down once again. After that the plan was to appoint a married couple to handle catering and the bar.

I thought it would be a nice change for a while as I was doing little apart from growing a few flowers and greenery
