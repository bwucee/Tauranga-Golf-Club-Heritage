She recalled Bronco’s methods did not involve hitting a ball for the first lesson or two, and new pupils were not allowed near a golf course for at least three months. This could be very frustrating to the keen beginner. The season following these sessions with Bronco saw Peg’s handicap down to single figures, a level she was able to maintain for many years. Although playing to one’s age is a not an uncommon feat amongst male players Peg is one of the few women we know to have achieved this. A strong commitment to the encouragement of junior players saw Peg an early disciple of the ‘Gillian Bannan’ method of introducing youngsters to the game.

Consequently she travelled to local schools with a trailer load of clubs, hoops, mats, buckets and flags, to teach groups of about twenty children the rudiments of golf. Peg served on the executive committee of the club and in 2000 became the first woman to be elected Club President, an honour very much deserved following years of involvement in so many aspects of both the Club and the game itself. Notable amongst these have been her commitment to spreading the word on the Rules of Golf and, along with husband Noel, to the organisation of Twilight Golf.

To recognise her outstanding service Peg was elected to Life Membership in 2005. In joining husband Noel in receiving this distinction they became the only married couple to have been so honoured. Sadly Peg passed away in December 2011 within days of her return from a much anticipated trip to Melbourne to follow the fortunes of the players competing in the President’s Cup. It was wonderful that she was able to enjoy every day of that great event featuring the American team versus the rest of the World, excluding Europe.

EDNA GALBALLY joined the Club in 1943 having arrived in Tauranga the previous year to take up a teaching appointment in the two Primary Schools at Otumoetai. As Committee member, Secretary, Handicapper, and Saturday Supervisor Edna spent 23 years as an officer of the Tauranga Ladies’ Golf Club. Her quiet efficiency, her wealth of golfing knowledge, and her encouragement both to committee members and players made her a greatly valued member of the Ladies’ Club. For her services over so many years she was elected to Life Membership in 1994.

NANCY HARE joined the Club in 1946. She served the Ladies’ Committee as Vice Captain, Captain, and President. Nancy won the Junior Championship soon after joining and, in 1975, reached the final of the Senior Championship where she met Una Wickham who was in her usual matchless form.

Women golfing personalities especially recalled by Nancy included Doreen Blundell (Juno) of the Mount Maunganui Club, Linda Caruthers, Joyce Carnachan, Merle Carter, Cookie Grant, Ruby Poynter and Rennie Gordon. OLGA JOHNSON came to Tauranga from South Africa in the 1950s. She and husband Eric were members for 34 years before joining the Mount Club when Eric found our hills rather too difficult to negotiate. During their time at Tauranga they were wonderful Club members, both fully involved in all aspects of club life. Even after resigning they were regular contestants in the Friday night Twilight competitions where it was not necessary for Eric to play the hilly first six holes.
