Administration

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

It was interesting to note in the Board minutes of June 2003 that the Board directed their Chairman to attend the next meeting of the Men’s Committee in order to stress to them the importance of that committee focusing their energies on the responsibilities allocated to them through Club rules. These were to attend to the needs of Men’s competitive golf. The Board was concerned that the Men’s Committee appeared to concentrate much of their energy on matters relevant to the Board and Management, and that the direction of much of this was negative. This was an example of the difficulty some individuals and groups found in adapting to the new management structure of Board and Committees.

Gradually the structure has been accepted as the benefits it confers have been recognised and most groups have learned to concentrate their efforts in furthering the interests of their own members. The Men’s Committee has understandably had the most difficulty with adapting to its role as for some 90 years their members had effectively managed all aspects of the Club’s affairs through their various sub-committees.

Included with the August 2003 Board minutes is a paper entitled ‘Where are we Heading’? This was presented to the Board by President David Mowat and discusses a number of aspects of Course and Club development that continue to be relevant to future planning. Over the years Committees and Boards have discussed many topics and put forward many ideas worthy of consideration by those who follow them. An occasional perusal of these ideas from the past might well prove a rewarding exercise for future administrations.

The 2004 Board received a report from David Ormsby of the NZ Turf Research Institute in which he recommended the Club concentrate on encouraging Browntop in the greens. While Poa annua may be seen to dominate over the winter months the Browntop would prevail from September through May and so provide an excellent playing surface over most of the year. To encourage Browntop more aeration and deep root encouragement was needed. The greens should be oversown with Browntop following each renovation. This represented a throwback to earlier times in the fifties and sixties when grooving and seeding with Browntop was a standard annual procedure on our greens. Sometimes progress is made in a circular fashion.

Other recommendations harking back to an earlier age were to keep the pH (acidity) level low between 4.9 and 5.3. Nitrogen levels were to be moderate and Potassium level low (below 3%). I have to wonder when, and why, we got off the Browntop track that had previously proved so successful. Mr Ormsby also stressed that the Cynodon fairways would require careful thatch management to provide the best possible playing surface. This has proved only too true. Opinion throughout the Club on the merits of Cynodon fairways was at this time still divided though the Board

was united on the need to push forward with the program. Brendan Allen and David Ormsby presented a summary of the main points for and against the contending strategies of Cynodon versus full fairway watering to the Board in 2004. The following points were made.

Water requirement Full irrigation would require 1020 cubic metres of water per day. The club had resource consent for only 200 cubic metres per day from the stream and was unlikely to be granted additional rights.
