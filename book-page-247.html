Tauranga’s next opportunity to stage a major tournament came through the misfortunes of the Mount Maunganui club. Subsequent to the staging of the 1973 NZPGA Championship on the course the greens there were struck by disease to such an extent that they had to be completely worked up and resown. As a result Tauranga was asked to stand by in case the Mount was not ready in time for the 1974 tournament. This proved to be the case and Tauranga staged its first NZPGA tournament in January 1974. The loss of their greens proved to be a blessing in disguise for the Mount Club as the new Pencross greens proved to be a great success earning high praise when the Club later hosted a number of prestigious tournaments including two Men’s Inter Provincial Teams events, the most recent in 2008 being won by the Bay of Plenty.

The 1974 PGA Championship brought Ted Ball’s comments of 1969 into sharp relief. That great gentleman of Australian golf, Kel Nagle, came to Tauranga and quickly demonstrated his talents. He showed he meant business even in the more relaxed atmosphere of the Pro/Am traditionally played the day before the tournament proper. On that day the American, Jerry Breaux, matched the score of 62 set by Charles and Hart in 1969 only to see Nagle better this by a stroke within the hour. Nagle’s 61 was free of blemish and contained seven birdies and an eagle three at the seventh. The latter helped him to a 29 outward nine. Breaux’ round was even more spectacular with eagles at the sixth, seventh and tenth holes to go with five birdies.

Dropped shots at the second, fifth, and seventeenth holes negated these to deny the American the chance to break 60. Perhaps to show that the Gate Pa course is not as benign as some are tempted to believe Breaux shot a 75 in the opening round of the tournament proper. Many a club golfer could empathise with this 13-stroke disparity between one round and the next. Breaux demonstrated his professionalism by shooting 7 under over the remaining three rounds to finish in a tie for fifteenth, a placing shared with Michael Nicholson, the leading Amateur. Michael’s final three rounds were all sub par following an opening 73, another meritorious tournament for the young star.

New Zealand World Cup representative Dennis Clark of Palmerston North led the field with a 64 in the first round while players of the calibre of Lindsay Sharp, Kel Nagle and John Lister lay within three strokes of him. Nagle indicated his desire to win his sixth NZPGA title with a second round 64, three under on each nine, while Sharp kept in touch with a 66. Stewart Ginn, the popular and very fashionable Australian, was the prime mover with a 64 to add to his opening 69. Sharp would have been confident of closing the gap to Nagle following his third round 64 but it was not to be with Nagle also home in

64. The New Zealand Professional Walter Godfrey, a young man of enormous potential, made the strongest move, his 63 taking him to 13 under and just two strokes down on Nagle. In the final round it was Godfrey, playing with Nagle, who kept the pressure on the leader as others fell away. None the less the gap had increased to four by the time they teed up at the long 13th. Here Godfrey was long and straight while Nagle pushed his drive to the right. An attempt to cut a five iron around the big pine that once stood on that corner instead produced a draw and a visit to the racecourse.

Even the greats get it wrong sometimes. Godfrey’s two-putt birdie reduced the deficit to one and a real contest was on for the run home.
