As a player Alan won the Junior Championship in 1946 and went on to play to single figures, his best round being a 69. Alan served in the Second World War as crew in Bomber raids over Germany. On one occasion his plane having been hit by Anti Aircraft fire Alan, believing the plane to be doomed, prepared to bail out. On his way to the escape hatch he touched the Rear Gunners shoulder saying: “the plane is going down. We have to get out”. A few seconds after they cleared the plane it exploded with the loss of the rest of the crew. Alan made it to ground but was captured by German troops and became a prisoner of war.

The Tauranga Golf Club was indeed fortunate that Alan survived to tell the tale. For many years Alan owned and managed the Business known as Davidson’s in Spring Street where many a fashion conscious lady member of the Golf Club bought her ensembles.

Keith Nattrass was first elected to the committee in 1957 joining an especially strong group made up of A L Davidson, A W Nicol, D Coutts, A Towsey, J Mowat, N H E Hockin, F Cassin, and S Randell. Colin Evitt was then the Honorary Secretary. Keith’s administrative abilities were immediately recognised and he was elected Vice President a year later and served as President from 1960-63. At District level Keith served as President of the BOPDGA in 1961-62. The high point of Keith’s golf administration came in 1969 when he assumed the position of North Island Vice President of the NZGA, a position bringing credit both to Keith and to the Tauranga Club.

In 1973 Keith was awarded the highest honour available to Golf Club members, that of Life Membership.

J C (John) Hare has been mentioned elsewhere in connection with his work in reconciling differences between the Racing Club and the Golf Club in relation to various privileges each might exercise in their use of the Reserve. He became a member of the Racecourse Domain Board, as it then was, in 1952. He later became its Chairman. John refrained from taking office in either of the tenant clubs in order to avoid any possible conflict of interest though he did serve the Golf Club in the role of honorary solicitor. For many years he acted as timekeeper for the Racing Club. His ‘apprentice’ in that capacity was Keith Frith who carried on the role following John’s death.

John was a partner in the law firm of Maltby, Hare and Willoughby with offices in Wharf Street. The firm later merged with Holland, Beckett and Partners to form Hobec, one of the leading Law Partnerships in the city. John was an enthusiastic golfer whose invaluable contribution to establishing and protecting the Club’s rights on the Reserve were recognised by the award of Life Membership in 1980.

Keith Frith will be remembered as ‘Captain Extraordinaire’. Keith joined the committee in 1971 as Vice Captain before assuming the role of Captain in 1973. Where it had been the custom for men to serve in this demanding role for two years Keith broke the mould by continuing in the post for seven years before accepting the position of Vice President for a further three years. Never one to relish the limelight Keith was reluctantly persuaded to accept the Presidency in 1983, an honour he richly deserved. Never really comfortable in the role of President Keith handed over the reins after just one year but it was not long before he returned to the committee.
