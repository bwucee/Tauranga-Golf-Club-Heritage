Twilight golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

David Mowat who would, each week, print out a new list of handicaps incorporating both the Friday- night players and the Business-House team members developed this system, and a Computer program to handle it. Non-scoring holes were counted as scoring one point when reviewing cards for handicap purposes. Peg and Kim Copestake became expert at checking and adjusting cards and isolating those that needed to be kept for the handicapper. When the Dot Golf Computer system was installed the technicians wrote and incorporated the Twilight Handicapping system onto the Club computer software. This program has saved countless volunteer hours since its introduction. Under the regular handicapping system women were at a clear disadvantage when competing against men in the Business House competition.

In recognition of this they were given a bonus of two points to add to their scores. Once the Twilight Handicap system was fully operating and men and women were being treated in exactly the same way for handicapping purposes it became clear this bonus was no longer justified and it was discontinued.

Twilight Golf proved to be a winner. The Club gained through the facilities being used during evenings when they would otherwise be deserted, potential members were exposed to the attractions full membership does offer and, most importantly, a large number of people were enabled to enjoy themselves in truly pleasant surroundings. It is to be hoped that some day a reinvigorated Friday evening Twilight program will supplement the Business-House competition.

Over the years many people have given their time to ensure the twilight programmes have run successfully and efficiently. Sometimes these have been Committee members, more often members who have simply enjoyed the opportunity to be part of a convivial group helping to provide pleasure to others and value to their Club. They deserve our admiration and our gratitude.
