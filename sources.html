HOW THIS COLLECTION IS BUILT

The evidence behind the stories.

A working collection, with clear references and room for corrections.

Primary supplied source

Pars from the Past: A History of the Tauranga Golf Club, edited by David G. S. Mowat, published in 2014. Page numbers refer to printed book pages, which match the supplied PDF page numbers.

The complete original book is available as a PDF through the archive. The story introductions are newly written from the source. The separate book collection preserves its original accounts in attributed, page-by-page review transcriptions, with images and original-layout reference pages. Readers can choose the original PDF or the web text view.

The club’s existing History page

The Tauranga Golf Club History page is an accepted historical reference alongside Mowat’s book. It confirms that the club dates from 1904 and has occupied its Racecourse Reserve home at Gate Pā since 1914.

Our historical foundation

Mowat’s extensively researched book is treated as generally affirmed historical material and the primary reference for this collection. Other historical contributions are compared against his original account and the club’s official history wherever they cover the subject. Rare exceptions require an explicit explanation and supporting evidence. Material beyond the book’s coverage keeps its own source and contributor attribution.

New material from the club heritage folder

The trophy register transcribes seven separately supplied records, including 168 year entries and their gaps. These are partial records, not a complete verified results database. Context links lead back to Mowat; differences such as the Ron Galloway Cup’s 1953 heading and the book’s 1954 donation account remain visible.

Separately supplied photographs retain their captions and source filenames. The Life Members page makes the book’s 2014 roll easier to find. Member memories uses prompts from the supplied interview template; it does not claim that interviews have already taken place.

Member histories supplied 10 November 2026

Jack Carter’s golfing life preserves his undated personal account from JACK CARTERS GOLF HISTORY.docx. His 2019 Life Membership is added separately from the book’s 2014 roll. References to 46 years of involvement describe the time of writing; the recollection is not treated as a current statement of club policy.

Life Members Landscape.pdf is a 21-page compilation of profiles, club notices, correspondence and third-party clippings. Ten Life Member profiles summarise the relevant material, with PDF page references and comparison links to Mowat. Later notices concerning Noel Copestake in 2016 and Phyll Sandlant in 2023 extend the record beyond the book. Private email details and signatures are omitted, and the compilation itself is not offered for download.

The 1968 Spalding Masters programme

The programme adds contemporary records and photographs of players, greenkeepers and the course, together with advertising that places golf within Tauranga life. Its material is indexed across subjects; apparent conflicts with the book remain identified. Programme photographs were authorised for publication on 10 November 2026.

Dates and gaps

1903 marks Brown’s arrival, not a claimed club foundation. The book and the club’s official history support 1904 as the foundation year; 1914 marks the move to Gate Pā. Its introduction explains the lack of early minute books and the use of recollections and newspaper research. Newspaper originals have not yet been independently checked for this preview.

The undated golfing-party and Avenue images are contextual material. Their inclusion does not establish a 1903–1923 date. The Domain course card also has a dating question noted by the book itself.

Additional source for nine-hole golf

Luke Balvert, “A hole in 100”, SunLive, 15 November 2014, identifies Patsy Hankins’s national role and her approach to Phyll Sandlant in 1998. The nine-hole story distinguishes this report from the book’s local account.

Reading the original book

The complete book collection retains Dave’s text and its own period context. Recollections, opinions, terminology and historical rules are not rewritten as current facts. The transcription removes running headers and stray layout characters; it has not been fully proofread. Multi-column honours and trophy reference pages retain their printed layout as images, alongside searchable text that must not be used to infer column relationships.

The last chapter explicitly includes an imagined future. Predictions are labelled at chapter and page level and do not appear in the factual timeline.

Details still to check

The source notes in the redesign, wartime, clubhouse and Veterans’ stories identify conflicting details within the book. They concern the lease chronology, wartime championship years, final clubhouse cost and a tournament date. These questions remain visible for members to help resolve.

Image register

Each archive record includes its book page, available credit, date status and reproduction authorisation. Images were extracted without generating or replacing historical detail; only web sizing and JPEG compression were applied.

Review edition and image permission

The project owner authorised all photographs and images for publication on 9 November 2026. Mowat’s book and the club’s official history provide the accepted historical foundation. New records and identifications remain open to specific corrections. This is a member review edition, shared to invite corrections and development suggestions. Review status allows the collection and its presentation to develop; it does not mean that the book’s researched history is treated as unconfirmed.

Alongside the main website

The existing club History page can remain a short introduction, linking to this collection when ready. This separate static collection does not change bookings, membership, news or the club’s existing navigation. Any future address or hosting arrangement is still to be agreed.
