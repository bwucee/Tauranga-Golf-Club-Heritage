Other companies had been determined to challenge Acushnet’s dominance and research funding seemed to be no problem. Even following the Court decision in favour of Golf Administration the research continued as companies vied to produce “the better ball” within the new regulations. Spin rates, dimple pattern, and cover properties were all fertile areas for further experimentation. Never the less an effective cap had been placed on distance, and club administrators could confidently expect that future course policy would not need to plan for ever increasing driving distances.

For the Tauranga Golf Club, and for Clubs World-wide, the successful introduction of the softer ball had far reaching consequences. The need to lengthen courses was no longer apparent while new courses could be developed on relatively small areas. This was important in an age when suitable available land had become increasingly scarce and expensive. The Tauranga course once again became sufficiently challenging to test the best players, both men and women, while offering fair competition to Club members at all levels of ability.

Though legislation to restrict the flight of the golf ball achieved the desired result Club officials were still interested in making changes, both major and minor, to course design. Three major weaknesses that had long been identified were successive par threes at the fourth and fifth holes, the fact that there were five par threes in all, and the lack of practice facilities. During the decade of the 2030s funds were found to develop two holes in Kopurererua Valley. The third hole was now played as a dog leg to the left, the drive being essentially the same as previously, the hole then turning in a south-westerly direction to a green requiring a mid to long iron second stroke from a drive of average length.

A new par four fourth hole was developed requiring a drive and a short iron to a green to the left of, and slightly beyond the present third green. This green was well bunkered to penalise a wayward short iron. These two holes were developed without any inconvenience to play as the existing third and fourth holes remained in play until the third was no longer required. The existing fourth, an excellent par three, became the fifth hole while the existing fifth was incorporated into extended practice facilities linked to the driving range. The abandonment of the existing fifth also enabled extensive contouring of the seventh fairway to provide a better landing area for the second stroke, a long sought improvement to that hole.

A number of other changes were made in the first half of the 21st Century. These included:

Hole 1 The green was moved back and tucked slightly to the right so that the tee shot needed to be played towards the left side of the fairway in order to gain the best approach to the pin. Hole 2 The Casuarinas to the right were removed and replaced by a cluster of fairway bunkers. Hole 6 A new bunker was installed to guard the left front of the green.
