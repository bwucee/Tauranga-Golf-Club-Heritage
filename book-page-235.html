Apart from a sponsored tournament held earlier in the season at Akarana the amateur field assembled at Tauranga was said to be the best assembled in New Zealand that year. Appearing on the leader board were regular supporters of Tauranga tournaments including Peter Maude (Otorohanga), Terry Leech (Taupo), Ross Newdick (Auckland), Trevor Ormsby (Otorohanga), and Frank Cullen (Matamata).

In the professional division the two regular placegetters, Ernie Southerden and Jim Paterson, along with Irishman Eddie Fennell of Gisborne, fought out a close contest with Southerden edging home by a stroke from the other two. Paterson should have won in a canter but for an uncharacteristic 82 in the middle round. No player, amateur or professional, came close to emulating Godfrey’s tournament total or his best round of 67.

In an unusual and much appreciated gesture Frank Buckler, now president of the NZPGA, presented trophies on behalf of the PGA to the Men’s and Women’s club captains of the Tauranga Golf Club “in appreciation of what the Club is doing, and trying to do, for golf in New Zealand”.

BUCKLER WINS AGAIN IN 1963

Frank Buckler crossed the Kaimai ranges once again to annexe his fourth Tauranga Invitational on what must surely be his favourite course. The Canada Cup representative finished four under the card this time playing consistently good rounds of 69, 72, and 68. On this occasion no one amongst Buckler’s professional colleagues could mount a serious challenge with only the long hitting amateur Peter Maude lying close after the first round. Maude held on to win the amateur title in spite of relatively poor rounds of 75 and 73 on the Sunday. Ross Newdick finished two strokes behind Maude.

The gap between Tauranga’s best and those from elsewhere in New Zealand was demonstrated when Tauranga’s leading competitor, D K (Doug) Furrie, finished eleven strokes shy of Peter Maude’s total while another four local players were much further adrift. It was to be many years before Tauranga men began to make their mark at National level. In the case of the women however the club had seen much stronger representation and local star Una Wickham was a popular winner of the Women’s division despite scoring rather more poorly than was her norm. Six strokes behind Una Sally Turvey, the 16 year old from Rotorua, threw out an indication of the future of the game as young players gradually came to the fore.

Another youngster from Rotorua, Jane Butler, also gave an indication of her potential, only a poor middle round preventing her from challenging for the lead. Jane went on to a very successful

career in golf and is now better known as Jane Little. In common with many quality players Jane’s father was a Green Keeper so she was in touch with the game from a very early age.

RECORDS APLENTY IN 1964

As the result of some changes to the course that really had little impact on its playing difficulty the course record of 64 set by Alex Murray ten years earlier was no longer recognised as applying to “the new course”. Consequently new records in both the Amateur and the Professional sections were recognised as the result of scores posted in the 1964 tournament.
