The first tee

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

Dr. W M Brown when he arrived from Dunedin in 1903, most probably by Coastal Steamer, carried with him the seed from which the Tauranga Golf Club grew. While it is uncertain if this Dr Brown and the Dr. William M Brown mentioned above as an inaugural member of the Dunedin Golf Club were one and the same it does seem extremely likely. Both were surgeons hailing from Dunedin, each man had an abiding interest in the game of golf, and the timing fits such a scenario. The earliest written evidence of golf in Tauranga can be found in the Archives of The Bay of Plenty Times dated 20th April 1904 where it is recorded that a Golf Club had been, or was being, formed in Tauranga.

On June 20th that same year the Times reported: ‘an interesting golf match was played here yesterday afternoon between members of the club for a clique (Sic) presented by Dr. Brown’. Mr. J Bull won this valuable trophy.1

Although the year 1911 has long been recognised as the foundation year of the Tauranga Golf Club it is clear the Club can trace its existence back to 1904. This is evidenced by a report in the BOP Times of 17th May 1905 in which it was observed:

‘The Tauranga Golf Club has started operations for the present season, and several of the members can be seen on the links at all hours of the day energetically practising. At the annual meeting held on Saturday evening Dr Brown and Mr J Bull were re-elected Captain and Secretary respectively’.

Clearly the club had been in existence prior to that meeting. That Dr. Brown was the inspiration behind the birth of the fledgling club is confirmed by the recollections of early residents who credited Dr. Brown with being instrumental in forming the club and in laying out nine holes based on the present Domain area in central Tauranga.2

Foundation in 1904 is further supported by documentary evidence to be found in archived material from the Bay of Plenty Times dated 18th of June 1906. In that edition there is a report of ‘a merry gathering at the Masonic Hotel when the past Mayor of Tauranga, Dr. W Brown, was entertained at dinner by the members of the Tauranga Golf and kindred clubs. Mr W Commons, Mayor, occupied the chair and, in proposing a toast to the guest of honour, he said all would regret that they were about to say farewell to Dr. Brown. It was not often that a town of this size had the services of one of such a high profession

Editor’s note: The correct name for the club presented by Dr. Brown is a cleek, a steep faced driving iron, though the BOP Times reporter of the day could be forgiven his lack of knowledge of the game and its implements. [Editor’s Note. The year 1911, upon which the 50th and 75th jubilee celebrations of 1961 and 1986 were based, appears to have been recognised as the Club’s foundation year based on evidence sourced from old Honours Boards. These commenced with the championship victory of Mr. A Matheson in that year of 1911. It is now clear that the club was founded in 1904 and moved to the present course at the Racecourse Reserve in 1914.

The Board of Management in 2009 decided not to proceed with Centennial celebrations in 2011 but instead to celebrate 100 years of golf at the Gate Pa links in 2014. Thereafter it is expected that the Club will recognise 1904 as the foundation year upon which to base significant milestones in the future.
