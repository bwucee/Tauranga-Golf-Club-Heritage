PROMINENT WOMEN PERSONALITIES

The following profiles are of a random assortment of women members who have made a mark through the years. They are not in any particular order, chronologically or otherwise. They are simply memories and anecdotes that came to mind to recent or present members who were asked for their suggestions. All those recognised below have made an indelible impression upon our history in their own particular way, some in competitive play, some in administration, some in each of these fields. All have been recognised by their fellow members as having contributed greatly to the very life-blood of their Club. We salute them all.

MISS ANNE MARK

Miss Mark joined the Club in 1935. She recalled that sheep then grazed the course and most greens were fenced. The course was the original layout that included holes in the racecourse oval. The ‘Hummocks’ and the ‘Drop’, both in the area of the present third, were difficult but exciting holes quite fondly remembered. Miss Mark tells her own story: “My first experience in helping Mrs. Hanna with the catering was in finding sticks to use as kindling to get the fire lit in the enormous Range in the Racing Club kitchen, and then cutting firewood to satisfy

its insatiable appetite. I first served the Ladies’ Committee as Handicap Manager in 1938, and from then on periodically until 1954. I held every office except President. I didn’t think I was the type for that. I think I won the Championship six times. We did not play it during the war years”. (Miss Mark did win the Championship on six occasions but would almost certainly have been successful in those ‘no competition’ years of 1941, 42, and 43, as she had been the winner in 1940, and again in 1944 and 1945. The other successes came in 1950 and finally in 1953 immediately before the ‘Wickham era’ began.)

Miss Mark continued: “In 1954 we formed the Bay of Plenty Ladies’ Golf Association and I was its President for three years. I then captained the Bay of Plenty/Thames Valley Russell Grace team until 1963. During that time we won the Cup three times and were runner-up once.

In 1955 I was elected to the LGU Council. The Ladies’ Golf Union was the National governing body at that time. It has now amalgamated with the Men’s governing body to form Golf New Zealand. I remained a member of the LGU Council until 1963, being honoured with the Presidency for the final two years. (It seems I overcame the aversion to accepting that role). In 1963 I had the wonderful experience of serving as non-playing Captain of the New Zealand team to the Commonwealth Tournament in Melbourne. In that same year the Tauranga Golf Club elected me to Life Membership. Despite all my previous experiences I rate that award as the most moving experience of my career in golf”.
