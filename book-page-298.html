CLUB TROPHIES

A full photographic record of all trophies has been compiled and a copy kept in the Club office. Consequently a full list of them will not be provided here. There are some trophies that warrant an explanation of their origins and others where it is important to acknowledge those people who donated them. In many cases the trophy donors were responsible for establishing a competition where none existed prior to their initiative. In most instances the donors have been notable figures in our history and their contributions to the development of the Club have been recorded elsewhere in this publication. This is especially true of the trophies involved in Veteran’s competitions, both Men and Women’s.

MEN’S TROPHIES

One of the earliest trophies to be contested was the Cameron Cup. Senior players compete for this over 36 holes of Stroke Play. Surprisingly the Cameron name is not derived from our main thoroughfare or from one of the Cameron families of the district. Miss Mae Cameron of Auckland was a regular visitor to Tauranga and enjoyed her visits so much that in 1924 she decided to present this cup to the club as a mark of her appreciation. The first of the two rounds of this competition serves as the qualifying round for the Aquila Cup. This is a senior handicap Match Play event.

The origin of the Aquila Cup has been lost with the passage of time. There was a coastal steamer by that name that made regular trips between Auckland and Tauranga and it is likely that some of the men manning her enjoyed a game of golf when in port. Perhaps the Captain or some of the men decided to express their appreciation for hospitality extended to them. I would like to think that this was the case.

The Waitemata Cup contested by Intermediate members in parallel with the Cameron Cup for senior players is an example of trophies that were presented to the Club in appreciation of the support given by the Club to the suppliers of certain products. Waitemata was a brand of beer produced by Dominion Breweries and supplied to the Club through Guiness Brothers who ran their business from premises at the Northern end of The Strand. Brownie and Joyce Carnachan, Colin and Betty Johnstone, were prominent Club members associated with the business, Betty and Joyce each being members of the

Guiness family. Those Intermediates qualifying for Match Play as a result of their performance in round one of the Waitemata Cup went forward to contest the Geo Wilcock Junior Cup, more commonly known simply as the Wilcock Cup. Life Member and Past President George Wilcock presented this trophy in an era when only two competition grades were recognised, Senior and Junior. When the Intermediate grade was instituted in 1946 it was felt appropriate that the Wilcock Cup should be contested by players at that level rather than by players in the newly defined junior grade.
