BERNICE WILLOUGHBY was a member from 1939 until 1999. She was another to win many competitions in her time. Bernice was a member early enough to remember the original clubhouse shared with the jockeys. Sometimes bales of hay were stored at one end. Occasionally these served as extra seating. Bernice fondly recalled scoring eagles at the sixth and fifteenth. Golf bags were much lighter then. Slung over the shoulder, as was the custom, they were more than heavy enough, especially towards the latter stages of a round. She remembered preferring the Bromford golf ball costing 1/9d. Bernice recalled Junior players always showing due deference to their elders in those times, perhaps implying that things were not quite the same today.

ANN BRANCH was a member from 1939 until 1991. She was a committee member in 1941. Her husband A J (Bronco) Branch owned a Sporting Goods store in the town that still operates under the name ‘Broncos’. Ann was one of the first members to wear slacks when playing, a trend much frowned upon by the more traditional of members, and certainly one that had long met with the disapproval of the hidebound members of the LGU hierarchy. What would they have thought of the on-course fashion of the young women players of today?

Bronco was a fine teaching Professional who gave lessons to Tauranga members, and others, over many years. His pupils were recognised for the slow tempo of their swings, and for the distinctive pause at the top of the backswing. Some felt his methods led to somewhat mechanical swings as opposed to more free flowing ‘natural’ swings. Whatever the rights or wrongs of these opinions there could be no denying the success enjoyed by his pupils. Notable amongst the more recent of these has been Peg Copestake, twice winner of our Senior Championship.

Peggy Turner, playing the knoll one day, pitched short of the bunker. The ball leaped the bunker, bounced between bunker and green, and ran unerringly into the hole. Ray Hay may have upstaged this. Ray was an unusual golfer who tended to mishit a series of three Iron strokes until close to the green when he was prone to finally hit one over the green. On one occasion Ray’s tee shot at the fifth flew low and fast before striking a tree behind the green. From there it bounced back across a bunker onto the green and into the hole. These things happen in golf yet many an excellent player goes through life without being able to claim a single ace.
