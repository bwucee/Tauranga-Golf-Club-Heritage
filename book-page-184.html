Water supply

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 184 / 305Next page →

The problem of obtaining adequate water at reasonable cost exercised the minds of successive Greens Committees over many years. The first supply was sourced from the Borough Council and was piped from Cameron Road and then pumped around the course from a small pump shed located behind the 11th tee. There was no storage tank so pressure depended upon the demands placed on the Borough supply by householders. The pipe system did not reach as far as the greens at the southern end of the course so they tended to deteriorate badly in the hot dry days of summer. The sprinklers were of the garden variety fed by hoses attached to taps near the green margins.

Consequently they required shifting two or three times in order to cover the entire green. Daytime watering was unpopular with members but a necessary evil. Grant Silvester was especially diligent in watering at night whenever practicable even though this meant he had to drive to the course at inconvenient times through the night in order to move sprinklers.

In 1963 the possibility of obtaining water from the Kopurereroa stream, which runs in close proximity to the western boundary of the Reserve, was first investigated. This concept was deferred indefinitely as it was considered to be a much more expensive option than continuing to utilise the Council supply. However the cost of Council water soon began to rise and it became once again imperative to seek a more permanent solution to the supply problem. In 1970 Brown Brothers from the Waikato were commissioned to investigate the prospect of obtaining water from a bore and promising sites were located near the stables on the 11th and to the west of the first tee.

It was decided to sink a bore at the latter site and this initially produced adequate supplies from a depth of some 45m. In July 1971 the Club minutes record that a quote had been obtained to extend the reticulation system to cover all greens at a cost of $5000 while another $12 000 was to be spent on the bore, deep well pump, motor and accessories. This work was proceeded with by utilising a $16 000 loan from the ANZ Bank which was guaranteed by a group of members. The terms of repayment were that $4000 was to be repaid in the first year, followed by a minimum of $1333 per year for nine years.

The interest rate was fixed at 7.5%. The completed system was still very basic and still involved placing and shifting sprinklers while the teeing grounds were not yet part of the network. Furthermore the flow from the bore gradually diminished and even though the present reservoir was constructed nearby it soon became apparent that the supply was never going to keep up with the demand. Forced to look elsewhere the committee again investigated piping water from the Kopurereroa stream. Fortunately the water quality was found to be appropriate for turf irrigation and the Club was granted a Water Right. Today water can be pumped from the stream to the 100,000-gallon reservoir at the rate of 7500 gallons per hour.

Initially it was planned to have a smaller reservoir but when Royce Lahood contacted the Boys’ College Mathematics

department to check the dimensions required he decided that adding an extra metre to the quoted radius would add little to the cost and be on the “safe side”. Little did Royce know what an enormous difference that 1m would make to the capacity of the reservoir but in hindsight it was a serendipitous decision on his part as the modern reticulation system the course now boasts is required to pump out a great volume of water when performing at full capacity.

The next development came in 1978 when after a long negotiation a new long-term lease was arranged with the Commissioner of Crown Lands.
