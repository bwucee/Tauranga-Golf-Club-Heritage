1913–1914

A new home at Gate Pa

The move to the Racecourse Reserve gave the club room to grow, but making a course took the work of its members.

The foreword places the first approaches to the Racecourse Reserve trustees in 1913. In 1914, the club took up a lease at the reserve and moved away from the increasingly crowded Domain grounds.

The land was exposed and initially unpromising. Members cleared gorse, protected greens with fences, built stiles and prepared the ground. Auckland professional Fred Rutter was engaged to lay out eighteen holes, including holes inside the racecourse oval.

The first lease was for seven years. That offered a home for golf without the long-term security the club would later obtain. The course became known as Gate Pa, although the book points out that the reserve is in Greerton.

A transport notice reproduced on page 27, dated 20 June 1914, arranged Saturday cars from the corner of Wharf Street and The Strand. Players had to tell the secretary by Friday night if they wanted a seat.

The photograph reproduced with that notice shows The Avenue, the original first hole (identified in the 2014 book as the eleventh). The photograph itself is not dated; its appearance alongside a 1914 notice does not establish when it was taken.

Source note

Adapted from Pars from the Past, edited by David G. S. Mowat (2014), pp. 3, 22, 24–28. Newspaper reports are described as cited in the book; original issues have not yet been checked for this preview.
