CLUBHOUSE REDEVELOPMENT

In February 1999 the Board established a Facilities Advisory Group (FAG). This group was tasked with the responsibility of advising the Board on the development and modernisation of the clubhouse facility to a standard more in keeping with a modern sports club providing food, beverage, and administrative services to a membership of more than 1000 together with annual visitor numbers in excess of 5000. This was in keeping with the Mission Statement adopted by the Board that reads: “To provide a quality golf experience for all players in surroundings of excellence”. The members of the group were Gary Baker and Tony Gallagher, representing the Board, Russell Black (Architectural Draftsman), Ismo Torvelainen (Engineer with Martin, McCaulay, Morton Ltd), and Grant Scoones (General Manager).

Later Alan Rydings volunteered his services as a qualified Quantity Surveyor/Estimator willing to assist with the tender process and with supervision of the contract as construction developed. Both Board representatives Gary and Tony had extensive experience in the construction industry while Russell, Ismo, and Alan had proven expertise in their respective fields. While Russell and Ismo were each paid for their professional services all three attended meetings and site visits on a voluntary basis as Club members wishing to contribute to the development of their

Club’s facilities.

The Board called a Special General Meeting in July 2000 for the purpose of placing before the membership a proposal to completely redevelop the Clubhouse. The building had first been erected in 1954 and had been extensively extended and refurbished on two subsequent occasions. In spite of this the clubhouse was overdue for a further upgrade in regard to the facilities it was required to provide and to the structural integrity of the building. The roof was of particular concern in the latter context. The Board had identified a number of areas where substantial expenditure would be required in the short term should a complete rebuild not be proceeded with.

These areas included the roof, the

lounge ceiling, all electrical and plumbing services, the floor coverings, the Bar and Kitchen units, and the outside decking. In view of all this necessary maintenance the Board recommended that the most appropriate solution would be to completely rebuild the structure above the existing basement level thus preserving the existing changing and storage rooms. The advantages of this approach were seen to be wide ranging. They included the urgent replacement of the roof, the creation of an appropriate and obvious front entry to the Clubhouse leading directly to the office, provision both of alternative entry points for players and much needed upstairs toilets. The provision of a committee room on a third floor level would be a welcome addition.

A special attraction would be the enhanced views possible from all points of the clubhouse resulting from the installation of larger, more modern windows throughout the upper levels. The poor visibility from within the clubhouse had long been a source of frustration to many a member. The concept proposed would open up sweeping views of the course, the racecourse, and in particular the superb vista provided by the hills to the East. Ultimately the views that were revealed when the new clubhouse was opened to the membership proved to be every bit as rewarding as had been anticipated. The Board did consider the idea of building a completely new clubhouse possibly located to the West of the putting green but rejected this as being impractical owing to financial considerations.
