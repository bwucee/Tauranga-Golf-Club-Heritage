Administration

(I doubt this count). Warwick Nicoll gave $200 to be held in trust, the interest to cover the prize associated with the Nicoll Cup. Installation of the Harvin irrigation system was completed in June. In response to some conflict between committee members and the green keepers the chairman of the Greens Committee, Des Wilson, said this: “In no way will I, or can I, instruct the Greenkeeper in the carrying out of his duties. My job is to provide a bridge of communication between him and the committee and to assist him with his programming.” The Racing Club were keen to install a 2200m starting point.

This would project into the 11th fairway. Pegs were placed to show the positioning. (Fortunately this development was not approved). Lance Bedford, an engineer, gave estimates relating to the Driving Range proposal. Earthworks were evaluated at $15 000, drainage at $2 000, and grassing at $5 000. 1980: A Special General Meeting voted to proceed with the Driving Range at a cost of $20 000. This same meeting approved the realignment of the entry road to separate Golf and racing areas. The 9th tee would move forward and the project would be at no cost to the Golf Club as the Racing Club would finance it through a raffle. 1981: The par of the 17th hole was increased to 5 with a stroke allocation of 15 on the motion of R Eaton and K Frith.

A motion to allow junior members access to the Club lounge wearing Club jerseys rather than jackets was defeated. All male members using the lounge must wear jackets. Our rental doubled from $1 000 to $2 000, set for the following five years. During development, and afterward, the Driving Range area was to be classified as a ’lateral hazard”. (This was again contrary to the rules of golf as there is no such thing as a ‘lateral hazard’ in those rules). 1982: Driving range opened with Alan Snape being authorised to provide practice balls for members though members were permitted to use their own. Members who were improperly dressed were to be served from the side bar that opened to the Dining Room.

The greens were now mown four times per week. 1983: The BOP Association advised that the larger ‘American’ ball would be mandatory in all competition under their jurisdiction. R Lahood attended his final meeting in June after 13.5 years service. The local rule re flagstick and four balls in the hole was rescinded. Reciprocal rights with Invercargill were discontinued. At the AGM a move to equate men and women’s subscriptions was defeated. Men’s were set at $225, women’s at $205. Long-playing membership was confirmed as applying after 40 years membership and age 65. Subscription was to be 50% of senior subscription.

Apparently the 1980 agreement with the Racing Club to finance the shift of the access road had been unsuccessful for it was now recorded that agreement had been reached on the issue. The clubs would now share the cost estimated at $10 900. 1984: The condition of the course over summer prompted 54 members to sign a petition claiming the greens were awful, the tees bad, weeds prolific, and the state of the trees unacceptable. (We had however developed some good paths during the employment of the then greenkeeper). The greens were now mown four days each week. Alan Snape requested release from his obligation to supply range balls owing to the high attrition rate.

The response was to pay him $2 000 per year as Range supervisor and grant him $500 to help defray losses over the previous two years.
