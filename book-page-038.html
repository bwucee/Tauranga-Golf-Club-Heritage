Out of the oval

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 38 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

NUMBER 5 - THE SPUR - PAR 3

The fifth hole proved to be a problem from the start and has perhaps remained so up to the present time. The hole has undergone significant changes, most recently in 2003 when the green was extensively remodelled. The original hole designed by Mr Murray was named ‘The Spur’ and was played from a raised tee, still discernible in the trees to the left of the fourth green, to a small green located where the new 7th green now lies. There was a difficult bunker with quite a steep face immediately in front of this green. This was a rather weak par three. Perhaps its only redeeming feature was that it minimised the amount of walking required to go from the 4th green to the 6th tee.

Some time later the green was relocated to its present position with no change in tee placement so that the hole remained a short par three with the tee shot being played over trees just short of the green. Generally you could not see the base of the pin from the tee.

In the days of the Invitation Tournament it was suggested that a more challenging hole could be provided for the professionals and top amateurs by building a tee in the area where it is now located so that the hole could be played parallel to the 7th fairway. This did prove to be more challenging and so the hole was retained for club play despite the extra walk required moving to the tee.

NUMBER 6 - MIRRIELEES - PAR 5

This was another hole developed when the club moved out of the oval. The excellent background provided to this hole by the positioning of the clubhouse on its present site was a welcome advantage of that siting. The hole is an excellent par five, if a shade short for the best players. It is notable for the Albatross twos scored by Norm Ducker, Ken Carter and Peg Copestake.

NUMBER 7 - MOUNT VIEW - PAR 5

This was the 12th hole and boasted a commanding view of Mount Maunganui before tree growth screened that out. Tree removal in the new Century restored the view. Several changes have been made to both tee and green and some would doubt the present design is the best that could be developed. It has long been a goal to establish a more equitable landing zone for the second shot or to find some other way to solve the problem of balls running off the side of the hill but this goal has proved elusive.

NUMBER 8 - McKINLAY’S - PAR 4 This was originally played as the 13th hole and known as ‘Lone Pine’. No one now seems to remember the tree for which it was named. McKinlay’s Paddock has long been the name of that part of the reserve North of the Racecourse so it was felt appropriate to retain the name for a hole in that area when the original ‘McKinlay’s’, a hole played into the racecourse, was discontinued.

NUMBER 9 - THE DIP - PAR 3

This new hole was named for the shallow dip that originally featured between tee and green when the tee was across the road at the top of the ridge overlooking McKinlay’s paddock. On race days players had a good view of the action should a race be taking place at the time they were on the tee. This was possible only after parking was discontinued on our fairways.
