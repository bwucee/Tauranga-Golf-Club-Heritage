With a great deal of courage the fledgling association decided, in 1953, to stage a five-day tournament from the 9th to the 13th of November that year. At the time the Association boasted just 23 members of which eight were on the committee. This was not unusual in many an organisation of the time. In any event the tournament must have proven to be a success as it was to be the forerunner of 24 annual tournaments to come.

During the first five years John McKenzie and Dick Phelan continued in office with their committee and Annual General Meetings would number no more than twelve to fifteen members. Play continued along the original lines until 1957 when Veterans’ day was changed to Thursday. This was not a problem as there were no Weekday Starting Sheets though sometimes a ball shoot was used. Thursday also gradually evolved into Businessman’s day and as their numbers increased so did the mutterings about ‘the old buggers in front’. Diplomacy finally came into it somewhere and ‘the old buggers’ changed their day to Tuesday where it has remained to this day.

Another change took place in the mid fifties when the Veterans’ Association renamed itself the Veterans’ Society in order to distinguish it from the NZ Veteran Golfers’ Association. Early members were never loath to dip into their own pockets to support the Society. For example we find that on the 4th of April 1957 when £3.15.0 was due to the NZVGA four members paid £1 each to meet the bill. It is not recorded what became of the extra five shillings. As early as 1955 the Vets had organised a summer competition known as the ‘Angus Plate’. The donor was Gavin Wallace who was often referred to as ‘Angus’. His trophy was awarded to the player with the best three nett medal rounds out of five nominated by the committee.

About this same time trophies were donated by Dick Phelan and George Willcock for annual competition and these, together with one later donated by Frank Macklow, are still contested on our annual calendar. Dick and George each embellished their trophies with prizes that the winners could keep. The winners were instructed to make their own purchase and send the account to the donor. In the following year a winner, assuming incorrectly that he could do the same, sent off a bill to Dick Phelan who promptly forwarded it to the committee. The committee did accept responsibility for the payment but made it clear the donors had not undertaken to supply the prize on an annual basis.

The committee also wrote a letter of apology to Mr Phelan.

Membership of the Society remained fairly static until as late as 1965 when Ken McIntyre can recall reporting to Jack Babington outside the Pro Shop where Jack was busily engaged in scribbling names on the back of an old envelope. Those who had reported were given a numbered disc and these were later drawn from a hat. On that day four were sent off the 7th tee, four off the 3rd, and two fours off the first. That was it and the field, about the standard number for that time, were away.
