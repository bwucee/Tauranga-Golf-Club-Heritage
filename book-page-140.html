Administration

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

In 1904 it was a Man’s World run by men for the benefit of men. In the realm of golf this was possibly even more pronounced than it was elsewhere. At the original Club based at the Domain women were welcome so long as they did not interfere with the ‘proper playing of the game’ as practised by the men. This is evidenced from Local Rule 9 of the card that read: “ On Saturday afternoons Gentlemen may pass Ladies on any part of the course”. Rennie Wallis’ recollection of a Flag Match played by the Ladies on a Saturday in the early years of golf at Gate Pa, as described in Chapter 11, further emphasises the position women golfers held in the Club hierarchy.

The Club Committee, which controlled all things related to the course, the finances, the clubhouse, social activity, employment, and men’s competition golf, was made up entirely of men until the election of 1974. That year marked the emancipation of women golfers at Tauranga. Evelyn (Nen) Sim, backed by the strong support of a large contingent of women members, threw her hat into the ring for election to the ‘Men’s Committee’. Nen’s election was never in doubt and she proved to be a wise choice to represent the women of the Club and an invaluable member of the committee over a significant period.

The constitution had never stood in the way of women serving on the committee and, had the women wished it, their voting strength could easily have seen women elected at any time prior to Nen’s pioneering success. Under the constitution women could have been elected to any position on the committee, even that of Men’s Captain! Possibly they were perfectly happy to run their own affairs during the week and let the men get on with the complexities of maintaining the course and facilities. Women did have full voting rights at annual meetings and could thus influence major issues should they so wish. We can also be sure that many a committeeman listened carefully to what his wife had to say before casting his vote on certain contentious issues.

The women members were very efficient in the organisation of their own affairs. Their competition programming, their handicapping, their social activities, were all organised with enthusiasm and expertise. In many of these respects they could have shown the men a thing or two, and probably sometimes did.

For four years Nen Sim carried the women’s banner on the Club Committee until 1979 when joined by Loeta Hutcheson. At the AGM in August 1978 the women members moved to have a woman member appointed to the General Committee annually. The motion was eventually carried on a show of hands after the voice vote had proved indeterminate. Some of the opposition to the move came from the fact that the opportunity already existed for women to have adequate representation. Hadn’t Nen Sim already demonstrated that? Loeta Hutcheson was subsequently appointed to the position and was

followed by Peg Cross in 1980. From then until the advent of the Board there were usually two women on committee, one elected and one appointed. Noeline Shiel was an elected member from 1987 through to 1994, a record term for a woman member. Later in 1994 when the Board became a reality Noeline was elected Vice President. Perhaps this paved the way for Peg Copestake who became the first woman to be elected Club President when she succeeded Gary Baker in 2000, a landmark event for the new Millennium. It had taken nearly 100 years but now the emancipation of women within the Club was complete with a woman being elected to the highest office.

It was not long before Anna Brabyn was to continue the trend by assuming the Presidential chair.
