It was further resolved to continue to hold the Christmas function on a Monday in November not designated for competition although some members were in favour of holding the meeting following a morning round of golf.

Mixed events in cooperation with the men vets proved very successful and popular with all those who participated during those earlier years. Following such an event in October 1992 the men donated three dozen balls to the lady vets to be used as prizes on their competition days. By 1993 membership had risen to 92. An informal ten holes of golf were organised to precede the Christmas luncheon and AGM that year. The Treasurer was able to report a profit of almost $600 on the year’s activities leading up to the 1994 AGM. Clearly the organization was in very good heart. Much of the profit generated was the result of the generosity of members both in donating prizes for the raffle table, and in purchasing tickets in those raffles.

I wonder how often someone won the prize they had donated.

From the beginning the Lady Vets affiliated themselves to the BOP/TV Lady Veterans’ Society with members paying a joining fee of $5. The District Society organises four official tournaments each year at two-monthly intervals in addition to a ‘Home Links’ teams competition and the Christmas Hamper events held in the five zones. All these events are open to women aged 55 and over. The tournament events are run in age groups, the highest of these being for golfers of 75 years or more. Shirley Crawford represented Tauranga on the District committee for a number of years before handing over to Kath Christie, another stalwart supporter and promoter of Veteran Ladies’ Golf.

In 1994 members voted in favour of travelling to Walton rather than Okoroire for the annual bus trip. The subsequent trip proved very successful. The inclusive cost of $20 for bus transport, green fees, and lunch at the Walton Club being considered very good value indeed. This experience opened up the idea of varying the destination chosen for these trips from year to year and in 1995 the trip was made to Paeroa. Though some found that course a little too undulating for their liking the trip was voted a success by the majority who travelled there. The reaction to course steepness was intensified by a later trip to Waihi following which it was cautioned that greater care needed to be exercised in selecting future venues as members were “not as young and fit as they once had been”.

The equitable distribution of golf balls as competition prizes appears to have been a contentious issue over a number of years. A Special General Meeting was even called in 1995 to debate the issue. At the meeting a proposal to divide the competition into three groups was lost and a subsequent motion to award balls based on Stableford points in one grade was carried by a wide margin.

In 1996 the Club was requested to stage the Home Links Teams event but when the Club Management required payment of $10 per participant the event was transferred elsewhere. The Lady Vets committee did try to negotiate a better deal with Management and were very disappointed that their pleas fell on deaf ears. They felt they had been let down by their own Club as the fee sought appeared to be excessive in comparison to those charged by other clubs in the District.
