The professionals come to play

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

The committee of 1952/53 sought ways to promote the club to the rest of the country in order to attract green fee visitors and to encourage membership applications from residents and newcomers alike. The age of closed membership had not yet arrived and increased membership together with greater returns from green fee paying visitors were seen as the path to prosperity at club level. The committee reasoned that a prestigious tournament featuring leading professional and amateur players from throughout New Zealand would have a good chance of achieving these aims. They were also cognisant of the fact that professionals in particular lacked opportunities to showcase their skills to the public and it would be beneficial to all parties if the professional game were to be encouraged.

Consequently the committee contacted the NZPGA and, with their full support, settled on a 54 hole Stroke Play Tournament to be staged on a weekend in June 1953.

That first tournament was judged to be a great success and the club has since continued to promote itself and the game on a wider stage by involving itself in the hosting of professional tournaments whenever the opportunity has arisen. In that first Invitation Tournament a field of 18 professionals competed for prize money of £200 while 42 invited amateurs made up a quality field. F X (Frank) Buckler, then of Auckland, averaged one over par through the three rounds to comfortably lead the professional field on 216, four strokes clear of Alex Murray, also of Auckland and no stranger to the course as he acted as adviser to the club on course design and development.

J A (James) Paterson, then of Wanganui but soon to fill the vacant professional position at Tauranga, finished third. Scoring by the first few professionals was steady if unspectacular, the best rounds being par 71s by the two leaders.

New Zealand Maori champion T P (Tori) Jones of Taumarunui matched these par rounds in his final round to head off tournament favourite A J (Andy) Fletcher by a single stroke. Amateurs P G F (Pax) Smith and W W Smith, each of Titirangi, returned the only sub par rounds, each one under the card.

President Syd Randell in his comments at the prize ceremony admitted the promotion had been an ambitious one for the club but the support of the players and spectators had made the effort well worthwhile. “We are proud of our course and this tournament will have done much to popularise it and make it known in other parts of the North Island and New Zealand generally”. Mr Randell went on to say that the tournament had been staged with the dual objectives of showing players what a good course Tauranga was and to provide young players with the opportunity to see the best New Zealand golfers in action. Both objectives had been achieved.

On the Tuesday following the tournament the BOP Times published a number of comments by participants. These included: Frank Buckler: “A beautiful course in excellent order. The only fault that could be found is that it might be short. Otherwise it is up to New Zealand championship standard. Compared to Auckland courses at this time of the year the condition of fairways and greens is simply amazing. The hazards are good and well laid out”.
