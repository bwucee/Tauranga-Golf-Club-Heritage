Men’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 61 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

Norman and his wife Nancy came to the Club in 1936 after experiencing golfing success at Dannevirke, Napier and Stratford before moving to Tauranga. Norman’s lowest handicap was 2 yet he played with a rudimentary set of five clubs: a brassie (2 wood), a 3 iron, a niblick (5 iron), a mashie (8/9 iron), and a putter. Norman never managed to win the Tauranga Club Championship to add to his successes at previous Clubs indicating that the standard of local play was showing marked improvement. The two outstanding players of the period were H J (Jack) Bateman and F W (Fred) Ward. These two won the Tauranga Open Championship in six of the seven years from 1946 to 1953.

Fred won again in 1958.

Jack Bateman, in addition to his Open successes, won the Club Championship in 1934, and then three more times in the four years from 1946 to 1949. To highlight his career he won the inaugural Bay of Plenty Champion of Champions title held at Whakatane in 1948, successfully defending it at Tauranga the following year. Further success came the way of Tauranga with victory in the Bay of Plenty District Golf Association (BOPDGA) Senior Inter Club Shield in 1951. Jack and Fred were vital members of the winning team. This shield was the forerunner of the BOP Championship Pennant Series. It was to be another 23 years before Tauranga were again able to hold the shield aloft, winning it three years in a row from 1974.

Fred Ward’s many golfing exploits have been included in a later chapter. His influence, both as

competitor and as administrator, projected far into the future of the Club.

Prior to 1948 five men were honoured with Life Membership of the Club. They were E T Baker, Denis Ivory, R T (Bert) Goulding, A J Mirrielees, and P H Roach. While we know that Denis Ivory (1936-39) served as Club President little is known of the service these men gave to the Club other than in the case of Mr Mirrielees whose contribution is recorded elsewhere. Mr Roach won the Senior Championship in 1919 and Bert Goulding won the same championship in 1933. We can be sure that each of these men together with Mr Baker made outstanding contributions to the growth and development of the Club and to the course at Gate Pa.

Life Membership would not have been granted lightly, especially in those times when members were more intimately involved in Club and Course management than they are today.

In the final year or two of the Forties C Steedman, H B Lusk, and Arthur Towsey were next to be honoured with Life Membership. Arthur’s long service as a member of the committee and as voluntary Keeper of the Bar has been well documented. He was a superb ‘Mine Host’ over a lengthy period during which he ruled his domain with a somewhat flexible ‘Rod of Iron’. Mr Lusk was intimately involved in the early design of the course when the Club first made the move to the Racecourse Reserve while memories of “Steedy” appear to have faded with the passing of his contemporaries.

S G (George) Wilcock will be remembered each year when the Intermediate men compete for the Wilcock Cup, a handicap Match Play event. George, who served as President from 1950-52 following his years as a member of the committee, was elected to Life membership in 1949. One year later George was elected Vice President of the BOPDGA
