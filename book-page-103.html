Women’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

In 1980 Joyce was elected to Life Membership of the Tauranga Golf Club and was still enjoying a few holes well into the new Millennium. Many wondered how Joyce managed to retain so youthful an appearance as the years went by. I am sure it was a reflection of her positive outlook, her interest in those around her, and her general zest for life. The game of golf does seem to keep a lot of participants in good health and spirit longer than is the case with many other people.

ALISON FAULKNER, who joined in 1960, recalled with pleasure the stricter etiquette that prevailed in an earlier age. Dress requirements were quite rigid. The initial Club uniform comprised grey skirts and navy cardigans. Subsequently a uniform featuring green skirts, a cream jersey, and a navy blouse replaced this. Another change meant the ladies all had to get Air Force blue skirts and jumpers with the Club insignia if they wanted to be up to date. Alison remembered Mrs. Robertson, Mrs Wagstaff, and Noeline Leech as caterers who went out of their way to ensure their service was to the satisfaction of all members. Alison recalled Nen Sim’s overseer role of the Social and House side of Club activities as being exemplary.

SHIRLEY EATON joined in 1963. In 1978 Shirley was the Ladies’ Club Captain and remembered that a highlight in the competitive history of the Club occurred that year when a team made up of Una Wickham, Val Hemingway, and Susan Harkin won the Bay of Plenty section of the Peter Stuyvesant National Team Championship played at Omanu. The team was made up of the three Club champions at Senior, Intermediate, and Junior levels. The sponsors then flew the team to Wellington, all expenses paid, to contest the National final at Heretaunga. The team did well to finish in fourth place out of a field of thirteen teams. Peter Stuyvesant was a popular brand of cigarette and tobacco companies were very active sponsors of a wide range of sporting events at that time.

They sponsored a similar team event for male golfers.

NOELINE SHIEL joined the Club in 1972. In 1987 she was elected to the Club Committee and continued to serve that committee until 1994 when the Board of Management was instituted. In that same year Noeline was elected Vice-President, the first woman ever to occupy that position. Noeline was an outstanding Chairperson of the Social Committee putting an enormous amount of time, energy, and imagination into the role. With husband Des providing unstinting support and assistance Noeline did much to foster club spirit at a time when counter attractions in other spheres made it more difficult to encourage members to support Club activities. The work Noeline and Des did in facilitating Friday night Twilight golf was truly appreciated by those who enjoyed that activity over many happy years.

DOROTHY GARLAND came to Tauranga from Reporoa in 1973. She served the Ladies’ Club as both Captain and Vice-Captain and also served as President of the BOPTV Association for four years during which time Jocelyn Glen assisted her in the role of Secretary.

ROSEMARY CLARK joined in 1981 and made an immediate impact on the competitive Club scene. At the end of that first year she annexed the Senior Championship. From 1991 to 1997 she won the title on another five occasions. Another four successive championships fell to Rosemary from 2001 - 2004.
