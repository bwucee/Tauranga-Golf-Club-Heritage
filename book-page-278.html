In this chapter I will attempt to look into the future to see how the Club and Course will develop during the next 100 years. The chapter will read as though written by someone looking back over the second century of Golf at Gate Pa. Sadly I will not know how perceptive these predictions prove to be.

THE SHORTCOURSE FACILITY

The second 100 years of our Club really started with the establishment of the new short course facility to the left of the second fairway. Suitable practice facilities, or the lack thereof, had long been a problem for the Club, restricted as it had been from the beginning by the lack of space upon which to expand. The available acreage was barely adequate to establish 18 holes of sufficient length to provide a realistic challenge, let alone allow for practice facilities. The problem became even more evident as advances in technology, particularly in ball technology, reached a peak in the early years of the new Century.

The land the new facility occupies was part of a block purchased by Jack Grant’s Grandfather over 100 years ago. Jack and his wife, well known, long standing, Club member Sybil Grant, farmed the land for many years. Older members will recall Jack and Sybil making hay on the area then transporting it across the second fairway to link with the driveway to Cameron Road.

The Tauranga City Council recently purchased the land, and some perceptive members of our Club saw the potential offered by the relatively flat area adjoining the second fairway. The legal expertise of Board member Michael J Smith overcame any possible obstacles and satisfactory lease terms were negotiated with the Council. The Short Course team then investigated the available products and made the decision to use synthetic materials to establish permanent, low maintenance, tees and greens. Field Turf NZ were the preferred product supplier and they were contracted to supply and install three greens together with the associated tees, a large practice mat, and an additional large green to be known as the High Performace area.

Our own staff established Windsor Green fairways using material harvested from our established fairways.

2013 Club Champion Dave Cooke, in tandem with Club Professional Jay Carter, designed the course to serve a wide variety of purposes for a wide variety of people. The course can be used as a short six-hole Pitch and Putt course, a practice area for anything from chipping to medium iron play, or as a versatile teaching facility where individuals or large groups might be accommodated. There is even a bunker where members can develop those skills so frequently required during most rounds. This particular feature, as with others, is the first of its kind in New Zealand. A product named Bunker Matrix was laid in the base of the bunker, this asphalt derived product encapsulates the clay base so as to keep contamination of the sand to a minimum.
