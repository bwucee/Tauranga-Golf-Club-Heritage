The Una Wickham Memorial Tournament

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 274 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

2003 While the 2002 final featured a player of Asian descent the 2003 tournament could be considered the real beginning of the trend that saw Asian women rising to the top of women’s golf in New Zealand. Five of the Championship flight were this year of Asian descent. The failure of one of these to arrive from Auckland infuriated Tournament organisers especially as they were not advised of the non-appearance until 15 minutes before tee off. Worse, the player was seeded number one for the tournament. Tournament officials were equally upset upon learning that a National training camp had been scheduled in Wellington for the same dates. Remarkably Women’s Golf NZ claimed to be “unaware of the status of the Una Wickham event”.

Sixteen top players were in Wellington for the camp. Women’s Golf NZ did adjust their calendar to avoid a clash in the future.

The final featured two young Asian women with 16 yr. old Bo Bae Kim defeating 12 yr. old Sharon Ahn one up. The power generated by these young players was evidenced on many occasions with drives of 250m and more being a regular feature of their play. The 15th hole that is normally played as a par five by women club players was reached by Ahn with a drive and five iron. Many a man could watch these young women and learn that hitting it long does not necessarily require brute strength. The feature that stands out is the co-ordinated rotation of the upper body with little apparent movement of the legs and knees.

2004 Stacey Tate (Huapai), a young player of genuine potential, defeated Mi Kyung Kim (Pupuke) 3/1 in the final after defeating the top seed, Tina Howard, in the semi-final. The final was notable for the over enthusiastic behaviour of Kim’s father who caddied for his daughter. At the sixth hole the referee ruled that Kim had conceded Tate a short putt for a half based on something she had said that caused Tate to pick up her ball. While Kim made no protest her father began to rage at the referee for “not being fair to his daughter” though it was difficult to understand what he was concerned about. When he started to protest again at the eighth tee the referee suggested it might be better if he ceased caddying and left his daughter to get on with the game.

He then handed the bag to another family member and the match continued in a better atmosphere. It sometimes seems that young players, especially those of Asian descent, would do better if their parents left them to find their own way in the game.

2005 Perhaps the most interesting match of this tournament took place during the second round on the Saturday afternoon. 13 yr. old Zoe Beth Brake (Kawerau) and Penny Newbrook, a former New Zealand number one, finished all square at the end of regulation play. At that stage darkness had set in so the pair set out virtually at dawn the following morning with Brake securing the win at the first extra hole.

Brake later claimed that she had looked up to Penny as her idol from the time she, Brake, had begun to play as a six yr. old so she had mixed feelings following her win.

The final featured two Asian women with Jackie Shin defeating last year’s runner up Mi Kyung Kim one up. Shin played the first nine in two under par to turn four up before her short game began to falter. Just one up with three to play a win at the sixteenth gave Shin just enough breathing space to hold out her rival.
