The first tee

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 13 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

With the patience of a Job, But the adjectives came naturally When we failed to hit the ‘Globe’.

He taught us how to tee a ball,

And from a bunker get; But he did not teach us stymie,

As it just is not etiquette.

And when we thought the game was done,

And finished for the day,

We had his innovation- The nineteenth hole to play.

Now the nineteenth hole is not a hole,

We never called out ‘Fore!’ But we always liked a halved one, And sometimes played ‘one more’.

We’ll think of him when he is gone,

And is miles and miles away. We’ll drink his health and happiness,

When the nineteenth hole we play.

He taught us how to Double Hearts,

Hold four aces, get a slam, And if we happened to revoke,

Why, he never used a damn.

There’ll be no such thing as Doubled Spades,

And Golf will ne’er be seen, And the little bird will build a nest,

In the hole on the putting green.

We will keep the Golf Balls snowy white,

And the Golf Clubs clean we’ll scrub,

In honour of the father of, The Tauranga Golfing Club.

We wish to him and Mrs. Brown,

Long life where’re they roam, But the best of wishes we can wish,

Is good health and safely home.
