The other hole in one came during the final of the Senior Four-Ball when Dad’s partner Cary Arneil holed out at the Knoll during the afternoon round. As I have said the base of the pin was not visible from the tee in those days and when I suggested the ball might have gone in the hole I was ignored. Nor was there any acknowledgement of my good guess when the ball was found in the hole. My pair went on to win the match just one up and, back in the clubhouse, Billy Castell, one of the opposing pair, announced that he had scored a two only to lose the hole.

I remembered that he had actually holed out in the alternate hole only a couple of feet from where his ball settled. Of course I said nothing (I would have had my ear clipped for butting in) as Billy’s version made a good story. One outcome of their win was that Cary, handicap 2, and Dad, handicap 4, were selected to represent the Club in the BOP Champion of Champions Foursomes which they duly won.

On another occasion I heard that Ron Parkes, a gentle man and a fine golfer, had made an ace at the Gully. I got excited but Dad explained that Ron had duffed his first shot into the swamp before holing his next for a three – a result known as a busted eagle. This was just subsequent to the rule change that imposed a penalty stroke in addition to counting the original stroke and his fellow competitors were disappointed that Ron’s perfect shot had not earned him a deserved birdie as would have been the case a month or two before.

During those years when I caddied regularly Fred Ward was the Club’s best player and always the man to beat in the championship. Fred was the manager of McKenzies Department Store in town and had been a pilot in the Air Force where Dad had also served so they got on very well. At the time Fred did not play a lot of golf and I seem to remember he got married about that time when he was probably in his late thirties. He seemed an old man to my young eyes. One year Fred went off and played in the New Zealand Open finishing 19th. He came back with a centre shafted Schenectady putter that he had been awarded as a result of his placing amongst the amateurs in the field.

The putter aroused much discussion, especially with regard to its legality, though Fred, like everyone else, continued to use his old blade putter.

In the first qualifying round of the1952 Championship Dad shot his best ever score, a 70, to lie equal

with Fred. Unfortunately Dad was ten strokes worse the following week but still qualified second. He went on to meet Fred in the final where he went down 3/2. On the day before that final Dad had played in the 36 hole final of the Aquilla Cup where he lost to Billy Castell. Dad was very disappointed to have lost two thirty six hole finals in the one weekend despite scoring birdies on eleven of the fifteen par five holes played.

I remember Fred once hitting his ball into the top of the bank above the swamp at the Gully. We eventually found the ball embedded in the muddy bank.
