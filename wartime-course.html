1939–1945

Nine holes and a different purpose

During the Second World War, part of the course was put to food production while a smaller course remained in play.

The book describes the course being reduced to nine holes during the war. The eighth, ninth and tenth fairways were ploughed and planted with potatoes, while other areas were left to grow hay.

Greenkeeper Mr Reeves maintained the remaining nine holes with limited equipment. The racecourse and its buildings also served as headquarters for the National Reserve, with members training there.

The Ladies’ minutes summarised in the history record fundraising for the Patriotic Shop in 1940. These contributions place the club within the wider demands of wartime, beyond the reduced playing programme.

The men’s senior championship appendix records no competition from 1941 to 1945. Accounts of the women’s wartime championships vary within the book: the appendix lists Anne Mark in 1941, while her profile describes that year as a gap. That discrepancy remains open for checking rather than being silently resolved.
