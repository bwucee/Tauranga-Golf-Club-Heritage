Twilight golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 197 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

Hokowhitu. With the encouragement and assistance of Noel and others Gary oversaw the instigation of a Wednesday evening competition that initially attracted fourteen teams, each consisting of four players.

The number of teams grew rapidly as the popularity of the competition became widely recognised in the Business Community and soon reached the maximum possible field of 36 teams. In spite of the large field of 144 the play flows relatively freely with everyone usually back in the clubhouse by 8pm. following the 5.30 start. The best three of the four Stableford scores of each team are counted to decide the winner of the teams match each night and also aggregated throughout the season. Team and individual prizes are awarded each evening and, at the end of the season the top teams in both Stableford and Match Play throughout the year are awarded major prizes.

Once again ordinary Club cards were used in the early years of this competition until some anomalies became apparent. For example a male five handicapper starting at the sixth tee would receive all five of his strokes in the ten holes he played whereas the same player starting at the fourteenth would receive only one stroke. A new card was consequently designed so as to be as fair as possible to all participants. Two principles were considered in allocating strokes on the new card. The first was that players starting on any one of the 18 holes should receive as close to one half of their handicap as

possible. A secondary requirement was to consider the relative difficulty of scoring Stableford points on a particular hole. These two principles are not entirely compatible so compromise was a necessary part of the process.

At first no consideration was given to developing a handicapping system for twilight golf as participants were all members of Tauranga or other local Clubs and simply used their Club handicaps. As the popularity of the events grew an increasing number of participants with no Club affiliation became

involved, especially in the Business House competition. It became obvious a handicap system especially designed for Twilight Golf was needed. At first the handicapper used his discretion in dealing with those who returned particularly good scores. This soon led to dissatisfaction as real or imagined disparities arose in the way similar performances were treated. In particular there appeared to be a reluctance to allow handicaps to move outward with the result that one really good performance could render the competitor uncompetitive for the remainder of the season. A feature of Nine or Ten Hole golf is that players are often able to produce exceptional scoring which they would be unlikely to sustain through the full 18 holes.

A regular club handicap is not necessarily a sound guide to a player’s likely performance over the shorter course. To overcome these problems a new system was developed using suggestions found in the NZGA Handicapping and Course Rating Booklet. This suggested that a player should score about 2.2 points per hole when playing to their handicap. On that basis 22 points was the expected score over ten holes so a buffer zone was set from 20 to 23 points inclusive where no handicap adjustment would result. Points below 20 would allow the handicap to move out one-quarter stroke to a maximum of one stroke. Points from 24 to 26 inclusive would each incur a half stroke reduction, while any points above 26 would each reduce the handicap by a further stroke.

Thus someone scoring 30 points over the ten holes, as happened more often than might have been expected, would find themselves cut 5.5 strokes.
