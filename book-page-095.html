MISS UNA WICKHAM

Una Wickham was born in Tauranga and began playing at a young age on a small nine-hole course. Armed with just one club and a putter Una followed in the wake of her parents. Her father was a very keen cricketer and encouraged Una to take an active part in that game and in all other sports she may have had access to. As a result Una excelled in tennis, hockey, and cricket as a boarder at Sacred Heart Girls’ School in Auckland where she was a student from 1937-39. In 1940 Una enrolled at Auckland Teachers’ Training College. On graduating from there she was appointed Sports Mistress at Diocesan Girls’ School.

One year later she graduated with a Diploma of Physical Education from Otago University. About the same time she started to play golf seriously and quickly demonstrated her considerable talent. As a member of The Grange Club she had reduced to a handicap of four by 1948 and eventually played to a low of plus two.

On a trip to England in 1953 Una was contacted by the Manager of a New Zealand representative team that was in England at the time. One of the team had been forced to return to New Zealand on account of illness and, in Una’s words: “by the greatest of good fortune I was asked to fill the gap”.

Una played with distinction even though equipped with “the wickedest set of clubs ever seen at that level. Two woods, three irons, a bunker club, a putter, and a borrowed eight iron”. In that same year she played in the British Open at Troon, and again at Portland the following year. In both years she claimed she was “early eliminated”. Not without a fight we would warrant. One aftermath of the exposure New Zealand women were then having to International competition was the call for longer, tougher courses in this country to better prepare the top women players for these contests. It was clear that our short and open courses fell well short of the standard required to extend our most promising players so as to condition them to compete with the best overseas.

Calls for the simple remedy that these top women should regularly play from the men’s tees were generally ignored. In addition club committees, dominated as they were by men, were not much interested in redesigning courses to suit women members. The best Una was able to do on her home course was to see some of the forward tees eliminated. Consequently those on the 2nd, 6th, 7th, and 16th were eliminated though that on the 7th has since been reinstated. Seldom do the best women players get to play from the back tees at holes

like the 3rd, 11th and 13th.

Una first joined the Tauranga Club in 1954 and was selected that year to represent Bay of Plenty (later Bay of Plenty/Thames Valley) in the Russell Grace Inter- provincial Tournament. That year the team won the tournament and by 1956 Una had been selected as number one, a position she retained for twenty years. The team followed the 1954 win with further wins in 1955, 59, 64, 66, and 75. At National level Miss Wickham represented New Zealand on eight occasions including Tasman Trophy and Commonwealth contests. In 1959 and 69 she won the Amateur Championship of New Zealand and in 1968, in a tribute to her longevity in the game, won the Mellsop Cup - the New Zealand Amateur
