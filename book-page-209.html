Another change and another secretary, a nice chap from Inglewood called Royce Lahood. Royce was another who was very good at delegating jobs and once again Pat Massey found herself doing most of the office work so far as I could see.

Royce was an ideal public relations man and easy to get along with. He fitted in very well and was soon right at home in the Club. One day he was doing something urgent so he brought the handicap books to me and asked if I would finish them for him. He explained the system and soon I got into the job. Royce never again did that job. It was interesting doing it but very time consuming. Any little bit of spare time I had was taken up doing handicapping. Hours of work spread over the week, masses of cards spread over the pool table, and the pins to move on the handicap board ready for weekend play.

Tuesday was Royce’s day off and sometimes Pat would tell me to go and have a game with the Vets. I would go off in the first group then rush in to get ready for the first few wanting drinks. It was very nice but a bit of a rush so I did not do it very often. Pat was a delightful woman who was wonderful to work with and a great asset to the Club. I think she also made Royce look good for the amount of work she did on his behalf.

The seasons slipped by, one year much the same as the last. The wages did improve but the work seemed to get harder and harder. Perhaps I just got older and older. I wish I had kept a day-to-day diary of all the things that happened. It is very hard to recall the sequence of events but I did enjoy the job because of all the good people I met and worked with over those years’.

Those who knew Arnold and his little Morris van would empathise with his description of this manoeuvre. Arnold and the van were both small of stature and you can be sure the task would have challenged much bigger men with more suitable transport. There was a fascinating incident involving this van and a blackbird, if something extending over a period of months could be called an incident. Arnold’s van had a mirror mounted on the front right hand wing as was common at that time and he would park the van where the bar manager still parks at the back of the clubhouse. One particular blackbird would perch every day on the wing and look at himself in the mirror.

Of course he did not recognise himself but saw a rival in the mirror and would hop around the back to remonstrate with that rival. Finding nothing he would come to the front again. He would continue this for an hour or more and for day after day eventually wearing a ring of paint off the van right around that mirror.
