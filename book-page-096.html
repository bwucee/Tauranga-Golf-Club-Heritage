Women’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

Stroke Play Championship. Her unparalleled sporting abilities were showcased by her selection to represent New Zealand in Cricket. Una also represented Auckland at hockey while in tennis she won the Championship at Sacred Heart (now Barradene College) in three successive years. Has any athlete, man or woman, equalled or surpassed such a record?

Dress was much more conventional and officialdom much more hidebound in Una’s heyday. Nancy Hare recalled that when Una returned from England where she had been living she was selected to make her Russell Grace debut and having suffered a spell of ill health she opted to wear black slacks when the weather turned nasty on the first morning. Not only were the LGU officials scandalised but influential Herald Sports writer Terry Mclean saw fit to write half a column the next day deploring the “dreadful attire” of this newcomer, and claiming that such garments were quite out of place on any golf course, let alone at an event of National significance.

This was not the only time Una ran into controversy on the issue. One year she featured in the New Zealand Championship final and requested permission of the LGU to play in Slacks as the weather was cold and unpleasant, conditions that adversely affected her health. Permission was refused.

At Club level Una won the Tauranga Senior Championship on no less than sixteen occasions, the first seven in succession starting in that first year of joining in 1954. Olga Johnson (5 times), Pat Kidd (three times), Peg Copestake (twice), and Kath Johnstone (once) interrupted Una’s winning streak over the succeeding years until Una’s final triumph in 1980.

In 1975 Una was honoured with election to the executive of the Ladies’ Golf Union of New Zealand (LGU). There she excelled in her administrative role, especially in the area of recognising and developing potential talent amongst young players. This was an interest she had exercised during her time as a teacher at Tauranga Intermediate School where she had encouraged John Ward, Noel Johnston, and others to start the game at a young age. Both John and Noel went on to represent Bay of Plenty in the Freyberg Rosebowl as the Inter-provincial Championship was then known. When the inaugural New Zealand Mixed Foursomes Championship was scheduled to be held in Wellington in 1973 Una suggested to the young Noel that they should try their luck as a team in the event.

Noel accepted with enthusiasm and the pair annexed the title in style, winning their preliminary matches by the convincing margins of 6/5, 4/3, and 5/4, on the way to a convincing 3/2 win over a Wellington pairing in the final. In her self-effacing style Una had nothing but praise for the form shown by her young partner.

Una’s untimely death in 1983 was a shock to all in the golfing fraternity. Her memory was celebrated through the institution of the prestigious Una Wickham Memorial Match Play Tournament that was held at Tauranga for many years.

MISS JOAN MIRRIELEES

Miss Mirrielees was the first to undertake a compilation of the history of the Tauranga Golf Club, beginning the research for this as early as 1974, and presenting an incomplete and unpublished version in time for the Jubilee celebrations of 1986.
