Women’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 90 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

Women, or Ladies as they were then known, were accepted as members from the earliest years of the Club. The initial subscription of 5/- was half that of male members and remained at a discount until the 1990s when it was moved that all senior members, men and women, should pay the same subscription. From the beginning the Club was run by men, for men. In common with worldwide practise the course design was intended to cater for the way men played the game and women had simply to make the best of it by playing from forward tees. Nothing much has changed in that regard although recent club and ball developments have meant that some of the original design features of our courses offer a more appropriate challenge to the women players of today than they once did.

Life Member Rennie Wallis (nee Daines) told a story from the earliest days of golf at Gate Pa that perhaps illustrates the standing of women in the club at the time. The women, ever inclined to be more innovative and adventurous in their competitions than were the men, decided to hold a Flag Match for their Saturday competition. At the time their club day was on the Saturday prior to the men’s starting times. Rennie described the event as follows: “there were about a dozen of us and we were each told to bring along a flag in our bags. We did this and each of us planted our flag at the point where our handicap strokes ran out.

A sea of waving flags greeted the first men as they approached the final green at the end of their rounds. On entering the clubhouse these men told us in no uncertain terms what we could do with our flags! Our Captain dashed out red-faced to remove the offending obstructions”.

Rennie recalled some of those early Lady Members: Mrs. Cook (Margaret Wrigley), Mrs. Wickham (Nan Daines), Mrs. Maltby (Violet Carr), Mrs. Hanna (Elsie Sweet), Mrs. Heald, Mrs. Jordan, Mrs. Saunders, Mrs. Ripley, Miss Phyllis Bateman, Miss Lynn Moller, Miss Nancy Snodgrass, Miss May Cameron, the three Goodyear sisters (Frances, Annie, and Eva), Miss Samuels, Miss Nora Wallis, and Miss Ivy Litten. Ladies Championships were at first played in two grades irrespective of handicaps. The first sixteen qualifiers were designated as the seniors, the second sixteen as juniors.

TITBITS FROM THE LADIES CLUB MINUTES

1937: There was a discussion on the separation of the Men and Ladies’ sections of the Club. It was unanimously decided that it would be unsuitable to change at this time.

1940: The first sub-committee was formed. It was to be responsible for the cleanliness and tidiness of the clubhouse. (Women clearly knew their place in the scheme of things). The Lady members were active in supporting the War effort by raising funds for the Patriotic Shop.

1944: The competition fee was set at 5/-.

1946: The services of a woman were secured to attend to Afternoon Teas on Wednesdays and Saturdays. The Businesswomen requested permission to play their competitions on Saturday afternoons. (It is not recorded what the reaction of the men was to this request!).
