Veteran men’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 130 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

Open original PDF at page 130 →

In 1992 under President John Newman the Society: • Built a set of steps and laid a path from the 3rd green to 4th tee. • Laid a path from the 11th tee to fairway. • Laid a path from the17th green to 18th tee. • Continued planting and development of “Vets’ Dell” below 3rd tee - now somewhat neglected. • Installed a plaque at the 11th tee entrance gate. In 1993 under President Bernie Baker the Society: • Installed a garden and steps at the 5th tee. • Supplied and installed a second flagpole on Club House. • Assisted in remodelling several bunkers in association with the Greens Staff. • Replaced stolen or vandalised tee markers. • Provided and installed fairway distance markers.

In 1994 under President Paul Munn the Society: • Painted all the seats around the course. • Refurbished the starting sheet display cabinet. • Installed gauge to indicate water level in reservoir.

In 1995 under President Bernie Child the Society: • Repainted the car park lines. • Financed and constructed new practice nets beside the reservoir. • Assisted in the installation of new course irrigation system’ • Trimmed the skirts of most trees that come into play. • Cut up firewood for sale. • Installed the David Coates memorial seat at the 10th tee.

The reference to firewood and skirt trimming above suggests that the Noel Copestake led group of Wednesday volunteers was initiated around that time. There has been a hard core of regulars on that team since, the core changing only as the years take their toll and new volunteers step forward. It would be interesting to know just how much the Club has gained from the activities of this group, both in terms of actual cash provided to specific projects, and in the amount saved in the costs of removing dead trees, trimming trees, tidying up fallen branches, and supplying firewood to the Club House. It would be difficult indeed to estimate the value gained from ‘Dads’ Army’, as the group is known, since

its inception. While the group as an entity deserve the accolades of us all some inevitably stand out. Without Noel’s enthusiastic example it is very doubtful the group would have survived more than a year or two, or perhaps even got started. Deserving of special mention would be Jim Swainson. Jim’s expertise with a chain saw has made a huge impact on the overall health, appearance, and ‘playability’ of the trees on the course. President in that year of 1995, Bernie Child, continues to lead projects as late as 2008 even though he retired as project manager in 1999. Bernie’s ability to design, develop, and maintain equipment has been a revelation to all who have worked with him.

The Club recognised his immense contribution over many years by conferring Life Membership upon him.
