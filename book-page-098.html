Mrs. Hanna was a pioneer in catering for such events as Inter-club fixtures when water had to be heated in an outside bath and small spirit burners were utilised for boiling kettles. Mrs. Hanna was elected to Life membership at some time prior to 1948 and was the first woman to be so honoured by the club.

MISS FRANCES GOODYEAR became a member with her two sisters, Annie and Eva in 1927. Membership was small in those years and all members became actively involved and worked hard for the good of the Club. The sisters were no exception. Frances was a schoolteacher and it was not unusual to see her dismiss her class a few minutes early on certain golfing afternoons. Grateful pupils were happy to polish her clubs, clean balls, and put water in the radiator of her Ford motorcar. Frances recalled interclub matches being arranged with players coming from Rotorua, Whakatane, Thames, Te Puke, Waihi, Matamata, Cambridge, and Paeroa.

MISS EVA GOODYEAR was interviewed on tape in 1993 when she was well into her eighties and provided us with some of the earliest memories of golf in Tauranga in those pioneering years. She

recalled the old Links at the Domain with the blue Irises and the Flag Lilies though she was obviously not then a golfer. Her father Canon William Goodyear had come to Tauranga in 1896 having been appointed by the Church Mission Society to serve as Supervising Maori Missioner for the Bay of Plenty. He had arrived in New Zealand in 1878 and was canonised in 1913 to recognise his sterling work for the Mission.

PHYLL, DAWN and HELEN HARTLEY were another group of three sisters who were early members of the Club. They joined in 1929 and Dawn would sometimes accompany Mr Mirrielees when he was planting trees. She recalled his criteria for spacing the trees was to ‘hit a ball, plant a tree’. Dawn remembered the wires around the greens to keep the sheep out. She was forever ruining her stockings clambering over them. The accepted mode of dress for a young lady on the course was grey pullover, grey culottes, and grey stockings. Rather a contrast to the young women glamorising the courses of today. Dawn remembered Kit Hardy and Alison Wilcox from those early days.

PHYLL BATEMAN joined the Club in 1931. Both she and her brother Jack were devoted to the sport and were dedicated Club members. Phyll served on committee from 1947-49. Her recollections of the early days included committee meetings held at night in hired rooms and having to hit over the road into the racecourse when playing McKinleys. Personalities Phyll recalled included top visiting players Bessie Gaisford (twice New Zealand Ladies’ Amateur Champion), Ruth Hodgeman, and Elsie Heald. Rennie Daines, Hannah Bartlett, Mrs. Page, Mrs Sanders (wife of a Colonel, but not the Colonel), Claire Mayfield, Audrey Gamman, and promising junior Kathy Moloney were some fellow Club members of whom Phyll had pleasant memories.
