Match Play events have always been popular with Club players. The Occupational Four Ball was an early introduction to the programme and was open to all members, the only restriction as to who could team together being that implied by the title. This event drew very large fields often requiring elimination matches to be played in order to allow pairs to advance to the main draw of 64. Eventually the competition was discontinued and replaced by the Senior/Junior Four Ball and the associated trophy transferred to the new event.

The Winter Four Balls are contested in three grades. Norman Robson and Paul O’Brien, well-known Club members who ran Businesses on The Strand, presented the cups awarded to the senior winners. Bob Eaton and Keith Frith presented the cups contested by the Intermediate pairs while the committee provided those for the juniors.

The most important Match Play events are those for the Club Championships. Several of the trophies associated with these are of very long standing. Bill Darley who was Secretary to the Club when it first moved to the present course in 1914 presented the W M Darley Cup. It is awarded to the player recording the best gross score over the two qualifying rounds. The Passmore Trophy goes to the best nett qualifier. Like the Waitemata Cup this is a trophy presented to the Club by a grateful supplier. CS Passmore was a liquor wholesaler based in Auckland. In the first few years after this trophy was presented the winner also received two bottles of the best of the Passmore range.

The first player to find this tradition had been discontinued was a very disappointed man.

Mr E T Baker, one of our earliest Life Members presented the Senior Championship Cup.

Stroke Play Championships were established in 1980. These were originally played over 72 holes as stand alone competitions not associated with the Match Play Championships. More recently they have been reduced to 54 holes and qualification for the Match Play Championships is based on rounds played in these competitions. Some consider that the staging of the two championships in tandem has reduced the importance of the Stroke Play Championship. The practise of allowing the best two of the three rounds to count for qualifying purposes also causes problems for purists and/or traditionalists.

Whether these innovations continue into the future remains to be seen.

David N Coleman, a prominent Tauranga Share Broker, presented the cups awarded to both the Senior and the Intermediate Stroke Play Champions while the Juniors play for the ANZ Bank Cup. The Alan Ward Memorial Trophy is awarded to the player scoring the best Nett over the first two rounds of the Stroke Play Championships. Alan was a member of the Ward family featured in Chapter Nineteen. Details of Alan’s service to the Club are given there. The James Mowat Memorial Trophy has gone through a number of transformations.
