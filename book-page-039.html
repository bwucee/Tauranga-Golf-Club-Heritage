Out of the oval

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

The increasing regularity of Race Days, Show Days, Orange Festival Parades and similar activities led to players becoming increasingly frustrated as streams of traffic flowed in front of them as they awaited a break so they could tee off. Consequently a new tee was constructed across the road, trees planted behind it to lessen the distraction of passing traffic, and the green extended toward the tenth fairway. One result is the hole no longer has the ‘dipping’ profile that gave it its name but the hole has lost little of its challenge.

NUMBER 10 - K D PAGE - PAR 4

Keith Page no doubt earned the right to have his name perpetuated as a result of the selfless efforts he made to produce the green at this hole. While a green did exist previously in this area it was not suitable for the newly designed tenth hole and was abandoned when play moved from the oval.

NUMBER 11 - THE AVENUE - PAR 4

This was the original opening hole. The stables have now been replaced further from the action, the three bunkers near the beginning of the practise area are no more, and bunkers have been created and removed beside the green. None-the-less the hole remains essentially the same today as when the course was established in 1914.

NUMBER 12 - THE KNOLL - PAR 3

This was originally the second hole and is as memorable now as it was when first played in 1914 in spite of the reduction in height and consequent increase in green size.

NUMBER 13 – THE CRESCENT - PAR 5

This was originally the third hole and named Pyes Pa. It was considered the most testing hole on the course and well deserved its number one stroke rating. When the new 15th and 16th holes were developed to replace those in the oval it was considered more appropriate to name one of these Pyes Pa and the thirteenth hole was renamed to fit its physical shape.

NUMBER 14 – SOUTH END - PAR 3

This was originally played as the sixteenth hole. It remains essentially the same as it then was although the massive pines behind the green that once provided welcome shelter from the strong Southerly winds that exert such an influence on the playing length of this particular hole are no longer there. There is an old adage that he who wins the fourteenth hole wins the match. This does seem to be the

case quite frequently.

NUMBER 15 - KAHAROA - PAR 4

Of all the holes on the course this one appears to play the longest for its carded length. Some translate the name Kaharoa as ‘long strength’, others as ‘long net’. The former is especially appropriate. The hole requires strength and stamina in order for it to be negotiated successfully. The ‘long net’ might also refer to the ability of this hole to trap the unwary. Michael Nicholson used to say that the final four holes on the course were those that separated the men from the boys.
