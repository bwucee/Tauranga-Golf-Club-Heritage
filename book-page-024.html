Administrators of both the Golf and Racing Clubs immediately recognised the danger that the new legislation represented. Having local Councillors replace the Minister of Crown Lands as their Landlord caused trepidation in the minds of those who could see the possibility of wider civic interests coming into play at some future date. Credit for the harmonious administrative niche that now exists is owed most particularly to Life Member E A (Ernie) Craig. Initially a subcommittee of the District Council was set up to assist Council to administer the Reserve. When this was found to be ineffective Mr. Craig lobbied for the establishment of what became known as the Racecourse Reserve Operation and Management Committee.

The new committee’s terms of reference were to deal with the operation of the Domain, and to liaise with the tenants. It was empowered to ascertain the level of rentals necessary to maintain the domain and to apply these to that maintenance. Hence all rentals charged were ultimately expended in the interests of the tenants in accordance with the terms of the 1880 Act.

The new committee came into being on the first day of January 1994 and included: • Four representatives of the City Council • one representative of the Golf Club • one representative of the Racing Club • one representative of the Equestrian group • one representative of the Greerton Soccer Club. (Now discontinued).

The four members appointed by the Council included one Councillor, the other three being members of the Public. Mr. Craig, having been elected to the Domain Board during the period when the Golf Club lobbied to increase their influence, continued to serve that Body, and its successor, for more than 50 years during which time he served equally both Golf and Racing interests. In recognition of this he was elected to Life Membership of both Clubs, a unique and fitting reward for a man who gave so much to both Clubs over such a long period. Prior to his retirement from the Reserve Committee in 2004 Ernie served as its Chairman and was responsible for much of the drive that ensured the Reserve was continually developed and improved for the benefit of the Clubs and for the Community.

Ernie, in the latter years of his service, was one of the four Council appointees and upon retirement was accorded a Civic farewell to allow the Council and public to formally recognise his outstanding contribution.

For the record the current lease from the Reserve Committee to the Golf Club was renewed in August 2006 and will expire in 2039. The work of Ernie Craig and those who served with him will surely have ensured that the Racecourse Reserve will continue to accommodate Racing, Golf, Equestrian Sport, and various public activities for the foreseeable future. It is a priceless asset to the City of Tauranga. In spite of those early uncertainties of tenure on the Reserve land the original Golf Club members gave their labour enthusiastically at regular working bees where they slashed gorse, erected fences to protect greens, built stiles, planted trees, and cut and rolled greens from the existing pasture.
