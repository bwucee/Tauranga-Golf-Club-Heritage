Professional golf returns to Tauranga

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

Caroline Bon of Northland made no race of the Women’s section, firing three sub-par rounds to finish 15 strokes clear of what has to be said was a rather mediocre field. This in no way detracted from the merit of Caroline’s performance, sub-par rounds being hardly commonplace in New Zealand Women’s Golf.

THE 2011 CARRUS TAURANGA OPEN CHAMPIONSHIP

Unheralded Waikato Professional Jim Cusdin won the 2011 Carrus Open in fine style in spite of his claim at the final day presentation that he had not been confident at the start as he had not played the course well on previous visits. Tournament favourite and clear first day leader Michael Hendry expressed very different sentiments in relation to the course. Said Hendry: “I love this course. It really suits my game. It is a course where you feel you can attack every hole but the key is not to push too hard. I did that on the 8th hole on day one and paid the price. The key is patience.” Hendry set the pace in round one, posting a five under par 65 in very windy conditions, one stroke ahead of local star Jared Pender and Te Anau Professional David Klein.

Co-favourite Gareth Paddison followed on 67. Hendry’s round included six birdies and a sole bogey on that 8th hole.

Gareth Paddison, showing the value of the experience he has gained from appearances on the PGA Tours of both the USA and of Europe over the previous season or two, scored an impressive 64 in favourable conditions to lead by three strokes over Dominic Barson (also 64) and Pieter Zwart after the second round. Michael Hendry dropped back with an even par 70, while Pender (67), and amateur Ryan Fox (67) remained well in contention. Starting at the 11th Fox four putted the Knoll, an experience not unknown to local members though they seldom play the green when it is running as fast as it was that day. Despite that set back Fox later birdied each hole from the second to the eighth with the exception of the par three Fifth.

Six birdies in seven holes demonstrated the true quality of golf of which

this talented New Zealand representative is capable.

The women competed over 54 holes so their competition finished on the Saturday afternoon with NZ representative Emily Perry of Waikato firing a final round of 72 to finish five under par and four strokes clear of Charlotte Thomas.
