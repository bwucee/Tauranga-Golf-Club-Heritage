The first tee

Following the 1905 report of an Annual General Meeting in the BOP Times the Club does not appear to have again been mentioned in that journal until June 10th 1908 when a report was carried of the AGM. This was held in the Orderly Room of Tauranga Mounted Rifles, with Mr Bull being elected Captain and Mr Ward Honorary Secretary/Treasurer. Reporting on the finances of the Club Mr Bull stated that the “annual expenses of maintenance had averaged about five pounds”. Some dozen new members were elected including four ladies. It was resolved to hold a 16-hole singles tournament under medal play format, an entrance fee of one shilling being made to cover prizes.

Players were to be handicapped and a handicapping committee of Messrs Bull, McKenzie, and Shackleford was appointed. Two rounds of the then eight hole course would need to be completed during the one afternoon and proper cards were to be kept with the card showing ‘each stroke played for each hole, and the total for the same’. Perhaps one hole had been lost to the sea at the time so reducing the course to 8 holes.

The BOP Times issue of 17th May 1909 reported the election of Mr. J Bull as Captain, Mr. G A Ward as Secretary-Treasurer, and Messrs. H A Sharp, A Matheson, and H Phillips, as committee members. Mr. J A McKenzie presided over the meeting at which it was reported the Balance Sheet was in credit to the tune of 2/6d. Subscriptions were set at 10s for gentlemen, 5s for ladies, and 2/6d for juniors under 18. As Mr. Matheson, soon to be recognised as the first club champion, was welcomed as a new member to this meeting it is apparent that there was no great competition for positions on the committee.

The Secretary reported that an order for £2.10.6d worth of balls had been placed but they had not yet been received.

Bill Darley, Secretary to the Club in 1951-53, used to enjoy relating a story from play on the old Domain course. A certain gentleman had the bad habit of playing up on the group ahead. There was a short hole played from the northern end of the Domain, across a small harbour inlet, to Sulphur Point. While Bill was putting out a ball landed on the edge of the green. The offender was at it again! Bill took out his Brassie and smote the ball a couple of hundred yards into the estuary. Bill was on the next green when the said gentleman arrived and asked if anyone had seen his ball.

Like the man he was Bill promptly owned up. “Yes, you played onto us and I hope your ball is now well on its way to the open sea somewhere near the Mount.”
