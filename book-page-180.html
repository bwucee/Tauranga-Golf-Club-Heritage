It is almost certainly true that the membership in general did not give Grant the credit his abilities deserved. When he began his time at Tauranga he inherited a course that, despite the best intentions of those involved, had not experienced the level of professional care that had prevailed during the Silvester/Hill period. Consequently the greens were suffering from gross mineral deficiency, the turf was weak and readily susceptible to disease, and the general state of the course was well below that which should have prevailed at the time. No Course Superintendent could have been expected to rectify those conditions over night.

Grant Patterson was no exception though the evidence at the time suggested he took the correct steps to get course management back onto a sound foundation. By the time he resigned to move back to Australia in 1985 it was clear that the course was well on the road to recovery.

The next significant appointment was that of Murray Clark in 1995. Murray had been in charge of the Poverty Bay Golf Club’s Awapuni course in Gisborne. Not long before he moved north he had been responsible for preparing that course for the Freyberg Rosebowl as the Inter-provincial Men’s Team Championship was then known. During that tournament the Awapuni greens were given unusually high praise for their exceptionally good condition. I had the pleasure of playing them the week before the tournament and can truthfully say I have never experienced better greens. They were fast and very true. Some of those who were players or spectators during the tournament afterwards vouched for the extremely high standard of putting those greens promoted.

Clearly Murray knew what was required to produce greens of that quality. Awapuni is near the coast and so is natural sand country that is ideal for the promotion of the fine turf grasses best suited to golf greens. Tauranga posed a different challenge, one that Murray proved, over the next few years, well able to meet. In his favour an excellent team of assistants aided him. The needs of the Club had by this time built up to the stage where it was considered that four full time assistants were required in order to groom the course to the higher standard now considered appropriate. Naturally there were members who believed this to be an unnecessarily high number but if the Club wished to satisfy the more exacting demands of the modern player then there was little choice but to supply the manpower to do the job.

In contrast to earlier, more relaxed times, when greens were mowed twice weekly, fairways mowed boundary to boundary, and bunkers given an occasional tidy up, greens were now required to be mown daily, fairways contoured and more frequently mown, and bunkers and surrounds manicured regularly. All of this is labour intensive and the staff must attempt to fit everything in and around people playing the course. Collectively somewhere in the vicinity of 1000 rounds are played each week of the year.

By 2002 Murray Clark began to feel he would like to relinquish some of the responsibility he then held and an agreement was reached whereby he would step down to an assistant role if the Club were able to attract a suitably qualified Course Supervisor. An outstanding young candidate in Brendan Allen applied for the position and was duly appointed. An old student of Tauranga Boys’ College, Brendan had almost completed a Business Studies degree at Waikato University when he decided he preferred the idea of an outdoor life and joined the staff of the Hamilton Golf Club at their St. Andrews course. On completing an apprenticeship there he moved to become second in charge at the Russley Golf Club in Christchurch where he experienced quite different climatic and soil conditions thus broadening his experience and knowledge.
