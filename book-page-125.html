JIMMY MOWAT

Born in Scotland Jimmy was the first Professional appointed to the Club in 1936. He was a foundation member of the Veterans and a regular Tuesday/Thursday participant from the earliest beginnings. Long after his reinstatement to the amateur ranks members, both male and female, would seek his advice on the finer points of the game. His interest in course development served the developing club well over a long period of time. When Jim and his wife Margaret sold their property, which is now the Tauranga Girls’ College, the turf on their tennis court was rolled up and delivered to the eleventh green where Grant Silvester laid it. It provided an excellent surface from the beginning and continues to do so today.

Margaret was also an enthusiastic golfer and active participant in club affairs. She made a great job of catering for our first Veterans’ Tournament in 1953.

FRANK MACKLOW

Frank has only recently had to give up golf. A perfectionist the slightest deviation from a straight shot down the fairway would see him on the practise fairway ironing out the problem prior to heading home. On the committee in his first year of joining the Vets he later served five years as Treasurer. Frank twice won the New Zealand Veterans’ Championship, first at Tauranga in 1964 and again in 1965 at the Hutt Club where he defeated Harold Black. In 1969 Frank was runner-up in the Championship.

SYD RANDELL

Syd, along with others, claimed responsibility for the lone pine tree between the 13th and 17th fairways. Originally there were three but two were removed after careful analysis revealed the one reprieved caught the most balls. Syd served the Tauranga Golf Club as President from 1954-56.

OTHER PERSONALITIES

There was the dignified ex school master Noel Gibson whose greatest profanity was “oh drat it”. A noisy opponent, caddie, or fellow competitor would immediately be quelled by a single look cast down from his full height of six feet. He was one of four to be given a presentation upon achieving the age of eighty, a tradition that still survives in the society. The other three were Harry Bradmore, Percy Maude, and Johnny Walker. Percy will be remembered for his home made trundler, and for his tees made from sections of milking inflations cut to different lengths and strung together with an old boot lace. Following Percy could be something of an ordeal as he was prone to shed tees, pieces of trundler, and articles of clothing as he went along his way.

Harry Smith contributed much towards the new Clubhouse built in 1954. When it was undergoing its first alteration Harry claimed: “they can knock down those walls but they will need a bulldozer for that corner. I laid those bricks myself”. They did in fact survive that first alteration but not the next.

Where do we stop? There are so many that could be mentioned. Men like Les Woods, Frank McFadyen, and Bob Innis, all served on our committee and are still with us in 1977. Later we will attempt to cover events and people from 1970 onwards. In the meantime all veterans salute and thank the ‘Old Stalwarts’ who pushed the boat out on its first days of a successful and rewarding voyage.
