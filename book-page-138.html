The women veterans’ golf club

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 138 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

In 2008 the group instigated an Interclub fixture where they invited veterans from Matamata and Te Puke to visit. A special general meeting the same year voted unanimously to reduce the qualifying age from 55 to 50 though a 55+ tournament was still held with Golf Fusion providing $800 worth of sponsorship, an exceptionally generous gesture indeed.

After many years of a roll-up starting system the veterans instigated a draw for their Monday competition, the change being accepted with enthusiasm by the vast majority of participants. It is nice to know where and when you are starting well in advance of teeing off.

Kathy Gregg, in announcing that she would be retiring from the presidency at the end of the 2010 season, donated a trophy to be known as the Women Veterans’ Presidents Trophy. The competition format for this would be the prerogative of the serving President each year.

Club funds had again reached a healthy profit and the sum of $500 was donated to the Golf Club with the suggestion that a rail be installed to assist descent from the sixth tee. This had not appeared by 2011.

The 2010 tournament was open to players 50 years and over and again attracted a strong field of some 90 players. Sponsorship of $1000 from Althorpe Village ensured the success of the tournament. Greerton Lotto meantime had established an on-going sponsorship involving $30 vouchers and three golf balls per month while Artisan Jewellers sponsored the Pairs tournament.

In 2010 Connie Breeds retired from organising the summer golf programme, a job she had done with enthusiasm and efficiency for a number of years. Beryl Smith and Adrienne Coad volunteered to continue the work so ensuring this successful initiative would continue. Over the years Women’s golf has not suffered from any lack of well qualified, enthusiastic volunteers to take on the many roles that need to be filled in providing for the administration of both the playing and the social side of golf. Trophies played for during the season include the Jean Mack trophy over three rounds of Stableford play for women of 65 years and over, the Birdie and Founder’s Trophies, each presented by Phyl Carter, the Joan Standford Memorial tray awarded to the player scoring most Stableford points on the day of the Club Birthday in June, and the Val Western Putting Trophy.

The Grandmothers’ Trophy for the best Stableford was donated by Tom and Edna Dick who also donated the Fillies Cup awarded to the woman recording the best Stableford score on Melbourne Cup day when the Veteran men invite the Ladies to join them for golf and sociability.
