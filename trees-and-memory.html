1930s–2014

A landscape planted by people

Trees gave shelter and shape to the course, and many also carried personal memories.

Dave traces a major planting programme to A. J. Mirrielees and the relief-worker scheme during the Depression. Later members continued the work, adding both useful shelter and individual specimen trees.

The chapter records the history of particular trees, their loss in storms, and the debate that often accompanied removal or replacement. It presents planting as part of course design as well as beautification.

It also considers how species selection affects turf, maintenance and wildlife. These observations are Dave’s account and opinions, preserved in the original chapter with its date and context.

The stories of planting and care belong alongside the playing records: the landscape experienced by each generation was partly the result of work by the people before it.
