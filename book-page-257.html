While Casper was not known as an especially long hitter his driving down the seventh fairway certainly impressed those present with both its length and its accuracy.

He had a caddie stand at the top of the hill while he drove balls almost to his feet. He would then wave the caddie back twenty yards or so and repeat the process. This he did once or twice more with the caddie barely moving to retrieve the balls. To have such control over the longest club in the bag was remarkable and his control of the iron clubs was equally impressive. A truly wonderful clinic staged by a great personality. For a description of the exhibition match that followed we are indebted to a first hand account from the man closest to the action, Jim Lancaster.

THE DAY I PARTNERED BILLY CASPER

I had only been playing golf for four years at the time when I was fortunate to win the Club Championship and thus gain the right to partner Billy Casper in the exhibition match. To be honest I had not read a lot about the top Professional players so was not as over-awed at the prospect of partnering a player of such fame as I may otherwise have been. I have since learned that Billy was the leading money winner on the PGA Tour in two of the previous three years, a five-times winner of the Vardon Trophy for the lowest scoring average on Tour, and winner of 68 Professional Tournaments, including 51 PGA Titles.

This places him at 7th on the all-time list of tour winners. He also won three majors.

At the time of the exhibition Billy was staying on a sheep station at Taupo during a holiday in New Zealand and consequently was very relaxed. Perhaps that was his nature anyway. I recall the exhibition clearly. The shots he demonstrated were amazing to me and I would think to all those present. Prior to hitting each shot Billy would explain what he intended to do. The caddie hardly moved as sliced shots, drawn shots, and even straight ones, landed at his feet even when he was up to 280 yards away. This was achieved using clubs with steel shafts and wooden heads. Also the old wound ball then in use flew considerably less in distance than does the modern one-piece ball.

Throughout the exhibition, and during the subsequent round, Billy was friendly, jovial, and talkative. I was surprised that he invited members of the gallery to take photographs and ask questions about any aspect of the game they wished.

I had not considered myself to be a nervous player but since I started bogey, triple bogey, double bogey, I must have been nervous without being fully aware of it. Poor Billy must have wondered what he had done to deserve such a partner. With his encouragement I settled down and played three under over the remaining holes to complete a respectable 74. It was a giant relief to redeem myself in the eyes of my partner and those of the large gallery that accompanied us throughout the day. We were matched against local Professional Jim Paterson and his colleague Noel Hayden. With Billy breezing around the course in a bogey-free 67, we won comfortably.
