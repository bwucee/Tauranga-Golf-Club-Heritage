Professional tuition Hazel organised raffles and the resulting lessons with Rick proved very valuable to the women. Not satisfied with the great job she was doing with the beginning ladies Hazel became involved teaching school children to play. In this she was helped by Joyce Paris and Fay O’Neill. When Nine Hole golf got underway Hazel was asked to look after the Saturday morning group. She loved doing this and continued as long as she was able to do so. For her dedicated service to ‘her girls’ over many years Hazel Casey was elected to Life Membership of the club in 2000.

NINE HOLE GOLF

The 1977 move by the Club to institute a nine hole handicap for women in order to cater for the needs of an older group of members who were finding it difficult to complete full 18 hole rounds on our demanding course proved to be the successful forerunner of a movement that was to have a major impact in later years. Perhaps inspired by the success of One-Day Cricket, administrators of New Zealand Golf began a campaign to institute Nine Hole Golf on a National scale. At the time NZGolf recognised that the wave of interest in the game that had flowed through New Zealand during the Sixties and Seventies had now ebbed and they were searching for ways to encourage more people to take up the game and join clubs.

Nine hole golf was seen as a game that would appeal to younger working people, and to mothers with young families, as it would not commit them to as much time away from the responsibilities of modern life as did the Eighteen hole game.

Tauranga Golf Club officials were quick to see the potential of Nine-hole Golf and the Women’s Committee immediately began to actively pursue the concept. Phyll Sandlant was an early volunteer to step forward and offer to lead the promotion. Her report on the evolution of this new form of the game follows. Phyll Sandlant reports: “In February 1998 Tauranga advertised for women who would like to ‘have a go’ at Nine Hole Golf. We intended to start a group at Tauranga, the first in the Bay of Plenty. Professional coaching was offered and it was hoped the idea would appeal to young mothers, working mothers, and perhaps

grandmothers. It is, after all, a game for life.

We were overwhelmed with the response when 96 women registered on the first call. With the invaluable assistance of our Professional, Rick Barker, coaching groups were organised. When each clinic finished the beginners would play a few holes accompanied by members of a wonderful group of volunteers who would walk with them to encourage and assist them in following Rick’s instructions. At the same time these volunteers would ensure the newcomers learnt aspects of etiquette and course management. Later we introduced the women to the mysteries of Match Play, Stroke Play, Stableford and Par competition.
