latest ideas and improvements that have been developed to assist in the playing of the game and in its administration. When computerised handicapping and card reading were first introduced the Club was amongst the first to trial the available systems. Fortunately we did not jump straight into one of the first available systems, preferring to wait, correctly anticipating that improvements would inevitably follow. As a result we were able to install Golf Autoscore when it first became available. In 2005 the Board approved this installation after presentations convinced them that the deficiencies noted in 2002 had now been remedied. The cost involved a purchase price of $8500, and a monthly service fee of $200.

It is arguably the best available system by a considerable margin and the administration and the membership have been well pleased with its performance.

It is possible that Tauranga was the first Club in New Zealand to have an opportunity to use computerised handicapping. During the Eighties a computer based handicapping program was written by some students and staff of Tauranga Boys’ College and demonstrated to committee members of the Club. The demonstration was successful in the sense that it accurately recalculated handicaps as new data were entered. It was even able to demonstrate that some of the records entered manually had not resulted in correct recalculation of handicaps. This was hardly surprising in view of the number of records that had to be kept, and the manual calculations that had to be made.

Arnold would indeed have to be congratulated on the overall accuracy of the handicap records he kept. Obviously the College could not offer a free service so suggested that they would do a weekly input of all men’s cards for a fee of $50. After some consideration the offer was declined and computerised handicapping had to wait for a few more years before being introduced.
