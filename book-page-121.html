Veteran men’s golf

In 1964 Tauranga were again hosts to the New Zealand Championship with Frank Macklow of our Club the winner on that occasion. The last time the Championship had been held here was in 1959 with E S Toogood of Poverty Bay the winner and Frank Macklow runner-up.

A perusal of some entries in the old Minute Books can be full of interest. Take for instance the financial summary following the initial tournament in 1953. It reads: Entry fees were £85.10.0 and a donation made total receipts £90. Trophies cost £60.0.4, Printing £7.15.6, Petty Cash and Secretary’s Expenses £7.1.9, Lime Juice £3.16.6, Tauranga Golf Club Green Fees £5, which left a balance of £6.5.11 profit. Also of interest is a list of the Secretary’s purchases of victuals for the 1961 tournament covering the Cocktail Party and lunches. Here they are. Cocktail Party: 6 Whisky, 3 Gin, 1 Brandy, 1 Sherry, 12 Beer (Dozens presumably), 15 dozen sandwiches, 15 dozen savouries, 6 bags potato chips and 2 lbs. of salted peanuts.

Lunches: 6 quarts milk, 4 cut loaves, 4 dozen cakes, 2 dozen scones, 3 dozen Cottage Pies, 6 lbs. Blade Steak, 6 Kidneys, 2lbs. tea, 8lbs. sugar, 2lbs. ham, 3lbs. tomatoes, 12 lbs. potatoes, 1 bottle tomato sauce, 1lb. cheese, 1 pkt. Gravyquick, 2lbs. onions, 2 large pkts. of frozen peas.

All this was just part of the Secretary’s job. No wonder it was a difficult post to fill. Those of our members

who have served on the Veterans’ Committee know that the job is no sinecure, particularly when it comes to tournament time. The planning, the need for detail, the frustrations, and all the general organisation required in order to present a successful tournament call for a great deal of expertise. How fortunate then that we have had the splendid assistance of Anne Mark and Rennie Gordon (Wallis) over these past 25 years. Together these two performed all the clerical work involved in the tournament until the early Sixties when Miss Mark took over on her own. In the minutes of the meeting following that first 1951 tournament it is recorded that “our thanks are due to Miss Anne Mark and Mrs Rennie Gordon

for the smooth and capable running of the tournament”. Every successive committee has echoed the same sentiment over the past 24 years. It is noteworthy that both ladies were made Life Members of the Tauranga Golf Club.

After work one afternoon I was getting my clubs from the Trundler shed ready to do some practise. It was during Veterans’ week. An elderly gentleman came in dragging his clubs slowly behind him. “My goodness” I said. “You do look as though you have had a hard day out there”. “Hard day” he said. “You have no idea. These old buggers are really tough. The one I played today was eight under his handicap. I had to go to the 22nd hole to beat him”. He did not seem to register where this explanation might have positioned him in relation to his own performance.
