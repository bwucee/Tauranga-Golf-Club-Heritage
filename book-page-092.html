Women’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 92 / 305Next page →

1957: Mary Shanks started golf this season and reduced her handicap to 26, winning the Junior Championship, the Junior Jubilee, and the trophy for the most improved player. It was suggested that Senior players should assist and guide this promising young player and see that she have the opportunity to play with low handicap players. (Traditionally low handicap players did not get drawn with those on longer handicaps). The Club cardigan colour was to be Navy Blue.

1958: There was another request to change the colour of the cardigan as Rotorua, Matamata and Te Puke already have Navy Blue as their club colour. 1960: Advice was received from LGU Executive member Anne Mark that from June 1st. it would no longer be necessary for any player to sign an extra-day Score.

1961: It was reported that Mrs. Rennie Gordon was assisting in compiling a History of the Club that would be published and issued as a souvenir booklet at a price of 30/-. (This presumably refers to the proposed publication first worked upon by Joan Mirrielees that was not published owing to lack of funding. Some of the material collected then has been incorporated into this publication).

1962: A proposal to allow the wearing of shorts on the course did not find favour. 1965: The resolution to start C-Graders on Tuesdays was rescinded. (C-Graders were players who had not yet earned a handicap of 36). It was determined to trial new start times for Wednesday play. Approximately six groups of handicapped players would commence play from tee 7 from 9am. C-Graders were to follow them. All others were to start from number one tee at 9.30am.

1966: The membership limits were eased to allow for 300 A-Category and 50 B-Category Women.

B-Category was to be accorded priority as vacancies arose. The Ladies’ AGM voted to have separate Club days for Junior and C-graders on Tuesday, and for Senior and Intermediate players on Wednesday. (This was probably greeted with relief by members of both groups, especially the C-Graders who were always under pressure from the more Senior players behind them, and understandably did not find the Seventh the most welcoming of starting holes. They would not be the only members to hold that sentiment.)

1969: The Club committee requested that in future the Ladies use the profits from their tournaments to meet their own expenses. (Unbiased opinion would readily concede that over the years the women have husbanded their financial resources better than have the men.)

1970: Bermuda Shorts were to be permitted on the course but not in the Clubhouse.

1971: The dress regulations were again extended to allow the wearing of Slacks on the course, but only Slack Suits would be acceptable in the Lounge. (Presumably the sky had not fallen the previous year when the first few brave souls wore shorts).
