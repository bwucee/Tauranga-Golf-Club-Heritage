Trees

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 191 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

Beside the entrance path to the clubhouse stands a European Olive from Galatos in Greece. This was planted by members of the RSA Golf Group in memory of New Zealand men who died in the Cretan Campaign. Beside it stands a Magnolia (Michaela dolposa) planted in 1983 in memory of Una Wickham, our greatest woman golfer. Three flowering cherries were planted adjacent to the tenth tee to enhance the setting for a dedicated seat placed there in memory of David Coates by his family. The family of Norm Roberts planted a Kauri behind the fourth tee in his memory. As we walk from the fifth green behind the seventh green we pass a Kauri tree planted in memory of Jack Pattison, son of member Brian Pattison.

This Kauri has since been joined by a matching Kauri donated by Tony Reynish and Jackie MacDonald. These two trees will, in time, form an imposing backdrop to the fifth green when viewed from the tee. Long serving professional Jim Paterson is commemorated by a Rimu planted near the fourteenth green by his family while close by can be seen the Pohutukawa commemorating Phill Johnston’s 60 years of membership. A fine Gingko tree was planted in memory of Henry Becker to the right of the seventeenth tee. Henry was a keen golfer in retirement, served as assistant secretary to Royce Lahood, and at one time was secretary to the BOP Golf Association.

The Dorothy Garland Competition was contested by teams of committee members from the Zones of the BOPTV Golf Association. Each year Dorothy donated a Camellia to be planted in honour of the winning team. Tauranga successes can be seen in the Camellias planted beside the first tee. The

Ladies Club also planted a Camellia in the garden beside the trundler shed in memory of Peg Cross (Later Bates).

The tradition of planting a tree in commemoration of past members whose association with the club gave them special pleasure is to be encouraged though it is important that tree selection and siting need to be authorised by the Club Administration to ensure that the long term policies of the Board are not compromised. A welcome alternative to the planting of a tree would be the donation of a bench seat

beside an appropriate tee. The seat beside the 12th tee, donated in memory of Joe Loughlin by his family, provides a welcome resting spot where players can pause while they contemplate how to handle the difficulties that await them on that challenging hole. The daughters of Barbara Carlisle went a step further when they erected an elaborate shelter below the fifth tee in memory of Barbara. Golfers can sit in comfort and safety while waiting for the players ahead to complete play from the tee. This was an appropriate and most welcome addition to the facilities around the course. In earlier times Harry Butts, who played on the old Domain course and later at Gate Pa, showed his appreciation of the pleasure he had derived from membership by donating seats for every tee on the course.

These were custom built seats with a fitted rubbish bin at one end and a water container at the other. On Fridays a member of the green staff would empty the rubbish and replenish the water container with clean water. Patent ball washers were unknown at the time and the water and associated cloth were well-used accessories. Mr Butts was a very well known identity in the Town, it being his custom to walk down to the Strand each morning, summer and winter to swim in the harbour. He kept this up until well into his eighties. It must have kept him in fine fettle for it was claimed he collected his BNZ superannuation longer than any other ex-employee of that venerable institution.

In the World at large trees serve a wide variety of vitally important roles. This is no less true on Golf
