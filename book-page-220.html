Personal experiences

The Mount. Jack probably set a record for continuous appearances in the Labour Weekend tournament – he competed over forty times I believe. In a world where only a favoured few had a post-war car Harry Smith stood out. He was the proud owner of a Ford “Fortyniner”, one of only two in the town. Harry’s business partner Tui Harvey was a prodigious, if erratic, hitter. One day someone bet Tui he could not drive a ball from the 13th tee into the newly installed bunker between the 13th and 17th fairways. At the time I had not seen it done, nor heard of anyone having achieved the feat but Tui lined the ball up and proceeded to carry it into that bunker on the fly.

Ten years later I returned to the area and joined the Mount Maunganui Golf Club. It gave me great pleasure to meet again with George Harrison, Jack Lloyd, and others from those earlier days, and to play with them regularly. Later Paul O’Brien shifted across to play at the Mount and he and I still form part of a regular Tuesday four.

Bert Hart was the greenkeeper and another friend. One weekday afternoon, while Dad and I were playing a few holes, Bert drove up on the old Massey Ferguson tractor and stopped for a word with Dad. Apparently there had been a complaint made that I was putting with a five iron. I am sure I never as much as scratched the surface of a green while putting with my old jigger but Dad suggested I had better use a putter from then on so a hickory shafted putter was added to double my club numbers. When I was about 14 I was allowed to use my mother’s clubs that had lain idle for a decade or more.

These included a spoon, a three iron and a mashie with very thin blades. There was also a Man’s eight iron with about as much bounce and loft as a modern sand wedge. There was even an ancient leather carry bag. I was in golfing heaven and my scores began to improve. At age 12 in what proved to be my last round at Tauranga during that period I went around the course in 81 strokes with the help of just those six clubs.

During my years at Tauranga I was largely unaware of there being any other junior players. I was the only son regularly caddying and there was no organised programme for juniors. When I got to college I found there were a few boys who played regularly, notably Michael Cambie, Robert Mowat, and Dick Wilson. These boys were a year or two ahead of me at school so our paths did not really cross. It was a few years later that I began to meet them at tournaments where they showed they had developed into fine golfers.

In the early Fifties the Club instituted an open Professional and Amateur 72 hole Stroke Play Tournament.

This was an innovative move as prior to that the only tournaments available to amateurs were three- day events featuring two qualifying rounds on a Saturday followed by four rounds of Match play. The Professionals had even fewer opportunities to compete against their peers. The tournament was held in autumn and every effort was made to attract a quality field, with leading players from Auckland and the Waikato being regular participants. I was about ten when these began and Dad allowed me to make my caddying services available to these golfers. This was great as far as I was concerned for they paid me 2/6 a round as opposed to my father’s occasional bottle of lemonade at the end of the day.
