I got the impression he knew just where each of his shots would land and how far it would run. With the small heads typical of woods of that time he was able to work the ball in any desired direction. He never looked in serious danger of dropping a shot and on the only couple of times I recall his missing a fairway he was able to play a great recovery shot.

I recall him enquiring where the third green was and, after receiving conflicting advice on his best approach to the hole he decided to take the trees on. His tee shot finished just 40 yards short of the front of the green. On the 7th he played a seven iron for his second contrary to the advice of his caddie who wanted him to take more club. As a consequence he finished short and failed to gain the birdie his drive deserved. He did birdie the 9th, his five-iron finishing about 10 ft. from the hole. Another birdie followed at the 10th to reward a drive of some 280 yards.

At the time I had the confidence of youth on the putting green and had the habit of walking forward ready to retrieve my ball from the hole following putts of ten feet or so. I did this again at the 11th only to have Billy tell me to “Let the ball get to the hole before you pick it up”. It was holed on this occasion or I would have looked a little silly.

Billy had a good sense of humour. I had a set of Kangaroo head covers and dropped the one from my driver beside the 17th tee. Billy immediately gave it a thump with his driver as he said, “what’s that critter. Quick, get it”.

Over the years I have been privileged to play in Tournaments such as the Spalding Masters and the PGA Championship where I have been paired with some outstanding Professionals from New Zealand and Overseas. None of these were of the calibre of Billy Casper who played with, and defeated, many greats of the game including Ben Hogan, Arnold Palmer, and Byron Nelson. In 2000 the United States Golf Digest ranked Billy as the 15th greatest golfer of all time. While, at the time of the exhibition, I was unaware of Billy’s true standing in the history of the game I have since come to realise how fortunate I was to have been one of a very few New Zealanders to have played with Billy Casper, a great golfer, a true gentleman, and a man who was patient enough to put a novice like myself at ease throughout the day.

At the end of the round the Club presented Billy with a book featuring the scenery of New Zealand. I received an inlaid wooden box suitably engraved as a memento of my experience. I treasured that box for 37 years until, sadly, it was stolen from my home in 2007.
