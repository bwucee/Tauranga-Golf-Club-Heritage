Administration

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 154 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

1966: A proposal to sell draught beer was lost. One month later a draught beer trial was approved. One committee member dissented on the grounds that it would not be good for ‘the decorum of the Club’. Another thought it might conflict with the law, apparently overlooking the fact that the entire operation of the bar was outside the law of the day. One month after draught beer sales were introduced consumption was reported to have reached an average of 100 flagons a week. A new course assessment gave an SSS (Standard Scratch Score) of 69.05. It was resolved that junior players not be allowed in the lounge except on tournament days or by permission of the Secretary Manager or Club Captain. 1967: Membership closed and limits set.

The power poles on the 8th fairway had now been removed. 1968: The appointment of a permanent third greenkeeper was approved. 1969: Flagon beer price was increased to 25¢ per jug. 1971: Match committee advised men’s 17th now designated a par 4 in line with the guidelines in the handicapping manual. An application was made to stage the 1973 Russell Grace Women’s Inter- provincials. 1972: Play over rule to be discontinued. A delegation from the RSA Golf committee requested that the decision to charge outside competitors in their tournament a $2 green fee be rescinded. This was unsuccessful. 1973: It was resolved to purchase a house at 1368 Cameron Road and sell the Club’s section off Hynd’s Road.

An offer of $5000 was accepted for the section. McKinlay’s Paddock made available for practise but very little used. A Pool table was purchased at a cost of $970. It was felt necessary to introduce crowd control for Finals Day on account of the large galleries being attracted. $40 000 was allocated for Clubhouse extensions. 1974: M Nicholson selected to represent NZ in Eisenhower team. BOP Association approached for a ruling re local clubs (notably Mt Maunganui) not observing handicap rules over summer. 1975: Twilight tournaments were introduced. A local rule was introduced banning attendance of the flagstick and requiring all four balls to be holed prior to retrieval. This was to protect the area surrounding the hole in winter.

(This was an illegal rule in conflict with the Rules of Golf.) 1976: A plan was introduced to clean up the area to the left of the 7th hole at a cost of $12 000 for the development of a Driving Range. This was subsequently rejected by the AGM of 1977. 1977: Reciprocal agreements were negotiated with the Cronulla Club of Sydney and with the Invercargill Golf Club. Three-tee starting was tried for the first time. The tradition of honouring Life Members at Finals Day was initiated. 1978: In view of reported cases of gambling associated with Pool games it was resolved to impose

tighter controls and to deal severely with offenders. Les Hill resigned and was replaced by Lyn Gillon. As Mr Gillon did not require the house it was rented at $40 per week. Bob Burstall and Willis Faulds were reported to have surveyed and named all of the trees on the course. ’Play the Lie’ instituted. Prior to this a ‘placing’ rule had applied all season only to be lifted the week prior to the Championship qualifying rounds! A long-term lease had finally been successfully negotiated with the Minister of Crown Lands. A water easement re the stream had been obtained and consequently a $48 000 irrigation scheme approved. 1979: The Pine trees adjacent to the car park were felled.
