MEMORIES OF TAURANGA GOLF CLUB FROM

BRIAN DUCKER My father Norm Ducker was a schoolteacher and a keen sportsman. In April 1949 when I was six years old we shifted from Thornton to Tauriko where Dad was to be their new headmaster. He had been a member of the Whakatane Golf Club where he had won the Championship in 1947. He joined up with Tauranga in that first year of 1949, playing off a handicap of four throughout most of the following six years.

There was no such thing as organised golf for children of Primary School age at that time and as a consequence I spent a large amount of time on Saturdays, and an occasional Sunday, caddying for my father. Once or twice a week I would play the back nine as the Tauriko School House was only a couple of miles from the course. There was a rule that sons could play with their father members for free on days other than competition days and I played for years with just a cut down jigger which was a hickory shafted club with the approximate loft of a three iron. The course lay out closely approximated that of the present day though changes were always envisaged.

The fifth hole was always a problem and changes were constantly made to try and improve the situation. When we first arrived the tee was at ground level to the left of the fourth green while the hole ran along the boundary fence to a small green that was barely visible and surrounded by Toitoi clumps that often trapped a wayward ball. Later a long narrow green was constructed more or less where the present green lies. The pin was still invisible even when a new raised tee was installed further to the left and well back in the corner. The remaining holes are still in the same place but one has to think hard to recall the many subtle changes these holes have undergone.

The greens were all smaller and there were very few bunkers in 1950. Now most holes have them. Virtually all the greens have been built up and enlarged while the back portions have been raised and extended. The 2nd, 4th, and 8th greens in particular were small

and steeply borrowed. Now they are cut into the hill resulting in helpful upward sloping backs to those greens. The base of the pin on the Knoll was invisible from the tee as the green was flatter and thus much easier to putt on than is the case now. The bunker in front was installed in Dad’s day. The turf was cut away at the top exposing a pumice “bullnose” face and balls rolled down it to a flat shelf at the bottom. Dad, not always the most confident of bunker players, would sometimes take a putter and bang the ball into the bank and up onto the green. At that time the Knoll green was relaid to provide a larger, lower, surface.

For months the substitute hole was a very short par three played with a chip from the front of the 7th tee to the putting green. The cards were not changed and the score made there was recorded as the score for the 12th.

The 3rd green was another to be significantly altered with the bank on the right being built up to prevent the automatic roll around onto the green, and to provide some space for bags and the trundlers that were just beginning to make their appearance.
