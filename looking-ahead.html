2000–FUTURE · POSSIBILITIES & DISCUSSION

Where might golf take us next?

A few simple possibilities for the game and the club. This is a place to look ahead, alongside the continuing historical record.

More ways to enjoy the game

Golf’s next chapter could offer more choices about how long a round takes and how people take part. Nine-hole golf, short courses, practice and social events provide starting points for imagining a club that fits different ages, abilities and amounts of spare time.

Explore the club groups → · The short-course story →

A course suited to its place

The Windsor Green story suggests a useful question for the future: how can the club keep offering enjoyable playing conditions while caring for water, trees and the land? Future choices may involve different turf, maintenance methods and expectations of what a good course looks like through the seasons.

Greenkeeping → · Windsor Green →

New tools, the same pleasure in playing

Imagine technology helping people learn, practise, find playing partners and organise competitions. How much could it improve access and enjoyment, and which parts of golf would members want to keep simple and personal?

A club people can afford and belong to

Membership choices, the cost of maintaining the course and the welcome offered to newcomers could shape who plays. The next generation may value a wider choice of experiences while still wanting the friendships, competition and sense of place that earlier members enjoyed.

Keeping the spirit of contribution

Volunteers and benefactors helped build this club. Future forms of service could preserve that sense of ownership as skills, available time, safety expectations and the organisation of work change.

Dave’s imagined second century

In 2014 Dave Mowat ended his history by imagining someone looking back over the following hundred years. His vision remains part of the original book. It offers another way to begin a conversation about what the club might become.
