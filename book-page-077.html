NOEL JOHNSON

Noel was another good young player who was a contemporary of John Ward, Michael Nicholson, and Jim Lancaster. Like them Noel was selected to represent Bay of Plenty Freyberg Rose Bowl teams of the Seventies and Eighties. Like Michael Noel spent some years in Whakatane where he won the Stroke Play Championship in 1987 and the Bay of Plenty Open in 1984. Noel was a multiple winner of the Tauranga Open played at Labour weekend.

E A (EDDIE) BURGESS

Eddie came to Tauranga with his family from Wellington when still at Primary School. He had already set himself the goal of becoming a top golfer before joining Tauranga and emulated Michael Nicholson by spending many an hour hitting wedge shots on our small practice fairway. Surprisingly Eddie won our Senior Championship on just the one occasion, his reputation tending to be built in other tournaments.

Misunderstanding the rules has cost many a golfer dearly. Our most infamous incident arose in the 1983 Senior Championship final between Brian Roderick and Michael Nicholson. Playing the 13th hole in the afternoon round Michael was on the green in three while Brian, having been out of bounds, was alongside the green in five. As Brian’s caddy moved towards the flagstick Brian made a gesture that Michael interpreted to mean Brian was conceding the hole.

When Michael lifted his ball Brian asked him why he had done so. In subsequent discussion Brian stated he had been asking his caddy to remove the flagstick. Bob Allen, the Captain, was refereeing the match. Bob was not a qualified referee and he awarded the hole to Brian though Michael should have replaced his ball under penalty of one stroke. Had this happened it is probable Michael would have won the hole and perhaps gone on to win the match. We will never know. The incident left Michael three down rather than one down. Michael showed his fighting spirit to square the match at the 18th, only to lose at the third extra hole.

Many opinions were expressed in relation to the incident. The facts may be summarised as follows. First it is unlikely that Brian would concede before chipping. Had he holed out he would have forced Michael to get down in two putts to win the hole. Secondly Michael should not have accepted Brian’s gesture before seeking confirmation of its meaning. Vague concessions are often made and it is wise to seek confirmation. Thirdly had Michael known the rules he could have put his case with a good chance of success. The incident highlighted the need for trained people to referee important matches and other fixtures. Fortunately since courses for Referees were instigated in 1986 our Club has had an enthusiastic core of qualified people available.

In a follow up to the above incident Michael was playing an Inter-Provincial match for Bay of Plenty when he marked and lifted his ball from the fringe of the green in the belief it was on the green. Realising his mistake he notified his opponent that he had incurred a penalty. The opponent claimed Michael had lost the hole. A referee, who happened to be a prominent Auckland administrator, was called. He agreed with the opponent that Michael had lost the hole whereupon Michael protested that he had been in the situation previously and requested the referee to consult the Rule Book before making a binding decision. The official did so and, somewhat shamefacedly, ruled in Michael’s favour.
