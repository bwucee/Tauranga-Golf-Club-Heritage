Colin Evitt was possibly the first professional to take on the job of Secretary/Treasurer following on from a series of Club members who had served in an honorary capacity. Col, as he was known, was a partner in an accountancy practise in the town. Though Col only held the job for a couple of years before moving to Hawkes Bay he established procedures that were to serve the Club well into the future. When Col vacated the job in 1957 he was replaced by N H E (Noel) Hockin, a colleague who had moved to Tauranga from Whakatane. A keen sportsman Noel had been active in cricket and golf at Whakatane, both in the playing side and in administration.

From 1951-53 he had been President of the Bay of Plenty District Golf Association. First appointed Secretary/Treasurer to the Club in 1957 Noel was promoted to the newly created post of Secretary Manager shortly afterward and became involved full time at the Club before retiring in 1967. With his background in sport and his professional qualifications in accounting Noel was a real asset to the Club. He was strong on public relations, a genial and friendly host, and a true gentleman in the old-fashioned sense of the word. His appointment was an inspired one and set the standard for those who were to follow.

As the job moved from Secretary to Secretary Manager and finally to Manager three men stand out as having brought something special to the role. A number of others came and went without leaving a lasting legacy in their wake. Noel Hockin was the first of the memorable trio. The next was R J (Royce) Lahood.

Royce Lahood was recruited from Inglewood where he and his brother Sonny owned a Snooker Parlour and a Dry Cleaning business. Like Noel, Royce was active in both golf and cricket as both player and administrator. At Inglewood Royce had not been averse to active involvement in pitch and ground preparation for cricket, nor in voluntary work at the Golf Course as required. Appointed to the Secretary Management job at Tauranga in 1970 Royce filled that role until 1983 when he retired. Royce was exceptional in the field of public relations. He knew every member by name and greeted each with warmth and enthusiasm thus giving him or her the impression that they were both respected and valued as Club members.

Even more impressive was his ability to recall the names of those visitors who came once a year to play in the Labour Weekend Tournament. These would be visibly surprised and delighted to be welcomed back by first name. Even green fee players who visited only occasionally

were sometimes surprised to be greeted by name.

The role of Club Manager is not an easy one to fill. In addition to good public relations the Manager is expected to be an expert Financial Manager, a good communicator, a convivial host, an excellent Personnel Manager, a promoter of the Club, a Tournament organiser. If in addition the incumbent should have an understanding of the game of golf including course management and player psychology this would certainly help him or her to better fill the role. To expect to find all this in one person is to expect the near impossible. Royce was no exception.
