Few lamented the demise of the green that had been developed in the early years of the new millennium. Hole 8 The fairway bunker on the right was eliminated and replaced 30m forward of the original position. Its partner on the left was enlarged by extending it towards the green. These bunkers now tended to penalise the better player’s poorer stroke rather than the average player’s best effort, a much more desirable aim on a club course. Hole 11 The bunker to the right and short of the green was reinstated but reconstructed so as to allow for easier maintenance.

It had long been felt the hole had lost much of its character when removal of the original bunker had opened up the right hand side of the green. Hole 14 The shrubbery in the Southeast corner was brought back into bounds. Hole 15 The Casuarina hedge along the first half of the fairway was removed after replacement groves of trees had been grown behind them. Fairway bunkers supported these groves in order to encourage play away from the left side of the fairway. The Out of Bounds line reverted to the boundary fence. Greenside bunkers were installed to the front left of the green to further encourage play away from the left side.

Hole 17 One of the bunkers in front of the green was eliminated in order to provide an opening to the green. It was considered unfair to expect an average to poorer player to be capable of playing a shot from one bunker across another to reach the pin. For men the hole was re-rated to play as a par four, its length being well within the recommended range, even now that driving distances had been constrained. As a corollary to these course changes the men’s committee had another look at stroke allocation and analysed stroke play cards returned by their own members over a three-month period including the 72 Hole Stroke Play Championships.

As a result of this analysis serious anomalies that had arisen earlier in the millennium were rectified. The review included the re-rating of holes two and twelve in particular. Now no par three hole rated ahead of any par four or five hole. The revised allocation closely resembled that which had existed throughout most of the previous sixty years. While the analysis showed the 17th hole deserved to be the third stroke hole this was considered to be too late in the round for that allocation as match players might very well lose their match before being able to exercise their right to use the stroke. In all respects the new allocation exactly followed the recommendations contained in

both the USGA and the NZGolf handbooks. The long-standing controversy associated with this topic was finally put to rest to the satisfaction of the majority of players following an awareness campaign to familiarise those interested in the aforementioned recommendations. As a result of the information made available the women’s committee came to realise that the par of a hole has no bearing on fair allocation of strokes for match play and their fifteenth hole was re-rated as the 5th stroke hole.
