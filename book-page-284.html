Many of these young people were those returning from Overseas in search of a more relaxed life style while highly qualified immigrants from a wide variety of countries added significantly to the pool of talent. The aspirations of these people led the country away from its traditional dependence on the production of meat, milk, wool, wine, wood and fruit. This eventually led to New Zealand becoming a World leader in Biotechnology. This was especially true of applications in genetic engineering related to the Agricultural, Horticultural, and Medical Sciences.

Developments in healthcare, environmentally friendly energy production, and other innovations in science and engineering contributed to significant advances in the wealth and living standards of the population.

With improved economic conditions golf enjoyed an upsurge of interest and the Tauranga Club was again forced to operate a waiting list. The four-day working week had become a reality for many people even though it was not made a constitutional requirement. This put further pressure upon courses as players made greater use of the facilities seven days a week. The population of Tauranga and the Western Bay of Plenty continued to grow and new courses were developed at Papamoa (opened 2026), and at Tauriko (opened 2035).

With more people enjoying a high standard of living and greater leisure time open Match Play tournaments of various formats began to be promoted by Clubs across the Country. The reinstatement of the once popular Five Day Veteran Golfers’ Match Play tournaments across the Country was in the forefront of this trend. In 2030 Tauranga became one of the first clubs to restage such an event. A year later the Club reinstated the Annual Labour Weekend Tournament in the original format that incorporated the Tauranga Open Championship last held in 1999. Full fields were attracted to both events with many participants coming from outside Clubs.

For much of its history the Tauranga Golf Club had struggled to maintain subscriptions at sufficiently high levels to ensure they covered annual running costs while allowing reserves to be put aside for future capital needs. For too long the Club had relied on sponsorship from City Businesses and on grants from those who controlled the distribution of profits from Poker machines. Sponsorships had always proved difficult to obtain while grants from Gaming Machines now tended to be channelled towards genuine charities rather than to Sporting Clubs. The growth of the city population, allied to higher living standards, meant that members were better able to meet the true costs of their sport.

In addition the advantageous siting of the course well within City boundaries meant there was never a shortage of people keen to join a Club that offered excellent playing conditions all year round together with Clubhouse facilities of a very high standard. The future promised to be at least as rewarding as had been the past.
