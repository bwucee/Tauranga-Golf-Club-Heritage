The rugby football ground alongside was also in active use while a cricket club had been established as early as 1873. The Tauranga Boating Club, involving both Rowing and Yachting, followed soon after in 1878. By 1880 prosperity and stability were beginning to develop and Tauranga was becoming much more than ‘an old soldiers settlement’. In 1882 Tauranga gained status as a Borough under the Municipalities Act. This allowed it to raise loans to finance the provision of public infrastructure such as roads and water supply, each being desperately needed. The first Mayor was George Vesey Stewart, an immigration agent from Ireland who had been largely responsible for arranging settlement of immigrants from Britain into the Western Bay of Plenty.

He is especially honoured as the founder of Katikati while Te Puke also owes its origins to him. His faithfully restored home, Athenree House, has become an important historic landmark on the road to Waihi Beach.

Wise forethought by the early settlers ensured that an area of 200 acres comprising sections 22-25 of the Parish of Te Papa was reserved from sale in order to preserve it for future use as a racecourse and recreation reserve. This was gazetted on 10th of October 1878 and the area became known as the Racecourse Reserve. Thus the stage was set for people further ahead in time to develop the Reserve as a permanent asset to the developing township.

While the origins of the game of Golf are widely debated credit for the establishment of officially organised golf must lie with the ‘Honourable Company of Edinburgh Golfers’ founded in 1744. The first British ‘Open’ was held at Prestwick in 1860 and was ‘open’ only to professionals. The amateur championship of Great Britain followed in 1885. With these dates in mind it must be said that golf in this part of the World got off to an early start as Royal Adelaide, the first Australian Golf Club was founded in 1870. The Dunedin Golf Club followed just one year later. Surprisingly the first Club in the USA, Oakhurst in Virginia, was not established until 1885.

John Reid, who founded the Saint Andrews Club at Yonkers in New York in 1888, is regarded as the father of golf in the USA as it was he who provided the initial stimulus that popularised the game there. Though the first United States Open was not held until as late as 1895, the growth of the game throughout America was such that by 1900 over 1000 courses had been established there. In that same year the A G Spalding and Brothers Sporting Goods Company contracted the immortal English Professional Harry Vardon to participate in a barnstorming exhibition tour of the USA to promote their new Gutta Percha ball, the Vardon Flyer.

The exhibitions during this ten-month tour attracted thousands of spectators, including many who had had no previous exposure to the great game. The spark that John Reid had struck was fanned into raging flames by the Vardon

tour. By the time it was over more than one hundred new courses were planned or under construction and the development of American Golf was in overdrive. In view of these facts it is significant that Golf had such an early start in the small, isolated settlement of Tauranga. Charles Richie Howden was the New Zealand equivalent of America’s John Reid. He arrived in Dunedin from Edinburgh in 1861 at the age of 23. He became the first Captain of the Dunedin Golf Club in 1871 where the subscription is recorded as having been set at one guinea. A guinea was 21 shillings designed to include the commission payable in the pound on livestock sales.
