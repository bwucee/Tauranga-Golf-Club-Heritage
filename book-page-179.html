It was almost as though it was his private park and to some extent he resented the golfers who came daily to make a mess of it by chopping up the fairways, walking their trundlers too close to the greens and tees, and otherwise making a nuisance of themselves. There are still those of us at the club who would not dream of taking a trundler across a tee, or close to a green for fear of provoking a roar of wrath from the ghost of a greenkeeper past.

One could understand Grant’s feeling that the course belonged to him as there can be no doubt that Grant Silvester put his heart and soul into producing the course in the best possible condition day by day, month by month, year by year, throughout his many years in the job. His signature remains to this day as so many of the tees, greens, bunkers, and other features of the course were reformed or developed during his tenure. He brought the course into a new age and laid the foundation for much that has followed. Grant left the Club to take on the role of Superintendent of Parks and Reserves for the Western Bay of Plenty District Council and later, with his wife Faye, retired to live in Mangonui, North Auckland, where he continued his love of fishing.

THE POST SILVESTER YEARS

Les Hill replaced Grant in 1973 and was able to maintain the rate of course improvement that members

had become accustomed to. Golf Course maintenance is like so many other aspects of life: unless you strive for improvement it seems you are doomed to go backwards. Les was enticed to join us from Rotorua by the offer of a salary of $3900 and the prospect of accommodation in a house owned by the Club. This house was located at 1368 Cameron Road almost directly opposite the entrance to the Racecourse Reserve and Mr. Hill took up residence there that year. To finance the purchase of this house the Club sold the section they owned off Hynd’s Road behind the Substation.

Les Hill was an excellent Greenkeeper, well versed in the modern concepts of Turf Management. During his tenure the course was well maintained and was further improved under his guidance. Unfortunately he pushed himself too hard in attempting to get as much done as possible. He virtually ran around the course and this self-imposed pressure could not be sustained indefinitely. In 1978 Les took up an appointment with the Pukekohe Club where he continued to produce playing conditions of which he could justifiably be proud.

Following a period during which a number of employees served short terms in charge of the course Alan Martin was enticed from Mt. Denby, the Whangarei headquarters of the Northland Golf Club. One of our members played Mt. Denby subsequent to this appointment and, upon mentioning that we now had Alan in charge of our course, the laconic response from the Mt. Denby member was to the effect that “we would now enjoy some excellent paths on our course.” Grant Patterson was appointed in 1980. Grant was a New Zealander who had been working in Australia for some years where he had considerable experience in developing golf courses from scratch.
