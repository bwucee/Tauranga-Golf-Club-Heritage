At that time few clubs held such tournaments and those tournaments that were held appear to have been given NZ status for the ‘principal event’. Thus at our first tournament 56 starters competed in four Match Play flights for: (1) The NZ Premiership - the first 16 gross qualifiers. Won by V Mortenson of Te Puke. (2) The BOP Handicap - the first 16 nett qualifiers. Won by R H Phelan of Tauranga. (3) The Tauranga Handicap - the second 16 nett qualifiers. Won by A Sharpe of Northcote. (4) Gate Pa Handicap. Won by G Willcock of Tauranga.

The tournament was operated from the old cottage style clubhouse behind the 18th green. First prize for the ‘NZ Premiership’ was a magnificent dinner set donated by Johnny Walker. It caused some consternation because of its value, especially relative to other division prizes. Later such problems were sorted out and the amount spent in each division became more or less equal.

For the 1954 tournament Ron Galloway and John McKenzie donated Cups, Gavin Wallace a Silver Plate, and Johnny Walker a Rose Bowl. In doing so they set the pattern under which the tournament was run over future years. In 1958 Ted Bartlett donated the Honours Board that still hangs in the clubhouse. In the same year, and again in 1959, Galloway, McKenzie, Wallace, and Walker all gave money to be used to provide personal awards to the winners of their respective trophies while George Willcock provided the prize for the final day Stableford. During the same year D C (David) Collins donated the Plate that is still in use for the National Vets’ Championship.

David was a winner of that championship and had earlier captained the New Zealand Cricket team long before it became known as the ‘Black Caps’. He and Ron Galloway had also given trophies to our local Veterans’ Society for competition in a ‘Yankee Tournament’.

In contemplating all of the foregoing one cannot help but be impressed by the way in which this handful of enthusiasts did all in their power to stimulate the interest of players of their day. As the number of entrants in the Five Day Tournament grew other trophies were presented. These include Dr John Mark’s Trophy (1966), George Mole’s Trophy (1973), Cliff Colson’s Salver (1974), and Basil Coxhead’s Salver (1977). The names of our trophies bring back memories of so many wonderful personalities who gave so much to the development of the Society and to the Golf Club itself.

In 1959 we held our first New Zealand Veteran Championships, the winner of the main event being W J (Billy) Castell, a member of our own Club. Almost every day for many years Billy would head for the Golf Course right after closing his shoe store on The Strand. He would then play two balls over as many holes as he could fit in, rarely having to move more than five or ten paces between the two balls. Maurice White was once scheduled to play Billy in the final of the Intermediate Club Championship. Maurice asked Jim Paterson the day before how Jimmy thought the match would go. “He will beat you by whatever margin he thinks appropriate” was the candid, if undiplomatic response.

So it proved the following day.
