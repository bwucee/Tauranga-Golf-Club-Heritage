Alternatively the cost of building over the existing basement and foundation was estimated at $630 000 of which $230 000 would be available following the wind up of the Golf Club Trust. This Trust had been set up following the sale of the house owned by the Club and located in Cameron Road near the Reserve entrance.

Concerns were expressed during the meeting that the Board should be paying off debt rather than incurring more by going ahead with the development. The General Manager was able to explain that term liabilities had totalled $440 000 in August 1998 at which point the Board had claimed that this debt would be paid off within six years. In fact all but $82 000 had been paid off by August 2000 meaning that $358 000 had been paid off in just two years. This indicated the Board’s financial policies were working and gave Board members the confidence to recommend the Club borrow the required funds to complete the proposed development.

Subsequent to the presentation the Special General Meeting authorised the Board to borrow up to $400 000 to complete the proposed development. Had the Trustees not resolved to wind up the Trust and make the funds available the project would not have been viable. In addition to the $230 000 in cash provided by them the Trustees resolved to write off a loan of $130 000 owed to the Trust by the Club. This had been advanced some years previously to enable the purchase of urgently required course maintenance machinery.

Financial sustainability was the first priority of the Board when considering the proposal. They were meticulous in their planning in order to ensure the budgetary goals were both conservative and sustainable. The Club’s auditors, Murray, Crossman and Partners, were able to report that, while results might vary significantly from those forecast, these variations were likely to be positive rather than negative as a result of conservative forecasts and probable growth of membership, the latter a hopeful consequence of the proposed upgrade. Financial projections for the subsequent years indicated the level of borrowing now envisaged would have little impact on subscription levels over succeeding years with repayments being amortised so as to require both present and future members to bear the cost.

Miden Construction, the principals of which were club member brothers Denis and Michael Smith, were awarded the contract in a fully competitive and open tendering process. The majority of sub-contracts

also went to club members again in open tendering. While these were the responsibility of Miden Construction it is probably true that they “sharpened their pencils” to give their club the best possible price. For that the Club should be very grateful. For the record club member related firms or individual contractors who were involved included Stewart Browne Electrical, Tauranga Hardware and Plumbing (Brian McCord), Don Allen Painting and Decorating, and Helen Fritchley Interior Design. During construction the normal business of the Club was planned to be as free from interruption as possible. A temporary office was located adjacent to the putting green and food and beverage services relocated to the trundler shed.
