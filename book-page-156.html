Administration

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 156 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

1985: Concern was expressed at the slide in membership. There were 23 vacancies in Men’s A category. New membership limits were set for 1986. These increased Men’s A to 375 with 25 discretionary places, Men’s B to 125, Ladies’ A plus B to 350. Fairway bunkers on the 8th were authorised. The requirement for men to wear a jacket in the lounge was deleted from regulations as a result of a motion to the AGM. 1992: The trimming of tree ‘skirts’ was very favourably received. The new eighth green was opened. 1993: A report from the NZ Turf Research Institute was very favourable to the condition of the course, especially the greens.

This reflected very well on the work of Grant Patterson and his staff. The Championship pennant team had won the BOP Pennant for the first time in fifteen years. Michael Nicholson gave praise to the younger players in the team. The junior programme had done much to bring about this success. The Board of Management concept was first mentioned as being worthy of consideration. Lack of financial support for the Golf Club Trust was seen as a matter of some concern. 1994: It was reported that the existing irrigation system made it difficult to maintain the greens to their best potential. The system had been poorly laid with sub-standard piping having been laid too shallow resulting in frequent breakdown.

An urgent upgrade was required before the course development plan could be properly instigated. The May committee meeting that year was disrupted by the breakout of a fire in the Bar. As luck would have it Neil Marson left the meeting to collect his wife Shirley from an appointment. He returned immediately to enquire why there was “steam” emanating from beneath the Bar slide. On looking out towards the Bar I said “that is not steam. Those are flames.” Rapid action followed and the fire was extinguished by using all the available extinguishers from upstairs and downstairs though not before substantial damage to the interior of the Bar had been sustained.

It transpired that the women volunteering to do Bar duty that day had cleaned the ash trays by tipping the butts into a waste bin under the Bar as was usual. Unfortunately on this occasion there were apparently some incompletely extinguished butts in the trays and several hours later the bin burst into open flames. Another example of the hidden dangers of smoking! It was fortunate the monthly committee meeting was scheduled for that Monday evening and that Neil had an errand that took him from the committee room at the critical moment. But for these circumstances the Clubhouse would certainly have been a total loss before the Fire Brigade had reached the scene.

Some thought it may have been better if the clubhouse had been destroyed and a new one built under insurance. Such thoughts ignore the serious loss of Club records, cups, trophies, and other paraphernalia that would have resulted. The minutes report that the meeting reconvened after the committee had “distinguished” the fire and the Fire Brigade had attended to check that there was no further risk. 1995: The Players Committee wrote to the Board requesting that they, the Players Committee, should

act as the Greens Committee. The Board reminded the committee that they, the Board, were responsible for ensuring that the course was presented in the best possible condition for the benefit of members. To facilitate this they had appointed a highly qualified Greens Superintendent and charged him with the responsibility of achieving this. As with other staff he would report directly to the General Manager. 1999: The AGM approved the establishment of the position of Club Captain, such elected member to chair the Players Committee and to serve on the Board. The membership category of Intermediate was also approved. The Golf Club Trust were now willing to wind up and to transfer their funds to the Board, such funds to be utilised in the proposed Clubhouse modernisation.
