Before liquor licences were granted to Sports Clubs these operated illegal bars and club members who were policemen found it appropriate to turn a blind eye so they could enjoy the same after match social hour as did their playing partners. The law is indeed an ass in certain situations. It was a relief to all when enlightened legislation allowed responsible clubs such as our own to legitimise their activities. Though it is now possible to run a Calcutta Sweep legally it is ironic that their attraction has long since passed.

This may be because people find a legal activity less attractive though a more likely explanation is that there is such a plethora of gambling opportunities now available that Calcuttas attract little bidding in comparison to days gone by. Certainly they were well past their ‘use by’ date before they were finally put to rest in our club environment.

Something similar happened to the Labour Weekend Tournament. From the days when you almost had to put your name down for the next year’s tournament at the closure of the current one participation steadily declined until, in the nineties, it became uneconomic to continue to promote the tournament. The attraction of other activities, the extension of weekend trade, the reluctance of people to travel and accommodate themselves away from home, all these and more contributed to the demise of the tournament. Several attempts were made to revive it without success. These included offering

competition for women and alternative formats but nothing came of these initiatives. Perhaps those who remember the ‘old days’ with affectionate nostalgia have a genuine basis for such feelings. Life does appear to have been more relaxed in earlier years and the Labour Weekend Tournament is but one of the casualties of the high-pressure age we have inherited.

THURSDAY GOLF

At our Club Thursday afternoon vies with Saturday afternoon as the most important day in the realm of Men’s Golf. It was not always so. The first mention of any semblance of organised golf on a Thursday comes from the recorded history of the Tauranga Veteran Golfing Society. Shortly after the Second World War a small group of elderly golfers began meeting on both Tuesday and Thursday afternoons to enjoy informal fourballs. Gradually every second Tuesday developed into a day when an informal draw was made and from this the Veteran Golfers Society evolved in the early fifties. Tuesday remained as Veteran’s day until 1957 when Thursday was adopted as their official day.

Gradually other members began to seek a mid-week game and these called themselves Businessmen to distinguish themselves from the Vets. As their numbers increased the ‘Businessmen’ became more assertive and it was not long before the first few grumbles about ‘those old buggers in front’ began to surface. Eventually the Vets decided to adhere to the old adage that discretion is the better part of valour and gracefully reverted to their original Tuesday afternoon draw. Naturally there were those amongst the older players who decided they wanted to continue their Thursday golf and thus a group of these continue to precede the ‘Ball in the Bucket’ brigade each Thursday afternoon, even to the present day.
