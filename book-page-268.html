The Una Wickham Memorial Tournament

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 268 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

The Club lost its greatest woman player with the untimely death of Una Wickham in 1983. The Women’s Committee decided to institute a tournament in her honour and endeavour to develop it into a truly prestigious event on the Northern Golfing Calendar. This became a reality with the tournament soon attracting the best players from Whangarei to Taupo and sometimes beyond. For some years it served as a trial for the Northern Inter Provincial teams but sadly lost support as other priorities commanded the attention of the top players. It is unfortunate that we no longer have the opportunity to see outstanding women players of the quality of Brenda Ormsby, Jan Arnold, Tina Howard, Gina Scott, our own Joanne Brooks, and many others.

These women inspired local players, both men and women, to endeavour to raise their own standards. It is to be hoped that one day the top women players from around the country will again grace our links in honour of our greatest woman player. A year by year history of the tournament, and its predecessor the Tauranga Women’s Open Match Play Tournament follows.

1976 Shirley Johnson of Rotorua won the final of this, the inaugural staging of the tournament, defeating Una Wickham 6/4 in a surprisingly one-sided final. Earlier Shirley had defeated a youthful Brenda Ormsby of Te Puke in another easy victory. Shirley was then playing number one in the Bay of Plenty representative team, a position she clearly warranted on these performances.

1977 Shirley Johnson successfully defended the title she had won the previous year, easily holding out the challenge of Penny MacDougall in the final. Earlier in the tournament Tauranga’s Mary Parkinson had provided Shirley with much stronger opposition before losing one down.

1978 Alison Eather of Auckland won the Championship after a series of tight matches. She was three down with five to play versus Tauranga’s Peg Copestake yet got home at the 20th. 1977 finalist Penny MacDougall was five up at one stage yet lost to Alison at the 18th. Playing Avis Osborne (Mt Maunganui) in the final Eather was two down with two to play yet again won on the 20th. Such a series of fighting come backs must seldom have been matched in this or any tournament. The semi-final against Cushla Hickey of Opotiki was the antithesis of the other three matches, Miss Hickey being five down after twelve holes only to come back strongly before losing by just one hole.

1979 Jocelyn Glanfield (North Shore) became the new champion, defeating Sue Bunt (Titirangi) one up in the final after accounting for the defending champion Alison Hasey (Nee Eather) two up in the semi- final. Sue Bunt went on to a distinguished career in Golf Administration where her work over a number of years did much to further Golf in Auckland and in New Zealand.

1980 The tournament continued to be seen as a trial for the Bay of Plenty Russell Grace team while the Auckland and Waikato selectors were also very interested in the form displayed by potential team members. As a result the tournament attracted strong entries from these regions. New Zealand representatives Cherry Kingham (Grange), Jan Arnold (Morrinsville), and Brenda Rhodes (Nee Ormsby) highlighted another strong field in 1980. In the final it was Jan Arnold who prevailed 4/3 over Kingham who never recovered from Arnold’s sizzling outward 32. Earlier Kingham had scored a surprisingly easy victory over Rhodes.
