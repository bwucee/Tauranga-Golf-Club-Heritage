ARTHUR TOWSEY

Arthur was another whose services the Club recognised by conferring Life Membership upon him. He is best remembered for his efficient management of the Bar over a long period dating back to the days of the old Clubhouse behind the 18th green. A martinet in his domain, woe betides anyone who overstepped the bounds of propriety. For many years a six o’clock closure was the rule despite the fact that the entire operation was outside the law applying at that time. Many a member narrowly avoided losing a finger or two as the shutter came down precisely on the hour. Fortunately the stairs then ran down at the back of the Bar and, if you were in the know, and Arthur was in a good mood, you might just grab a quick one on your way out from the back door of the lounge where it opened onto the stairs.

Whilst on the subject of the ‘Nineteenth Hole’ it seems that the Veterans’ must have been a ‘dry’ society for, in 1960, a subcommittee waited upon the Club Committee to ask if Bar facilities could be made available on Tuesdays and Thursdays. This was granted much to the relief of many a member.

Dr JOHN MARK Johnny was perhaps the most dedicated of all who served the Veterans’ Society. He was President for five years after previously serving on committee. For two years John served as both President and Secretary and, as he himself put it, general factotum. This was occasioned by the fact that no one at the Annual General Meeting was willing to accept the role of Secretary so it was left to the committee to fill the post. This they never did so Johnny simply carried on. Possibly the task of running the Tournament was beginning to look formidable enough to discourage potential Secretaries and, when Harry Du Faur resigned in 1965 after three terms in the job no one was anxious to succeed him.

Harry had established a very efficient Secretarial Management System which few felt they could emulate.

John Mark and his sister Anne developed the Tournament draw system we use so that a player does not play with his opponent until he meets him in a match. When John died in 1967 the Mount and Tauranga Veterans began playing for the Mark Memorial Shield to recognise his work for Veterans’ golf. In 1965 George Mole and Ron Horman presented this shield for competition between the two clubs. In 1966 Dr Mark presented a trophy for our annual Tournament and it was fitting that this was presented to the winner by his sister Anne following the Jubilee year Tournament in 1977.

HARRY BRADMORE

Harry was another to be honoured with Life Membership of the Golf Club. A foundation member of the Veterans he continued a program of planting and care of the trees on the course that had been initiated

by Mr Mirrielees in the first years of course development. In this Harry was assisted by his son Maurice, another keen golfer with a real interest in improving the aesthetics of the course. Another son, Alan, was a victim of World War Two. Alan is remembered through the Alan Bradmore Memorial Trophy that is contested in the Saturday Programme.

GAVIN WALLACE

Gavin was our second President and also served as Treasurer for a number of years. He stimulated a lot of interest in Veteran activities and retained a lively interest in the society beyond his playing days. He donated a number of trophies during his membership.
