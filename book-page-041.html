Out of the oval

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 41 / 305Next page →

The move from the oval was a dramatic development in the history of the club. It signalled a growing maturity whereby players would no longer be satisfied with makeshift fairways and greens. The time had come to develop the property into a course that all could be proud of. The year 1936 was seen as being especially significant in foreshadowing a new era in the Club’s development. Quoting from the BOP Times of March 30th that year:

“A disability which the Club has suffered from in the past has been that its ground staff have not had experience in golf green keeping and have been at a disadvantage in not knowing just what they should work for to afford the best conditions for play. The committee has accordingly engaged an experienced greenkeeper, Mr. W H Coles, formerly greenkeeper to the Napier Golf Club and a foundation member and member of the executive of the New Zealand Greenkeepers’ Association. Mr. Coles takes over his duties today and, with the benefit of his experience, should do much to produce and keep good greens and fairways on the course.

The Club is also fortunate in that it has been able to obtain the services of Mr. Jas. Mowat to serve as its Professional. Mr. Mowat has for some years been Professional to the Poverty Bay Golf Club and is a member of the Executive Committee of the New Zealand Professional Golfers’ Association. He has had experience both in Scotland and in the United States of America, and comes to the Club with first class recommendations, both as a player and as a teacher. For young players and learners there is a tremendous advantage in being kept to the right style until the game is consolidated. For the more experienced golfer there is much to be gained from having an expert on hand to locate and cure the inevitable faults that creep into the swing.

Under Mr. Mowat’s guidance it is to be hoped that there will be an all round improvement in the standard of the game in Tauranga. Mr. Mowat has already arrived and takes up his duties forthwith”.

The next significant development in the evolution of the course came in 1967 when noted British Golf Architect, Commander John Harris, was invited to visit the course and advise on possible improvements that could be made. Commander Harris was in New Zealand under contract to the Government owned Tourist Corporation of New Zealand that was at the time developing the Wairakei Resort Course near Taupo. He was free to make himself available to clubs such as ours who sought his assistance. His charge to visit Tauranga for a day and make a detailed report covering ten pages was £75, a truly bargain price for world-class advice. Commander Harris, Greens Chairman Alan Davidson, and Greens Superintendent Grant Silvester walked the course with Alan and Grant hanging on every word spoken

by the designer. Grant later commented: “That man seems to have a built in spirit level and chain measure as, when I carried out his recommendations, I found the levels and distances to be exactly as he described them. I found his plans for alterations so clear and precise that any contractor or green keeper could carry them through with utmost expediency.” Alan made comprehensive notes from which, together with the Harris report, he was able to construct Pleistocene models depicting each of the holes as Commander Harris had envisaged them. These models were used by Grant and his staff, greatly assisted by the earth moving and contracting skills of Garth Renner, to faithfully complete the process of converting the Harris report from paper, through Pleistocene model, to completed golf holes, over the next few years.

This entire process was continually supervised and monitored by Mr Davidson
