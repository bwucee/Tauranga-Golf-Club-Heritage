Out of the oval

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

In 1934, with the confidence of the new 33-year lease behind them, the Club administration was encouraged to explore new possibilities in course design and facilities. Having determined that the oval was not conducive to the development of quality golf holes the committee commissioned well-known Auckland Professional Alex Murray to advise on the layout of new holes. As part of his commission he was requested to see if it would be feasible to eliminate ‘The Drop’ and ‘The Hummocks’ holes that, quite understandably, were most unpopular with members who did not enjoy the climb back up the hill from the present third green. Anyone foolish enough not to play a provisional ball following a doubtful drive at the third hole today will readily appreciate the point.

As a result of Alex Murray’s review the five holes that utilised the oval were discontinued along with the old 7th, 10th, and 11th holes. To replace them the present 3rd, 4th, 6th, 9th, 10th, 15th, and 16th were developed and they remain in much the same form today. The remaining ten original holes were retained but renumbered to accommodate the very different sequence of holes necessitated by the changes. Below is an outline of the holes as they are now played with a brief summary of their individual history.

NUMBER 1 - THE PA - PAR 4

This was first played as the 8th hole and featured a slight dogleg to the right from a tee west of the present position. A large gum on the ridge some 50m from the green was a prominent feature. Those driving past the gum were inordinately proud of the achievement. As has been the case with other prominent gum trees this one was lost to high winds. Gums are not especially long lived in our conditions.

NUMBER 2 - THE SUMMIT - PAR 4

This was the ninth hole and originally named ‘The Ridge’. The men’s tee consisted of two shallow terraces cut into the hillock forming the present tee. The women played from a tee near the gum tree on the ridge. The right hand green was developed in the sixties in order to enable the main green to be rested. It continues to be used on a regular basis, perhaps over-used. NUMBER 3 - JUDEA - PAR 4 This was developed preparatory to the move out of the oval. Recent developments in equipment now allow quite ordinary players to ‘take on the corner’. Few, if any amongst club members, were able to achieve this feat when using the old wound ball.

NUMBER 4 - THE GULLY - PAR 3

Another hole developed to replace those in the oval. This is a classical short par three where the green and surrounds have remained in their original form. Extended tees are considered by some to add to the challenge offered while others see them as defeating the intention of the original design. The causeway was built up in the Fifties to provide a much easier passage across the gully. The pond is our one water feature though hardly considered a hazard to most players. It has served its intended purpose of draining the area at the bottom of the driving range.
