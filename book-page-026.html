More distant fairways

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

Relationships were established early with neighbouring clubs and tournaments and Interclub days attracted visiting players from Rotorua, Whakatane and Matamata. A Ladies’ Committee was established and they worked hard under difficult conditions to cater for such occasions. They cleaned the ‘tin shed’, peeled buckets of potatoes, cooked meats at home, cut up baths of green salads, and baked mountains of cakes and scones. We can be confident no visitor left the course hungry. Inspired by the competition met on the occasion of these visits members were keen to improve their games and to this end Mr Hood and Mr McCormack, professionals based in Rotorua, were encouraged to visit Tauranga on some weekends in order to conduct lessons.

These lessons had to be held on weekends for few members could afford to take time off during the week to play golf and the grazing stock were seldom disturbed by players during week days.

The new course was for many years known as the Gate Pa course just as the Racing Club was said to race at Gate Pa even though the Reserve is more accurately located in Greerton, a mile or so South of the site of the 1864 battle of Gate Pa and the commemorative Church that now stands on that site. Greerton was named in honour of Colonel Greer who was a prominent British military leader during the battle of Gate Pa. The suburb of Greerton had its origins when it was divided into allotments that were granted to soldiers who wished to remain in the area after the cessation of hostilities.

At some time in the thirties Dr. John Mark was playing the 10th hole when ‘over the road’ was not out of bounds. Somehow his ball lodged in the roof of a small shed that was sited there. John’s companions insisted he play the lie so they assisted him onto the roof from whence he was able to knock the ball down the corrugations onto the fairway.

The Bay of Plenty Beacon was a Newspaper of the time that was published in Whakatane. The issue for July 7th 1939 carried an item naming the Tauranga team to represent the Club versus Whakatane Golf Club at Whakatane on the following Sunday. Many of those names are notable in the Club’s history and/or in the history of the town. They were: Green, Bateman, Renshaw, Randell, Lusk, Page, Bradmore, Bullock, Douglas, Roach, Hay, Sisson, Kennedy, Jordan, Morpeth, Snodgrass, Harley, Phelan, Matheson, Kirkby, Steele, Verry, Strang. No first names or initials were given. Many of these men feature elsewhere in this publication. Older readers will remember many above such as Bob Hay, who owned a Chemist Shop in the town.
