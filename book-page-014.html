The first tee

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 14 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

As no other claimants have come forward, or are ever likely to do so, we are happy to recognise Dr. Brown as the founder of golf in Tauranga and to give to him the honour of opening proceedings by driving the first ball to commence the first one hundred years of golf in the region.

A report of the AGM of 1910 was printed in the BOP Times of 27th April of that year. The meeting was held in the office of Mr. Phillips who was elected Captain, while Messrs. Sharp, Bull, and McKenzie were elected to the committee. The position of Secretary-Treasurer was not filled and it was left to the committee to fill the vacancy. Misses E and M Wrigley were welcomed as new members along with Messrs. D Selby, A Tudhope, J A McLeod, and G L Cook.

The name Wrigley was well known in the Town in earlier years as 16th Avenue was known as Wrigley Street until some ‘progressive’ council decided to destroy so much of Tauranga’s history by dropping many original Street names and replacing them with rather uninspired numbered Avenues. The Avenues originally did not go beyond 11th Avenue. The intersection of that street with Cameron Road was known as Faulkner’s Corner in recognition of the General Store run by the pioneer Faulkner family. Mr Wrigley himself was a well-known local merchant who owned cutters that ran a regular service between Auckland and Tauranga. He also served a term as Mayor of the township.

The names Sharp and Tudhope remained prominent in the City more than a century later in the Legal Partnership of that name.

In addition to the names mentioned above the original list of members who played at the Domain course included W H Clemson, R Stubbing, F Cramer-Roberts, W M Darley, J A McKenzie, H B Mountfort and ET Baker. The subscription for men rose to one pound and for women just five shillings. Dr Brown arrived at golf in a two-wheel gig driven by Arthur Faulkner whose family were to have a significant influence on trading and transport in the district for many years into the future. The good doctor’s bag contained just four hickory-shafted clubs: a driver/brassie, a cleek, a lofter and a putter. His driving club was somewhat between a 1 and a 2 wood, the cleek was akin to a 2 or 3 iron while the lofter would be a little like a modern sand iron.

The responsibility of transporting this burden around the course was delegated to his caddie, one Ossie Daines, who received a shiny sixpence for his trouble. Other players may well have had a more extensive range of equipment including the mid-iron, mashie, mashie niblick, and niblick, corresponding more or less to the 3, 5, 7, and 9 irons of today. The fairway woods called Brassie and Spoon (3 wood) would hardly be required given the length of the holes they played. The 1911 balance sheet of the club showed just one asset, a hand mower valued at thirty shillings.

There were no employees to be paid and probably no shortage of volunteers when and as required.

The course laid out by Dr Brown and based at the Domain was bounded to the North by Brown Street, to the East by Cameron Road, to the South by McLean Street, and to the West by the sea. It started from the South West corner of the Wharepai Domain, the first green being located where the Band Rotunda used to stand at the top of the rise near the entrance to the Tennis Courts and behind the Bowling Green The second hole then ran through the present Rugby ground into the Domain extension which was first designated as High School reserve land. The course then ran through swampy ground below The Elms Mission House before moving down on to Sulphur Point.
