Born in 1903, Joan came to Tauranga with her family in 1910. Her father, A J Mirrielees, was a pharmacist in the town and a very active member of the original club based at the Tauranga Domain. He maintained his interest when the Club moved out to the Racecourse Reserve in 1914 and was largely responsible for the planting of a great variety of trees on the Reserve. Many of these survive to this day adding both beauty and strategic difficulty to the golf course. Joan was encouraged by her father to take up the game from an early age. Joan maintained this interest throughout her life, so much so that she was able to achieve victory in the Rotorua Ladies’ Golf Club Annual Veterans’ Tourney in 1965, shooting a meritorious 89 to annex the title.

Joan graduated as a teacher in 1924 and taught at all levels in schools throughout the North Island. In 1938 Joan went to Bradford in Yorkshire, England, as an Exchange teacher, later moving on to Lytham St. Anne’s, well known to New Zealanders as the scene of Bob Charles’ Open victory in 1963.

Back home in New Zealand Joan was active on a number of fronts including a stint as the hostess of the Women’s Hour and Shopping Report on local radio 1ZD (now BOP 95 FM) in 1959 and the early sixties. For many years she filled the role of editor of the Tauranga Historical Society Journal, researching and publishing many invaluable articles about the early history of the district. In 1984 Joan’s contribution in this sphere was recognised with the Societies’ Heritage award. Another special honour came Joan’s way when she was presented with the Key to the City in 1975. Credit for the early work done on this history goes to Miss Mirrielees.

She would have loved to see her efforts culminate in a published version but, sadly, that was not to be achieved in her time.

MRS EVELYN SIM. (NEN)

In 1974 Nen Sim became the first women member to be elected to the Executive of the Club. In later years other women members served on the committee following a successful resolution to allow for a representative from the Women’s committee to join the Executive. Nen, on the other hand, was a pioneer, being elected in open competition with male nominees. No better pioneer in the role could have been found. Nen had considerable experience on the Women’s committee having served as handicapper and for many years she had written a weekly column of Ladies’ Golf News in the Bay of Plenty Times under the pseudonym ‘Bunkered’. Contrary to the fears of some on the ‘Men’s Committee’ Nen did not play the part of a militant feminist upon joining the committee.

Instead it soon became apparent that she was there for one purpose only, to look after the interests of all members of the Club,

both male and female. In her quiet and pleasant manner Nen did much over the next eight years to change the mind set of those members of the committee who still retained some of the old concepts of the club being run by the men for the benefit of the men. Nen has a logical mind and her contributions to discussion were always to the point and frequently indicated the best path to follow. During Nen’s time on the committee the social life of the Club experienced a period of enhanced activity with successful dinner/dance evenings complementing the regular Opening and Closing day highlights. In 1985 Nen

was honoured with Life Membership of the Club. Few could be more worthy of the honour.
