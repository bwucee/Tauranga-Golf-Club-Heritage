The original Gate Pa links

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 33 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

The layout of the course has been changed several times and no doubt there will be further changes in the future though these are likely to be minor in comparison to what has gone before. The first course designed by Fred Rutter utilised land within the circumference of the racetrack as well as much of the present leased area. We have been fortunate to obtain a description of the course as it was prior to 1934 from Mr Jack Bateman, a fine player who followed his 1934 Championship win with another three victories in the years from 1946 to 1949. In 1948 and 1949 Jack added the BOP Champion of Champions title to his club victories, an honour not previously achieved by one of our own and not matched until the Michael Nicholson era began in 1970.

The following is a description of those original 18 holes as Jack recalled them.

NUMBER 1 - THE AVENUE - PAR 4

This is now the 11th hole and essentially the same as it was then. The tee was conveniently placed adjacent to the ’Jockey’s Room’ clubhouse and the large gum trees that now form the avenue were then in their infancy.

NUMBER 2- THE KNOLL - PAR 3

This has now become the 12th and is known as Tauranga’s signature hole. According to Jack it is now considerably less formidable than when he played it. The tee shot was played through an avenue of gorse to a sloping green perched on a pinnacle. This was somewhat higher than the present cone, the green having been lowered and thus enlarged at some time in the forties. There were shallow bunkers at the sides and back and the entire green was surrounded by long grass and stumpy gorse. While there was no bunker at the front a high premium was still placed on accuracy and correct club selection. When asked his opinion of this hole in 1967 commander Harris glanced briefly at it, said: “it might have been all right in 1900.

Take five feet off the top and dish it.” With that he walked to the 13th tee.

NUMBER 3 - PYES PA - PAR 5

This is now the 13th and renamed the Crescent. Apart from the new greenside bunker the hole was essentially the same as it is now and followed the racecourse fence around the top turn.

NUMBER 4 - THE STABLES - PAR 4

The tee was close to where the 14th tee is now located and the stroke was played across the oval towards the now 11th fairway to a green near the straight entrance.

NUMBER 5 - THE STRAIGHT - PAR 4

This hole was played as a slight dogleg more or less following the inside running rail to a green near the winning post.

NUMBER 6 - THE TERRACE - PAR 3

Here the tee stroke was played from inside the oval to a green located south of the grandstand and behind the then first tee. This would be where overflow parking can now be found.
