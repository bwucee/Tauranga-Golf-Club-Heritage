Professional golf returns to Tauranga

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 260 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

When well performed New Zealand Professional Greg Turner promoted a series of tournaments to provide opportunities for budding young players in New Zealand the Tauranga Club was quick to volunteer to be one of the host clubs. Paul and Sheryl Adams of Carrus Corporation, a local company involved in sub-division developments, became the name sponsor. Many other local companies became involved to ensure that the first tournament was able to attract a quality field including leading Amateurs together with the best of the Country’s Professionals.

THE 2006 CARRUS TAURANGA OPEN CHAMPIONSHIP

The tournament proved to be an outstanding success, not least because of the runaway success of local Amateur Josh Geary. While Josh was best known as a member of the Mt. Maunganui Golf Club as a boy he was also a member both of Otumoetai and of Tauranga. In 2001, still a schoolboy, he featured in the final of our senior championship where he went down narrowly to the experienced Gavin Smillie. Geary played superlative golf throughout the Carrus Open apart from one hole during the third round when he struck all sorts of trouble on the long 7th hole to record a ten, his first double figure score since his first tournament start as an eleven year old.

Cruising well clear of Tauranga’s Kevin Smith prior to this Geary yielded no less than seven strokes of his lead as Smith eagled the same hole. It was a mark of Geary’s temperament and the quality of his game that he was still able to complete the round in 70. With par surprisingly retained at 71 for the event Geary completed the tournament a nominal 22 shots under par. (Traditionally the 17th hole, never a genuine par 5, is reclassified as a par 4 during Professional events). None-the-less rounds of 63, 63, 70, and 66 represented golf of the highest quality.

BIRDS

One evening I was walking home in the gathering darkness when I saw what appeared to be one of those long-necked driver covers lying on the fifteenth fairway. As I bent to pick it up it moved away. It was the Golden Amhurst pheasant that lived on the course for a number of years, a very beautiful bird and a pleasure to see. The unusual blackbirds with white markings that are seen near the clubhouse have been with us for many ‘bird generations’. The rare genetic sport causing this has not been in evidence elsewhere to my knowledge. Another bird that attracted attention briefly was ‘Morrie Minor’, a very friendly mynah bird that was soon captured and sent off to an aviary somewhere.

This was rather sad as he was becoming quite a character on the course. Members welcome the annual visit by many Tuis that enjoy the nectar from Japanese flowering cherries. Unfortunately those same trees are becoming a serious weed in the countryside as the tuis spread the seed far from the original trees..
