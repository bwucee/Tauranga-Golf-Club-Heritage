Knowing Alan it can be certain that his reward came from the satisfaction of knowing that the course was so much the better upon the completion of the program. The changes made under the Harris plan were not major, nor particularly dramatic. Commander Harris recognised the course for what it is, an ideal club course offering variety and challenge to the vast majority of those who play it. He did not see it as a championship course as it lacked both the length, and the potential to extend that length, to achieve championship status. Consequently his suggested changes involved defining preferred lines of play for optimum results and to assist players to ‘navigate’ their way around the course.

This was achieved by incorporating subtle bunkering and mounding on many of the existing holes. These changes were nowhere major, nor unduly expensive, yet there is no doubt that adoption of the Harris plan had a major, and lasting, influence on the level of enjoyment experienced by members and visitors alike when playing the course.

Commander Harris, in association with Michael Wolveridge and Peter Thomson, developed Wairakei to be arguably the best course in the country at that time and today it remains in the very top echelon of New Zealand courses. Commander Harris was involved in the development of some thirty courses worldwide and Tauranga was most fortunate to obtain his expert advice. It is perhaps regrettable that subsequent administrations have, for a variety of reasons, seen fit to nullify some of the improvements made as a result of his visit. Fortunately this tendency has been relatively limited and the course continues to enjoy many of the Harris subtleties incorporated in those years. As shown by many an International course sound design survives the test of time even in the face of technological advances.

While the super stars may require greater length and difficulty in the face of these advances golf offers challenge enough to the vast majority of players on the courses they play on a regular basis. Improvement in the quality of playing conditions offered on courses such as our own will do more to enhance the golfing experience for the average club member than will continued fine tuning of design. Improvement in playing conditions has been made possible through the development of machinery specifically designed for golf course maintenance, through research into the soil environment, disease control, turf species, water application, and many other aspects of turf management. In particular the comprehensive training of staff has enabled them to make the best use of these advances.

Tauranga has been fortunate to have all these factors adopted with skill and enthusiasm by our staff over many years.

Public interest in golf surged in New Zealand in the Sixties and Seventies driven by such television series as Big Three Golf and Challenge Golf featuring Arnold Palmer, Gary Player, Jack Nicklaus, and other greats. This was before the days of Sky TV and the public had long been able only to read of the exploits of such giants of the game. Now they were able to see them in action and people from all walks of life were keen to see if they could emulate some of the inspiring golf they were able to watch in their living rooms each week. As a result clubs around the country welcomed a surge in membership and the pattern of play changed so that courses had to stand up to more concentrated play and to an extended season that soon stretched to a full twelve months as new members became full time golfers rather than going off to bowls, cricket, boating, or other activities in summer.
