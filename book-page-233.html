1957 - BIG TED ENTERS THE SCENE

E J (Ted) McDougall, then of Pukekohe, was to become a regular visitor to Tauranga over many years, both as an amateur and as a professional. He was just 19 when he won the Lionel Smith Rose Bowl as leading amateur in the 1957 tournament. Born in Scotland Ted was to become an outstanding competitor at every level of New Zealand golf. Following his Tauranga victory he went on to win the National amateur championship later in the season. As Jones had done the previous Invitation Tournament McDougall put the professionals in the shade as he improved on each of his rounds carding 73, 71, and a final 67 to lead Ross Newdick, later to win a PGA title at Mt Maunganui, by five strokes.

Ernie Southerden headed the Professional field one stroke behind Newdick with Buckler another stroke adrift. Stuart Jones honoured his pledge of the year before to return to Tauranga but an uncharacteristic 81 in his opening round put paid to any chance of him successfully defending his title. In accepting his prize from club president Warwick Nicoll Mr McDougall congratulated the committee on the course. The greens, he said, were much superior to any in Auckland.

BUCKLER AGAIN IN 1958

The 1958 tournament was notable for Frank Buckler’s third win in the six tournaments staged to that date and for the victory of W W Smith in the amateur section. WW had played in each tournament since inception without winning the Lionel Smith Rose Bowl, the major trophy for amateurs. Scoring was not up to the standard set in earlier years with Smith averaging 72s and Buckler one stroke worse on 217.

The 16 year-old Aucklander W J (Walter) Godfrey impressed many in the gallery. The BOP Times sports reporter of the day wrote in his tournament coverage that Godfrey’s easy swing indicated he would produce fine golf as he developed physically. How right his prophecy proved to be.

McDOUGALL REPEATS IN 1959 Now a member of the professional ranks Ted McDougall thrilled a large gallery in his first round by scoring a 67 after which he was never threatened, finishing nine strokes clear of veteran Alf Guy with a further stroke back to Frank Buckler in third position. In spite of this success so early in his professional life McDougall rejoined the amateur ranks less than two years later and went on to have a distinguished

career there, a second victory in the New Zealand amateur championship coming in 1970.

A C (Alf) Relph of Rotorua was a very popular winner of the Lionel Smith Rose Bowl finishing seven strokes clear of R A Court of New Plymouth. Alf Relph was a wonderful servant of Rotorua and Bay of Plenty Golf for many years representing each as a player and as an administrator. He is one of only four men to be honoured with Life Membership of the Bay of Plenty District Golf Association. Our own Jim Reid, who served as Captain and later President of our Club in the sixties, was one of the other three. Following the amalgamation of the administration of men’s golf with that of women the BOPDGA has now been re-incorporated as GolfBOP.
