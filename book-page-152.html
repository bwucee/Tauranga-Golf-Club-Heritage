In this he was certainly successful. He was instrumental in driving the building project that gave the Club an excellent clubhouse at a very competitive cost. His organisation of Cafeteria and Bar facilities, and his employment and supervision of the staff to run them, have provided members with outstanding service. The service provided by Jack and Jay Carter and their team in the Pro Shop has been a revelation. As each year goes by the golf course, the most important aspect of the Club, looks more and more the sort of course any golfer would be keen to play.

Green fee players invariably give the course the thumbs up. Yes, there will always be critics but members with open minds will have no difficulty recognising the progress that has been made over recent years. It is no coincidence that progress in terms of course and clubhouse facilities, staffing, and financial stability has been most pronounced since Grant Scoones was appointed in 1999. It was unfortunate that Grant decided he was not willing to extend his term of service beyond 2011. In his fourteen years as General Manager he achieved a great deal on behalf of the Club. He had proven to be the right man for the role at a time when it was vitally important that the Club move positively towards a sound financial future.

His legacy is likely to gain even greater recognition as we move forward from the base he established.

The new look Board that was installed in 2011 employed an outside consultant, Peter Dale, one time CEO of NZGolf, to advise them on an appropriate management structure to serve the Club’s future needs. It is too early to comment on the success or otherwise of the new directions the Board resolved to follow.

Jim Paterson told a few stories in his time. Some may have come from a lively imagination but there always seemed to be a ring of authenticity to them. Once he told us of a visiting American player who came into the shop one morning, paid his green fee, and spent some time discussing the characteristics of the different brands of balls Jim had available. After due consideration he settled upon a dozen Dunlop 65s and headed for the first tee. Jim went out to wave him on his way. After some limbering exercises and a few practise swings our man teed up and took a lusty swing, missing the ball by a narrow margin.

Two air shots later he stowed his club and ball in his bag, turned to Jim saying “It is not much of a golf course anyway” and headed back to the car park.
