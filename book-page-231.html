The professionals come to play

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 231 / 305Next page →

Alex Murray: “The course was 100% in all respects. The fact that not one golfer in the field broke 70 showed the course is neither too short nor too easy. Conditions were ideal, no wind nor rain, yet the best 54 hole total was three over par. The green keepers are to be congratulated on excellent presentation and I believe the course would be ideal for a National championship”.

Bob Jackson: “The best conditioned course I have ever played on. The bunkers too are the best conditioned I have seen in New Zealand. Their condition does not put a player in a hopeless position yet they do test his skill”.

Editor’s note: At the time of the 1953 tournament the sand in the bunkers was pumice sand that was dug from a pit on what is now the back of the fourth tee. When dry and stirred up to a fluffy state this material provided an excellent medium from which to play the old-fashioned explosion shot favoured by players of the time. After rain however it became hard packed and difficult from which to gain much control over recovery shots even for talented players. It was not until 1963 that Papamoa sand was tried on the course and today players are fortunate to have bunkers filled with quality river sand.

While players now may complain of bad lies and insufficient sand in bunkers I doubt they would appreciate a return to the pumice sand of those years. The heavier sand does require a different technique and the modern club player might be better rewarded by learning to use the correct approach rather than complaining about the state of the bunkers. All in all though it is worth remembering that bunkers are supposed to be hazards irrespective of what we see on our television screens.

TS Galloway (President NZPGA): “I saw the course first in 1924 and again in 1939. It has improved out of all sight. It is in my opinion one of the best country courses I have seen. The short fourth is a gem and the fifteenth one of the best par fours I have experienced”.

Basil Smith Jnr: “I have not seen a course more perfectly grassed. I have not heard one adverse comment regarding the course nor any part of the tournament. The Club is to be congratulated not only on the fine course, which is well up to National Championship standard, but also on a very fine tournament”.

TP Jones: “It is a grand course in every respect and, having won, I think it is just perfect. It is my first visit but I will be back”.

From the above comments it is clear that the staff and administrators of the time were getting many things right and the professionals appreciated the effort made to offer them an opportunity to play for a prize purse and for the opportunity to meet with their colleagues in convivial surroundings.

ALEX SHATTERS THE COURSE RECORD
