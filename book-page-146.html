Town water supply would be prohibitively expensive. Bore water availability is questionable at best, and expensive. The Racing Club bored to 1500ft to get 75cubic metres per day at a temperature of 40ºC.

Reticulation Cost estimate for full irrigation to fairways was $446 000 plus GST or $500 000 if the greens and tees reticulation was to be upgraded in conjunction. Maintenance costs Current maintenance costs on the two Windsor Green fairways was approximately $3500/ha/annum. The other fairways with travelling irrigators cost approximately $3000/ha/annum. Over time this difference would disappear as the cost of controlling Poa annua on the Windsor Green fairways reduced. Extra cost of maintaining irrigated fairways Irrigated fairway does not mean perfect fairway. Additionally there is a cost spiral involving extra fertiliser, extra use of fungicides and insecticides, extra system maintenance and extra mowing. This extra mowing means an increase in costs through additional fuel, staffing, and machinery maintenance and replacement costs.

Without major drainage and regular renovation work the fairways would become very wet in winter. All this meant the additional costs associated with irrigated fairways were conservatively estimated at $40 000 per annum plus another staff member at $25 000 minimum. This estimate was in line with what had been found at other courses. Comparative playing surface Compared to the existing fairways Windsor Green is

1) Far superior from January to April, 2) Superior in May, June, November and December, 3) Similar from July through to October.

Their conclusion Developing Windsor Green is a far superior strategy for the Tauranga Golf Club to adopt than that of irrigating existing fairways. It will be cheaper to introduce, no more costly to maintain, and will give

better playing surfaces longer term.

Faced with information such as this, and with the evidence developed from the first two trial fairways, the Board had no difficulty in adopting a policy to convert all fairways to Windsor Green (a Cynodon variety) as rapidly as resources would allow. While this variety does show winter dormancy in line with other warm weather grasses it still provides an adequate playing surface through the dormancy period. Over the following years improved techniques were developed to convert fairways using stolons harvested from our existing fairways.
