Resident professionals

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 170 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

In 1969 Allan got on a ship to visit relatives in New Zealand and has been in this country ever since. He immediately hit the headlines in New Zealand winning five important Amateur titles in that first year. His only failure came in a semi-final loss to Ted McDougall in the New Zealand Amateur Championship. These successes encouraged him to try the Professional game. Consequently he served a 12-month trainee period as required in order to gain entry to the NZPGA. In 1971 Allan played with moderate success in Asia and Europe. The highlight of this trip came when Allan played in, and made the cut, in the Centennial British Open.

Allan married in 1971 and held down two jobs while trying to play in as many tournaments as possible, managing to win some 14 Pro-Ams along the way. Success in the New Zealand Club Pro Championship came on two occasions.

The pressures of this life while supporting a family of three children proved too much and, in 1978, Allan accepted the professional position at the Flaxmere Club in Hawkes Bay. Though Flaxmere proved an enjoyable experience Tauranga appeared to offer better prospects and Allan took up the position here in 1980. Disaster struck in 1986 when a major fire swept through the Pro Shop and Trundler Shed destroying the shop and stock, together with some 256 sets of members clubs. The Club held a special golf day raising substantial revenue that was presented to the Snape family to help Allan get re-established.

In 1988 Allan resigned his Tauranga position and became attached to both Omanu and the Western Bay (Omokoroa) Clubs. He also established a coaching circuit through the Coromandel, setting up shop at Whangamata through holiday periods. Allan was instrumental in getting the Golf Range developed off the end of the Omanu course. This was a private undertaking involving some business partners of Allan’s.

Some years later Allan was diagnosed with Prostate cancer. This ultimately spread to his spine. Despite this Allan determined to get back to playing golf as soon as possible. Sheer determination saw him graduate from walking with a frame, to doing so with two sticks, then unaided. Eventually he was able to stand to hit a few balls. Finally he managed to play 18 holes, even carrying a light bag to do so. By the end of 2009 deterioration of the spine was so bad that Allan lost the strength to remain on his feet and life in a wheelchair became the only option. This was a sad final phase in the life of a talented sportsman who never lost his zest for life.

Jeff Macnab must hold the record for holes in one scored on our course. During his years as a member Jeff scored five of his seven lifetime aces on our course. An excellent golfer who played representative golf at Provincial level Jeff learned to play in Scotland so he was well equipped to handle those windy days that severely test most players. Jeff may well have become known as the peripatetic golfer as he was at various times a member of Omokoroa, Omanu, and Mt Maunganui Golf Clubs in addition to his membership atTauranga. Jeff served several years as a member of our committee during the Eighties including a term as Vice- Captain.
