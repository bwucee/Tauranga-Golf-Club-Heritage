For these reasons the Board of Management, early this Century, initiated the development of “Windsor Green” fairways. The decision to do so was made in consultation with David Ormsby of the New Zealand Sports Turf Institute. Other Cynodon species were considered before the decision to go with Windsor Green was made. Properly managed this grass thrives under hot dry conditions without the need for any applied water. Windsor Green has obvious faults, chief of which is the period of dormancy it undergoes in winter. Even then it provides a very playable surface, something that could not be said of the drought stricken fairways encountered in many a previous summer.

The wisdom of the policy to convert our fairways becomes more and more apparent as the years go by.

The first trial introduction of Windsor Green was made in 2000 when it was incorporated into the 11th and 13th fairways as a trial. The fairways were first sprayed with Roundup to eliminate all existing growth. The contracted supplier used a machine that simultaneously cut grooves, shredded the turf, and laid the shredded material in the grooves. This was an expensive exercise, the price to the contractor alone being $17 000, while our own staff spent many hours ensuring the implants had the best chance of survival. Conditions for growth that first summer were far from ideal with the result that those who were promoting the development of these new fairways had some anxious moments as the material struggled to become established.

The doubters, of whom there were many, claimed the Executive had made a serious error of judgement. Fortunately conditions improved and a solid carpet of turf was finally achieved. That was not the end of the doubts however as the staff had to learn how to manage the new turf to achieve the best playing conditions and to ensure these were maintained through the growing season. Fortunately Brendan Allen was enthusiastic about the new turf and developed a management program that was successful in achieving these ends. Cynodon species, and Windsor Green in particular, have an extremely vigorous and robust growth form and require quite severe mechanical treatment to achieve ideal fairway conditions.

This meant increased mowing while regular grooving had to be implemented in order to maintain turf vigour. These requirements increased the demands upon staff resources, a factor partially offset by lower needs for fairway watering.

It was Brendan’s belief that the groovings from established fairways could be used to spread the Windsor Green to other fairways. Consequently he piled up large stacks of these with the intention of trialling this technique. Unfortunately some members of the Board were not totally convinced that the policy of introducing Windsor Green throughout the fairways had yet proven to be the correct option for

the future. While they debated the issue the stored material deteriorated and became useless much to Brendan’s disappointment. It was not until after Brendan’s departure that a trial was finally undertaken, the second fairway, and part of the sixth, being “seeded” with groovings in 2005. Though establishment was slow much was learnt from the trial and the final result was successful following a second season of growth.
