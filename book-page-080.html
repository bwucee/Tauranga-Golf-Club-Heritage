JARED PENDER

Jared is the son of Jim Pender, a racehorse trainer based on the course next door. Jared spent much of his time on the adjacent fairways while growing up. An independent personality Jared seemed reluctant to accept advice during his early years and perhaps this hindered his golfing development. As he matured and his game developed to a standard where he became an automatic selection in Bay of Plenty teams the difficulties of the past were left well behind. A strong young man with an excellent swing and the ability to hit the ball a very long way Jared earned New Zealand selection in the Eisenhower team that played in Melbourne in 2008.

Soon afterward he turned Professional but, like Eddie Burgess before him, he found opportunities hard to come by. The developing American players have a huge advantage in being able to temper their games in the fiery cauldron of competitive College Golf. Our own young players might well consider the advantages of emulating Sir Bob Charles and getting much more experience courtesy of New Zealand Golf before attempting the move to Professional competition.

KEVIN SMITH

Kevin was already a low handicap player when he moved here from England. During his time in Tauranga he experienced success when representing both his club and his province before attracting the attention of the National selectors and consequently being selected to represent New Zealand at senior level. He featured with distinction on a number of occasions when playing the Carrus Tournament against the professionals and leading amateurs on his home course. Kevin moved to Melbourne early

in the new century.

Ron Holden was popular around the Club over many years. His standard stroke stayed close to the ground and moved in a comparatively sharp curve to the left. To see him start the ball out some 45 degrees right of the target line and have it curve back to the green was a sight to treasure. On occasion the ball refused to swing back to the left and that led to some fine entertainment for Ron’s playing partners. In the days when the practice range to the right of the 4th hole was still a scrub-covered swamp this would occasionally happen to Ron’s tee stroke on that hole. To see a ball aimed for the middle of the swamp sail unerringly there was enough to reduce even the kindest of companions to helpless laughter.

It was always best to have the honour there in case that should happen. Ron is the only player known to have hit a ball out of bounds into the Bus Depot when playing the 10th hole. On that occasion his low flying tee shot struck the Ladies’ tee marker, flew straight back past him, ran right across the 8th fairway and through the fence.
