1903–1906

A doctor, four clubs, and a beginning

Dr W. M. Brown brought an enthusiasm for golf to Tauranga. Within a year of his arrival, a club was taking shape.

Brown arrived from Dunedin in 1903. The book identifies a Bay of Plenty Times report of 20 April 1904 as the earliest written evidence of a golf club being formed in Tauranga. That distinction matters: 1903 begins this collection, while 1904 is the foundation year supported by the book.

By May 1905, the newspaper was reporting Brown’s re-election as captain and J. Bull’s re-election as secretary. The re-elections are another indication that this was already an established organisation.

The history recalls Brown arriving in a two-wheel gig driven by Arthur Faulkner. His bag held just four hickory-shafted clubs: a driver/brassie, cleek, lofter and putter. Ossie Daines carried them for sixpence. These details come from recollections assembled in the book, rather than surviving club minutes.

In June 1906, members of the golf and other local clubs joined a farewell dinner for Brown at the Masonic Hotel. His stay was short, but the game he helped establish endured.
