Late to the game he became a true enthusiast claiming to find a new secret, and even a new swing, each time he went out to hit practise balls. Henry served not only as assistant to Royce but also at one stage took on the job of Secretary to the BOPDGA.

Those who worked with Royce will remember him with great respect and admiration. During his nearly 14 years as Manager the Club was involved in a high level of activity. On the competition side the Men’s North Island Championship and the Women’s Inter Provincial (Russell Grace) Tournament were each held. These were additional to the regular Amateur tournaments, and to the Spalding Masters and PGA Tournaments. Major clubhouse extensions were undertaken and irrigation installed to tees and greens. Water was sourced from the Kopurereroa stream for the first time. Social activity reached a peak with Twilight Tournaments and Friday night meals proving popular. The pool table was at the height of its popularity, which added to the conviviality throughout the Club.

Bar trading remained outside the law and application had to be made for a new Booth license to cover each special occasion. Under cover of this a large supply of liquor would be purchased to meet future needs up to a year ahead.

The third member of the ‘Great Triumvirate’ was appointed in 1998. Grant Scoones served in the New Zealand Air Force before taking on Sports Management. Before his appointment to Tauranga Grant managed the Port Nicholson Yacht Club and was CEO of Squash New Zealand. Grant was the first to truly serve the management role in the Club. He had the vision to see what needed to be done in order to set the Club on a sound financial base and the drive to lead the Board towards that goal. The major weakness in the financial management of the Club had been a failure to set subscriptions sufficiently high enough to enable course and clubhouse development to progress at the level most members would consider desirable.

From the very beginning subscriptions had been set at a level to cover little more than annual running costs. No provision had been made for machinery replacement or course improvement. Staffing was kept at minimal levels. Any expenditure beyond this had to be borrowed against the hope that future members would eventually repay the debt. Whilst it is perfectly reasonable to expect future members to cover a share of development costs it is not reasonable to expect them to cover daily operating costs. This failure to maintain subscriptions at such a level as to cover annual operating costs and also provide for the replacement of plant and machinery had placed a burden upon the Club’s resources throughout much of its history.

Many an AGM featured long discussions on the question of subscription increases. Successive committees were frustrated as they presented thoroughly researched documentation to justify subscription increases that would enable them to meet

current costs and also put aside funds for the future only to have these voted down or substantially reduced. The 1970 AGM will probably best be remembered for an amendment by Mr Fookes to the motion calling for a $5 increase in subscriptions. The amendment was to limit all subscription increases to a maximum of $3. The said gentleman (a banker) was thereafter known as “Three dollar Fookes”. The Club still suffered under the same burden of debt resultant upon this type of thinking twenty-three years later. The 1993 AGM received a proposal from the committee to increase subscriptions by $20 per member. It was claimed from the floor that adopting this proposal would result in a serious fall in membership.
