A decision was made to trial Windsor Green on the 11th and 13th fairways and the contractor supplied and planted stolons into these in November 2001. This experimental planting was made possible by a grant of $25 000 from Renner’s Bar and Pub Charity. Members of the Board waited anxiously as the new turf slowly established itself and spread across the bare ground. Meanwhile Club members were dubious about the prospects for the turf and unhappy with the inconvenience of lifting balls from the developing areas. Many were critical and vociferous in their claims that the Board had made a very unwise decision.

The eventual success of the fairway conversion program will be told later in this story.

Following the AGM of 2001 the new Board consisted of elected representatives David Mowat and Bob Birch, invited members Margot McCool and Richard Cashmore, President Peg Copestake, and Player Representative John Roberts. It was noted that the objective of increasing membership had seen some degree of success with total membership increasing from a low of 855 in 1997 to a healthier 1109 in 2002. These figures do not tell the full story though as senior membership, not total membership, is the important factor in relation to Club finances.

The Board determined that it was not logical that the President should continue to chair General Meetings of the Club as the Board were the body responsible for the Financial and Administrative functions of the Club and consequently the Board Chairperson was best positioned to conduct such meetings. The Club adopted this policy.

David Mowat completed his three years as Board Chairman in November 2002 and was replaced by Richard Cashmore. David was elected to the Presidency and so remained on the Board. Bruce Calderwood and Tony Reynish were newly elected Board members. One of this Board’s first responsibilities was to appoint a Greens Superintendent to replace Murray Clark who had decided to step down in order to reduce his responsibilities. Brendan Allen was appointed and he made an immediate and significant impact in the role. It was unfortunate that he was induced to move to bigger things in Auckland before he had time to fully develop his ideas for our course.

The Board had long recognised the difficulties that were associated with the Financial and Membership years being significantly different. Consequently plans were made to align these in order to facilitate budgeting and other issues of financial management. Changes were adopted by the AGM of 2003 with the result that the financial year now concludes on January 31st with the AGM required to be held before

April 30th. The opportunity was taken at this meeting to present a major upgrade of Club rules. This was adopted without serious opposition.

During the period of this Board a number of major alterations to the course were made. The 5th, 7th, 10th, 13th and 17th holes were altered significantly, while minor alterations were made elsewhere. Opinion on the value of these to the playing quality of the course will differ from one player to the next but it would be true to say that not all such alterations have been widely accepted as being positive steps forward.
