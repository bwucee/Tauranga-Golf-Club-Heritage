1962–2005

From record books to card readers

Handicapping became a less visible but increasingly technical part of keeping competition fair.

The book contrasts early discretionary systems with the record-keeping required after the national adoption of a USGA-based system for men in 1962. Arnold Shanks maintained large quantities of cards and handwritten records alongside his other club duties.

Dave discusses the move to Slope in September 2000 and the practical task of comparing performance across courses. These passages reflect the rules and systems then in use.

The chapter also records an early computer demonstration by Tauranga Boys’ College staff and students and, later, approval of Golf Autoscore in 2005.

It is a history of how the club managed the task, not a guide to the current handicap system. The full chapter preserves the examples, disagreements and personal recollections that give the subject its local character.
