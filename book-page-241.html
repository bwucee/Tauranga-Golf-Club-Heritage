The Spalding Masters and the NZPGA Championship

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

The 1965 Tournament proved to be the last of the Invitation Tournaments to be held at Tauranga but by no means did it herald the end of quality Professional and Amateur tournaments at the club. We were soon to see even bigger and brighter stars on show thanks to the initiative of local businessman R A (Bob) Owens; later Sir Bob. In 1965 Mr Owens had been instrumental in establishing the NZPGA Championship at Mount Maunganui. Prior to that time the Professional Championship of New Zealand was run by the New Zealand Golf Association in conjunction with their Open Championship and, like the Amateur Championship, it was a match play event following the Open.

The Professional Championship was not played in 1964 and Mr Owens saw the opportunity to promote the Tauranga area, his company, and the game of golf. Once he settled upon a course of action Mr Owens was the type of man who made certain that a positive outcome ensued and the first STARS Travel (Stevedores Travel and Reservation Service) NZPGA Championship was held before enthusiastic, beach dressed, galleries at the height of summer in January 1965.

During these years a fledgling New Zealand Golf Circuit had developed with sponsored events being played at courses throughout the country. The committee at Tauranga felt it would be wise to drop the June/July Invitation Tournament and concentrate their efforts on becoming part of that circuit by promoting an event in January while visiting Professionals from New Zealand and Overseas were in the district. It was therefore fortuitous that in April 1967 the Spalding Company, then possibly the most important sporting goods company serving New Zealand, approached Tauranga with a view to staging the Spalding Masters as a pre-curser to the NZPGA at the Mount. The initiative Tauranga had shown in offering competition and prize money for the Professionals in earlier years when very little was provided for them undoubtedly had much to do with the approach now being made.

As was to be expected Tauranga enthusiastically accepted the Spalding offer and the Tournament was scheduled for the 8th/9th of January 1968 over 54 holes for prize money of £1000. The Spalding Company put up £750 while the club decided to put in a further £250 so as to make the purse appear so much more attractive. This was considered an excellent purse at the time especially as the Professional purse for the Invitation Tournament had been only £200. Prior to the tournament New Zealand adopted decimal currency so the field ultimately played for total prize money of $2000. This was sufficient to attract a quality field headed by reigning PGA champion of Australia E W (Billy) Dunk.

The first day of the tournament saw a spate of sub par scoring which resulted in the accepted course record of 67 set by Walter Godfrey in 1964 being bettered no less than five times during the day before the 20 year old John Lister of Timaru settled the issue with a 64 towards the end of the day. There will always be debate about what constitutes a course record and many will feel as I do that this 64 only equalled the 64 scored by Alex Murray fourteen years previously. In the intervening period the course had been altered by siting the tee at the par three fifth to the South so the hole paralleled the seventh fairway and was some 30 metres longer.

How much more difficult this made the course is impossible to measure, but since 64 was to be bettered within twelve months the question did not remain relevant for very long. On the more general question of course records it could be argued that they belong in the same genera as the fastest times in Marathons. There is no record for a Marathon as courses differ so much from one to another. In the same way Golf Courses do not offer the same conditions from one day to the next.
