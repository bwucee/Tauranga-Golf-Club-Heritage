NUMBER 16 - PYES PA - PAR 3

Like the fifteenth this hole was developed to replace those made obsolete following the move from the oval. It has stood the test of time and remains challenge enough for club players of all levels of ability. That test is precisely what we should look for when considering hole design on a course such as our own.

NUMBER 17 - WESTWARD HO - PAR 5

As stated earlier this hole, and the eighteenth, are essentially unchanged and are still played as the same numbered holes as they were in the original layout. The seventeenth has undergone a number of remakes over the years and I do not doubt that some future committee will make another some day. Committees over the years have not been able to agree on whether it warrants the label par 4 or par 5 for men. In its earlier years, given the equipment of the day, even quality players found difficulty reaching the green in two strokes. Now they achieve this with ease. It remains below the length normally defined as being the upper limit for par fours.

NUMBER 18 - MORETON BAY - PAR 4

The tree from which the hole derives its name has created controversy from time to time because of the difficulties it does create for clubhouse maintenance. In this case the pluses appear to exceed the minuses and the tree will likely be there for many a year to come. The hole has changed little over the years and the first real clubhouse was built behind this green to give an excellent view down the length of the hole.

The old stables that once stood to the left of the eleventh hole often came into play and something was lost from the character of the hole when they were demolished. The new stables are set a little further back from our boundary and do not have the same physical or psychological presence as did their predecessors. Many were the tee shots that found their way back to the fairway as a result of striking the roof or walls of the original buildings. One windy day a player was expecting just such a result as his ‘duck hook’ streaked towards a stable door. Just as the ball was about to strike the door a gust of wind blew the door open and the ball disappeared inside.

To add insult to injury the door then blew shut as if to ensure the ball remained securely swallowed. Such are the vicissitudes of this game of golf.
