This proposal was agreed to and, although the five day tournament is now consigned to history, the Vets have continued to support the Club through a wide variety of projects and services right through to the present day.

Peter Steward warrants special appreciation for his immaculate calligraphy and fine photography that grace the pages of the album from its birth in 1993 until 2004 when Peter’s failing eyesight made it impossible for him to continue the work. His back-dating of the album over the 20 years prior to 1993 must have required many hours of meticulous work before the album was ready for presentation to the Society. It is to be hoped that the album will be updated from 2004, and continue to be maintained as an invaluable record of the activities and administration of the society.

While many individual veteran members had provided voluntary services to the Club over earlier years it was not until 1985 that official projects began as a routine part of the Society’s year. In that year the following projects were successfully undertaken under the leadership of President Phill Johnston: • Provision of a new Golf Club flag • Overhaul and maintenance of the ball washers on the tees • Improvements to the first tee surrounds

• Repainting of the Men’s locker room • Stripping and repainting the walls in the Dining Room.

Phill Johnston, a fine sportsman who played both golf and tennis until very late in life, probably holds the record for the longest continual membership of Tauranga Golf Club. This was commemorated through the planting of a Pohutukawa tree to the left of the 14th green. An accompanying plaque records that Phill was a member from 1936 until 1996.

In 1986 President Wally Calver saw the following projects to fruition:

• Construction of a brick entranceway adjacent to the 10th green. This was to commemorate the 75th anniversary of the Club. The bricklaying expertise of Andy Graham was especially valuable in this undertaking, the bricks being chosen to blend with those featured on the Club House at the time. Unfortunately those bricks are no longer discernible, having been plastered over presumably to match the Club House. • Replacing stolen and vandalised bunker rakes. • Painting 11th tee shelter. • Painting trundler shed interior. • Assisting with sweeping and removing cores from greens. This assistance continued twice yearly until 2008 when machinery was hired to do the job. Walter Tombleson and Peter Baveystock come to mind as two who participated in this work at virtually every coring throughout the period.

In 1987 under President Noel Payne the Society:

• Provided the Club with a Walkie Talkie set to enable communication with the starter on tee 11. • Rebuilt the surrounds of the first tee.
