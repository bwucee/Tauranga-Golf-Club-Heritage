JAY CARTER AND GOLF FUSION

Jay began as a junior at the Tauranga Club while still a schoolboy. He developed into a player of considerable promise and, upon leaving school, spent time in Wellington developing his game as a full time student under Mal Tongue. When the Professional contract was up for renewal at Tauranga Jay and his father decided to put in an application to run the business. The Board had made it a priority to upgrade the Pro Shop and now advertised a contract to operate the business. This contract extended the role of the Professional to an extent that had not previously been envisaged.

When the contract was awarded to the Carter team Jay was not then a member of the PGA. In order for him to become a member it was required that he serve his time under a full member. Doug Milne, previously Professional to the Whakatane Golf Club was therefore employed as the PGA Professional. This arrangement remained in place until Jay graduated to full membership of the PGA.

Members were soon able to appreciate the new services introduced by Golf Fusion as the business is known. A fleet of new Golf Carts arrived and were available for hire by members and visitors alike, the new look Pro Shop was soon stocked with a wide range of equipment, lessons were readily available, junior clinics were developed, and the Pro Shop staff acted as starters to get the field efficiently on its way. Buckets of practice balls were made available for use on the Driving Range and later the Card

Issuing machine was located in the Pro Shop for easy access by members. Staff would even accept bookings and cancellations as part of the service offered. Manufacturer demonstrations became a regular feature of the service and many a member took the opportunity to try out new clubs to see what these might do for their game.

The story of Jay Carter and Golf Fusion is still unfolding at time of writing though there is no doubt the concept has changed the nature of the services offered to our members.

Playing to your age is an achievement any golfer would be proud of. This feat is becoming more common as more players live on into their older years in robust good health. Prominent members of our Veterans Society, Pat Kendall and Clive Sheerin, maintained quite a competition between themselves as they seemingly vied to outdo the other in adding to their total number of rounds to, or under, their age. Neither of these redoubtable proponents of the game however was able to match the feat of another of the Society, George Reilly. George must have established the record for our Club as the youngest to achieve the feat when he returned a 67 at that age.

Peg Copestake, to the best of our knowledge remains the only woman member to have achieved the feat.
