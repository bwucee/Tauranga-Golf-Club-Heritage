An intriguing battle developed for second place worth a healthy $3560. Roger Davis prevailed, his 68 giving him a total of 267 that would have been good enough to win many a tournament. A pair of 65s in the final rounds catapulted Billy Dunk up the leader board for a share of third place with Simon Owen. John Lister went one better over those final rounds with a 65 and a 64. Rick Barker dropped a couple of strokes but still completed a very strong tournament at five under.

Gillian Bannan, now a full member of the NZPGA, achieved a notable first by completing four rounds playing off the same tees as the men. Her total of 34 over par reflected the difficulty women would always face in competing against men on equal terms.

THE 1981 NZPGA CHAMPIONSHIP

That year the Tournament attracted a field of 144 drawn from Australia (47), The United States (22), The United Kingdom (5), Japan (3), Canada (2), and New Zealand. Prize Money had now increased to $50 000 with a top prize of $9000. Though one $5000 sponsor failed to “hole out” the prizemoney and the total entry both represented records for a PGA Tournament in New Zealand. The change from the traditional New Year dates to late November to tie in with the NZ Open at Titirangi would have had much to do with the superb field attracted to the tournament.

The Pro/Am on the Wednesday gave a good indication of the sort of scoring that was to follow. Terry Gale of Australia led with a 63, three others shot 64, and four made 65s. As an indication of the quality of the field 25 players were on 68 or better.

While Terry Hulls, a 23 year old from Australia, replicated his Countryman’s 63 of the previous day, the first round highlight was credited to a young New Zealand professional from North Shore, John Reid. Reid ended the day on what would prove to be a very ordinary score of 70, but he came away the proud owner of a sparkling new Honda Civic valued at $13 200, compliments of his hole in one at the 14th hole. John used a nine iron for this perfect stroke. Such a club choice would be more in keeping with the modern game as now seen on the USPGA Tour rather than that of 1981.

This was but one of an unprecedented string of aces scored during the week. Barry Vivian set the trend during the Pro/Am by acing the Knoll, while on Saturday Bob Charles went into the lead when acing the fifth. Moments later Terry Gale completed the quartet when his tee shot found the cup at the ninth hole.

Terry Hull’s 63 included an eagle and seven birdies while New Zealand hopes, Bob Charles and John Lister, were well placed on 65. The hot scoring continued on the Friday with the cut being made at one over par 141. Notable players to miss the cut included Simon Owen, Jack Newton and Wayne Grady, a further indication of the quality of the field. John Lister scored successive 65s yet complained of a bad break at the Knoll (who hasn’t on occasion?). His nine iron landed well up on the green, or so he claimed, before “sinking back” into the bunker. Ted Ball, the Australian credited with the fastest swing in golf, matched Lister’s paired 65s, while Charles was just one stroke off the pace.
