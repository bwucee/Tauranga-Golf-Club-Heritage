Once again Brenda had fallen just short in her desire to win this tournament, a desire even stronger now that the tournament had been dedicated to the memory of a woman whose golf had been an inspiration to her.

A highlight of the tournament from a local aspect was the hole in one scored by Noeline Grant at the fifth hole during Sunday play.

1986 The quality that has for so long been displayed in this tournament was emphasised this year when the semi-finals featured four of the very best players in the country. The Auckland pair of Karrin Duckworth and Marnie Maguire were shortly off to Asia to represent New Zealand in the Queen Sirikit Tournament, while Bay of Plenty’s Jan Cooke was named non-travelling reserve for that team. Brenda Ormsby, now back in the Bay and based at Springfield, made up the semi-final group. Jan Cooke looked all over the winner against Duckworth only to lose the lead late in the day to go down at the 19th. Meanwhile Brenda Ormsby took an early 3 up lead against 16-year old Maguire and, though Maguire was able to square the match immediately after the turn, Ormsby pulled ahead again for a comfortable 2/1 victory to leave her poised to win her first Tauranga Una Wickham Open Championship.

Alas for Ormsby it was to be another defeat in her fourth final. It was Duckworth’s exceptional play on, and around, the greens that made the difference in the final. Four down at the turn it looked all over for Ormsby but she came back strongly to square the match with three holes to play only to see her opponent win two of those final three holes for an entertaining two up victory.

1987 There was a new look to the field this year with many of the name players of previous tournaments missing for one reason or another. The final had a real Hawkes Bay flavour to it with Karen de Lautour (ex H Bay, now Remuera) defeating Ingrid van Steenbergen (ex Tauranga, now Hawkes Bay) 3/2 after van Steenbergen had set the early pace. Prior to this tournament van Steenbergen, previously apprenticed to the Greens Staff at Tauranga, had been appointed Head Green Keeper at the Flaxmere course in Hawkes Bay. She was probably the first woman to be appointed to such a position in New Zealand.

1988 Ingrid van Steenbergen returned to have another attempt at the title that had eluded her the previous year. Her quest was successful when, in a close fought final, she defeated former National trialist Jan Malcolm (Lochiel) at the 19th. Local teenager Catherine Rodgers proved something of a giant killer as she eliminated Gay McMillan (Paeroa) and Jan Cooke (now Tauranga) on the way to a

semi-final loss to Jan Malcolm.

1989 Jan Higgens (nee Cooke), now of Manakau, defeated defending champion Ingrid van Steenbergen at the 19th, the same hole where van Steenbergen had won her title one year before. Both finalists had previously been members at Tauranga and both had represented New Zealand at Overseas tournaments in 1988 so there was a lot of local interest in the final. The crowd were not disappointed as the pair fought out a close match where not more than one hole separated them as they each played the course in not more than one over par, this from the blue tees as was always the case for the Championship group.
