At pennant level Eddie was an automatic choice in the strong and successful Championship Teams of the 1990s. At Schoolboy and Under 23 level he had an enviable record in local tournaments, winning both the Spalding Masters and Davidson Cup Match Play titles on more than one occasion, the former five times. In 1993 Eddie represented New Zealand at under-18 level versus Australia, having played in the Clare Hingston Trophy fixture in Christchurch the year before. In 2000 Eddie reached the pinnacle of his career by winning the New Zealand Amateur Championship. Soon afterwards he turned Professional with the intention of playing the Asian Circuit but was frustrated by a lack of opportunities.

Gaining tournament entries proved extremely difficult and Eddie eventually applied for reinstatement as an amateur.

JAY CARTER

Jay has been associated with the club from a young age. His father Jack has served both the Club and the Bay of Plenty Association in administrative roles while his Grandfather George was a popular member in an earlier age. After reaching high standards as a Junior that culminated in his selection in the New Zealand under 18 team to Australia in 1995 Jay spent a year developing his game under the tutelage of Mal Tongue at Manor Park in Wellington. He then returned to Tauranga where he and his father took on the contract to run the Pro Shop business at the Club. The business initially employed Doug Milne, previously professional at Whakatane, to allow Jay to serve his apprenticeship under a PGA Professional and so qualify as a fully-fledged PGA Professional himself.

The service provided by Jack, Jay, and their associates has been outstanding.

BRUCE BURBERY

Bruce joined the Club as a junior in the years before there was much encouragement to juniors from many senior members. Juniors were often regarded more as a nuisance than as an asset by a number of those members. With no background in the game Bruce gripped the club in a way that felt natural to him with his left hand below his right. With this grip he was soon able to hit the ball a very long way. The subtleties of the game seemed of little interest to him at that stage. When playing with his school friends he was mainly concerned with how far he could hit the tee shot.

After that he was inclined to head for the next tee, the finer arts of chipping and putting holding little interest for him. Once when playing with Ken Fraser, donor of the Fraser Cup for the Schoolboy Championship, and myself Bruce was persuaded to try the ‘proper’ right hand down grip as we attempted to convince him that there was little future for him in the game unless he changed. After trying the new grip with mixed success he

stood on the 18th tee saying: ‘I have had enough of that. I’ll hit one my way.’ With that he launched a drive to the foot of the hill, a very long drive in that era of the wound ball and laminated-wooden headed driver. We complimented his effort before suggesting he hit another ball using our suggested grip. He did so and the ball finished about 40m ahead of the first. From that day onward he stuck to the new grip and was rewarded with both length and accuracy.

Always a threat to anyone in Match Play Bruce was not to win the Senior Championship until the year 2000. He then claimed to have been trying to win it for 40 years. He was a very proud and deserving winner. He repeated that victory ten years later in 2010. A very valuable member of our Senior Pennant
