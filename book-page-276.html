The Una Wickham Memorial Tournament

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 276 / 305Next page →

Una Wickham, so often a major contributor to Bay of Plenty successes turned her talents to a spot of soothsaying prior to the start of tournament play. She predicted that the Waikato/King Country combination could well prove to be the dark horse of the tournament. Waikato number one Ruth Middleton indicated her intention to prove this prediction to be justified when she recorded six birdies in leading her team to a shock opening round victory over defending champions Auckland. This was Miss Middleton’s seventeenth Russell Grace and seldom had she played better golf. Four birdies in the first nine holes had the talented Gillian Bannan tottering yet Ruth had to birdie the 15th and 16th in order to prevail 3 and 2.

This match set the standard for much high quality play leading to many a hard fought match throughout the tournament.

Two Tauranga players, Miss Wickham and Mrs Kath Johnston were included in the BOP/TV team. The formidable Wellingtonians were despatched that first morning but the highly favoured Manawatu/ Wanganui team proved too strong in the afternoon thus diminishing hopes of a local victory. The BOP/ TV team finally finished a very credible fifth.

Miss Middleton led from the front in becoming the only number one to remain unbeaten throughout the tournament as her team continued on its winning way. On the final day Canterbury, the most likely team to deny Waikato/King Country victory, was locked in an exciting battle with BOP/TV. Una Wickham, playing her best golf of the tournament, defeated Canterbury number one Sue Boag but it was not enough as Canterbury scraped home by three matches to two. Canterbury could then only sit back and hope that their neighbours, South Canterbury, could topple Waikato/King Country. That was not to be and Waikato/ King Country had their name on the cup for the first time in their history.

They thoroughly deserved what was very much a team success. Each individual member had wins when they were needed and though Canterbury scored more individual wins by a margin of four Waikato took the title by eleven team points to ten.

The organization of the tournament had been excellent, the fine weather played its part, the course was in as good a condition as any player could wish, and much of the golf had been of a standard to entertain the most discerning of spectators. The only occurrence to cast a gloom over the tournament came at the end of the final day. An excellent gallery had followed the last few holes as the tension mounted to see which of the two leading teams would prevail. As a result a large gallery was assembled around the 18th green in the gathering gloom and there they remained in the expectation that the ceremonial presentation of the cup would follow very soon after the final putt was holed.

Alas it was not to be. The organisers representing the Club were ready, the Press was waiting, the spectators were expectant. Where were the LGU representatives? Finally someone tracked them down. For some reason known only to themselves they had found that particular time to be the most appropriate hour to cloister themselves in the privacy of the Ladies’ locker room to deliberate on the selection of teams for something known as the ‘Home Internationals’! By the time they emerged the crowd had departed for warmer destinations.
