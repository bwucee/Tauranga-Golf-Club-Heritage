Club President Noel Copestake and Player’s Representative Paul Munn joined them to form the nucleus of the new Board. In accordance with the new rules these four then invited Ian Cross and Jackie McDonald to fill the remaining positions Inaugural Chairman Bruce Partridge had a difficult two-year term as the Board strove to establish itself in a club environment that was not entirely supportive. In spite of the democratic process by which the Board came into being there were those who lamented the demise of the previous committee system.

To their mind the Board had too much power, the players and their committees too little. The Players Committee suggested they should act as the Greens Committee in addition to their other responsibilities. The roles of Board, Committee, and General Manager were clearly not yet established in the minds of all participants. Attitudes changed as successive Boards began to convince Club members that the Club was able to make real progress under the new management structure. The financial position, a problem for as long as anyone could remember, improved under Board management, the clubhouse was virtually rebuilt, the Pro Shop was enlarged and made more attractive and, most importantly, the course showed continual improvement in appearance and playing quality.

While some would claim these improvements would have been made equally quickly under the old committee system it is difficult to believe that to be true. Progress under the old system had been stalled for far too long. Aylene McGregor, who had done an excellent job in a role she had taken on with some reluctance, continued to act as Manager during 1995 though she was unwilling to take on the more onerous responsibilities associated with the role now envisaged for the General Manager. The Board subsequently appointed Chris Linn to fill the position. He took up the new role in October 1995. The work of the inaugural Board was made more difficult as a result of this selection as the new manager proved somewhat less than satisfactory and after three years a replacement was sought.

In December 1996 Bruce Partridge stood down from the Chair, citing pressure of work. Bruce remained on the Board until the AGM of November 97 when the Board elected Joyce Paris to fill the Chair. Jackie McDonald stood down in 1996 and was replaced by Barbara Glynn who, soon afterward, was appointed Chairperson. By November 1997 Barbara had a new look Board to Chair with Bruce Simister and David Mowat as the elected members, Michael Sandlant and Noelene Shiel as President and Player Representative respectively, while Barbara and Ian Cross continued as the invited members. The first task of the new Board was to appoint a General Manager to replace Chris Linn.

Barbara led the panel that recommended the appointment of Grant Scoones who took office in March 1998. The two proved to work very well together. Barbara was a strong Chairperson who established excellent procedures for future Boards to follow. At one stage of her term the Trustees of the Golf Club Trust were expressing doubts as to

the ability of the Board to run the affairs of the Club. These were powerful personalities who wanted to impose conditions upon the Board before they were willing to loosen the purse strings by which they held the Trust funds. As an accountant Barbara had sound financial skills and was able to approach these people and convince them that both she and the rest of the Board had both the wisdom and the expertise required to utilise the funds in a responsible manner. In the end the Trustees were pleased to wind up the Trust and make the funds available to the Board to be used in the Clubhouse rebuild.
