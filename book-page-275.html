2006 Preparations were made for the tournament in the usual manner that year but, sadly, the entries were not sufficient to make it viable to continue. The tournament appears to have been a victim of the times. More women were now committed to full-time work, the appeal of tournaments away from home was on the wane, and economic conditions seemed to have become more difficult for many potential participants. The women were not alone in these problems. The long established and previously highly popular Labour Weekend Tournament for men, featuring the Tauranga Open Title, had struggled to survive in the same era before finally fading into history. The sad realities of a more pressured world were exerting their influence over a multitude of activities.

Golf, including the social aspects of our game, was to be no exception to the trend. The demise of these tournaments could in no way be attributed to those charged with their organisation. Everything that could reasonably have been done to ensure their popularity and consequent continuation was done. The world had simply moved on.

THE RUSSELL GRACE TOURNAMENT

The most important Women’s tournament ever staged at Tauranga has been the 1973 Inter-Provincial Championship then known as the Russell Grace Inter-Provincials just as the equivalent Men’s tournament was known as the Freyberg. These tournaments actually involve competition between

teams representing the District Golf Associations rather than Provinces but what’s in a name? While sponsorship has led to the demise of these old respected names this has been a small price to pay for the very welcome benefits that sponsors have brought to the Championships.

Anna Brabyn has given us information relating to the history of the tournament and the Russell Grace Cup. Mrs Nancy Young donated the cup to the NZLGU in memory of her mother, Mrs Russell Grace, who was a talented Wanganui representative golfer and long time member of the LGU. It had been Wanganui that first suggested that a National Inter-Provincial Championship be held. Originally the teams competed on a Regional basis, the first final being held at Invercargill in 1949 when Auckland became the first name engraved on the cup. All Districts competed at the one venue for the first time at the Ngamotu course in New Plymouth in 1963.

Auckland was again successful though they had not dominated in the intervening years, this being their third victory. Bay of Plenty and Canterbury had been equally successful. Bay of Plenty/Thames Valley were again successful in 1983 at Heretaunga when the cup was awarded for the last time prior to the Tournament becoming known as the Stylemaster Inter-Provincial Championship. The Russell Grace Cup that somewhat incongruously had started life as a trophy awarded to the winner of a prestigious race at the Wanganui Racing Club was finally retired to a supposedly permanent home at the Wanganui Golf Club. The name and the cup were however resurrected to serve the New Zealand Masters Inter-Provincial Tournament first held at Methven in 1992.

Many competitors in that first tournament would have been well aware of the proud history of the trophy.

The 1973 Russell Grace Tournament was held in perfect autumn weather at our Gate Pa course. It has often been said that our course is at its best in the Autumn months and, with the extra work put into course preparation for the tournament, this certainly proved to be the case.
