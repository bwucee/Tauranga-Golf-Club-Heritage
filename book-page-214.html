At the beginning of the following year I headed back to University and it was not until three years later that I found myself back in Tauranga at something of a loose end. While deciding where my future lay I was asked to fill in once again on the course as the assistant had resigned and Grant needed some help. I was happy to do so for a short time though it turned out to be for most of the year. At some stage in the year Garth Sim, Principal of Tauranga Boys’ College and husband of Life Member Nen Sim, was playing on the course with my old First XV coach Pat Keenan.

Spotting me they stopped and wanted to know what I was doing mowing grass when they were short of Science and Mathematics teachers. They would be happy to recommend me to the recruiting officer who was to be in town the following week. I found out the money was not too bad so much to my surprise I soon found myself studying at Christchurch Training College. I had never had any inclination to be a teacher prior to that time and yet I signed on under Garth the following year and spent the next 31 years at the College. So ended my green keeping career but certainly not my interest in all things relating to the great game of golf.

A regular Friday afternoon four made up of three Businessmen and the Club Professional were inclined to play for a dollar or three. One of the Businessmen, who had something of a reputation for absentmindedness, was busy totting up the state of his financial obligations as the others teed off on the 15th. When they prepared to play their second strokes two balls were located on the 16th fairway, the Pro’s was well up the middle, while our man’s was nowhere to be seen. He was adamant that his tee shot had been ‘straight down the middle’. After some time of searching under leaves that had accumulated in the fairway he put his hand in his pocket and there was his ball.

‘What the hell is going on here’ he said as he strode back to the tee. On another occasion a group were following the same gentleman and were about to play to the third green when they realised there was no flag to play to. Eventually someone spotted the flag being carried in the ‘slope arms’ position up the path leading to the fourth tee.
