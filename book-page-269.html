The Una Wickham Memorial Tournament

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 269 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

1981 In spite of two New Zealand representatives being involved the final was a disappointing match. Neither Kingham nor Rhodes was able to show the form expected of them. It could be said, probably unkindly, that Kingham made fewer mistakes to prevail 2/1.

1982 The final featured the finalists from the previous year in Cherry Kingham and Brenda Rhodes. These two could now lay claim to being the best two women golfers in the country. Rhodes, who had held the National Championship for the previous two years, was again frustrated in her ambition to win this tournament. The match featured superb tee to green play by Rhodes in particular but some tentative putting by her allowed Kingham to stay in touch before finally taking control to win 2/1. Despite the excellence of much of the iron play only one birdie was scored in the entire match.

1983 Ruth Middleton (Matamata), now in the veteran class, was able to show the youngsters she still had plenty of quality to her game and probably caused provincial selectors some perturbation as she defeated three potential Bay of Plenty representatives on her way to the final against Jocelyn Queenan (Nee Glanfield). Ruth, a long time supporter of Tauranga tournaments, was a very popular winner of the final.

1984 This was the year that the tournament was first played under the title of The Una Wickham Open Match Play Tournament in memory of the Club’s greatest woman player. Former New Zealand Champion, Jan Arnold, won the tournament. defeating eighteen-year old Jan Cooke of Western Bay, now Omokoroa Golf Club, who played very well to be beaten just two down. Jan Cooke might well have been considered a prodigy of Miss Wickham as she had been pulled from relative obscurity by Una to fill the number five spot in the winning BOP Russell Grace team of 1983. A win by either finalist would have seemed a fitting tribute to the late Miss Wickham.

On the way to the final Miss Cooke had defeated two previous winners in Jocelyn Queenan and Alison Hasey to demonstrate her form and her potential.

1985 The tournament this year again featured Jan Arnold and Brenda Ormsby, the latter having reverted to her maiden name. This time the two came fresh from finishing second to the USA in the World Team Championship. Mary Laugerson (ex Wanganui) who was widely recognised as one of New Zealand’s most knowledgeable golf authorities claimed the two finalists to be the best New Zealand had ever produced. While other candidates for such an honour will spring to many a mind it has to be true that these two names would rank very highly in a New Zealand Golfing Hall of fame.

The dominance these two held over a quality field was amply demonstrated in the semi- finals where Arnold beat the Auckland number one, Karrin Duckworth 7/6, while Ormsby beat Bay of Plenty number one, Jane Little, 6/5.

The final featured quality golf with many birdie opportunities, a pleasing number of which were converted. Successive birdies at the fifth and sixth edged Arnold ahead and, although Ormsby squared the match a number of times, Arnold was never behind before winning just one up after Ormsby missed the final green with her approach. The winner was an approximate two under par at the close.
