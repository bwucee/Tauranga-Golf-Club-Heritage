It is always exciting to do something new. A temporary bar was set up in the back of the trundler shed. Fortunately there was hot water and wash up facilities but it was still pretty primitive, just a counter on trestles to serve from, a few tables each side of an old carpet runner, and enough chairs at a pinch. Knowing it was only a temporary inconvenience the arrangement was accepted in good spirit by members and there were a lot of happy times there. Jim Paterson was a great help to me at that time, always pleasant and willing to give a hand in tidying and cleaning up what was a less than ideal facility.

I did enjoy the company and the change of life style. It was an interesting job. The alterations went ahead on schedule and no one was greatly inconvenienced. The new caterers took over and I went home to the life I had been leading. At the Club all was settled and going smoothly, or so I imagined. For my part I know I missed the company. As part of the official opening of the new facilities there was a social evening and dinner in the clubhouse. I did not go but later heard it had not proved to be the success anticipated. The golf day had been a success and the dinner tables very nicely set up with several quite elaborate decorations.

Obviously the new husband and wife team had gone to a good deal of trouble to make a good initial impression. There was a big crowd but unfortunately the caterer was not familiar with hungry golfers. There were to be two servings but practically all the food disappeared at the first of these and many went home hungry and dissatisfied. It was a bad start but allowances had to be made for the new staff. They would improve. After all they had come with glowing credentials from an Australian firm. Things did improve for a time though there were doubts. The barman was slow with service and with giving change.

I think the members missed a familiar face behind the bar and someone they could tell their golfing woes to. One day, not too long after the opening, I had another ring from Noel Hockin. Could I come out to a committee meeting at the clubhouse that evening? The House Committee were there - Noel, Arthur Bluett, Owen Evans, Jim Reid, and Frank Warnes. The new caretaker couple were leaving and they would like me to take over the Bar and Clubhouse maintenance role. The job would be full time including running the bar and cleaning the clubhouse and changing rooms, a much bigger job than I had been doing before the alterations.

The committee had worked out a schedule of duties, hours of work, and pay. The hours were long and the pay not very high but I felt it would be interesting and pleasant working with people I knew. After giving the matter some thought I accepted. If it didn’t work out from my point of view or that of the committee I could always resign. At least I would have something to fill the

rather empty life that lay ahead. Everyone was pleased. The committee were free of the responsibility of advertising the job and all that would have entailed. It was intended I would start work the following Monday but even that did not go to plan. Whenever the previous barman required a day off during the week the gap had been filled by Bill Drummond, a retired engine driver. Bill was a nice quiet man who was to continue in that role on my days off. On the Friday prior to my official start I had a call from Noel. There had been an accident. From the bar storeroom there was a lift for bringing up full beer crates and other supplies and returning empties to ground level.
