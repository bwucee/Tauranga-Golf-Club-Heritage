For some time there had been agitation from some elements of the Greerton Community who claimed they were not able to derive much benefit from the ‘Park in their backyard’. This was in spite of the fact that many local residents did use the Reserve to jog, to walk, or to attend activities. A meeting of the combined committees of Golf and Racing, meeting under the auspices of the Domain Board, negotiated an agreement whereby the Golf Club would transfer the lease they had long held over McKinlay’s paddock and one quarter of the inner oval to the Racing Club. These areas, though in the Golf Club lease, had not been utilised by them since the days when the Golf course extended into the oval.

In return the Racing Club undertook to make facilities available to the public so far as was possible without interfering with their own activities. Consequently the Greerton Soccer (Football) Club was granted use of land within the oval where they developed two fields. These fields remained in use until the Club moved elsewhere in 2008. Picnic facilities were developed in McKinlay’s Paddock where specimen trees were planted to provide shade and generally beautify the area. In addition the Racing Club sub-leased an area within the oval to Equestrian Interests. These measures were successful to the extent that the interests of many appeared to be satisfied, and local agitation was no longer heard.

Fortunately the interests of Golf and Racing had prevailed and eventually the future of the Racecourse Reserve, and tenure in perpetuity of the Tauranga Golf Club upon it, were enshrined in legislation. Generations of the future could look forward to enjoying the game of golf on a course that had offered great playing conditions and a more than adequate challenge to

players of all levels of ability since early in the twentieth century.

The original lease term granted to the Golf Club had been for a period of just seven years. This did not confer upon the fledgling club a great measure of security of tenure. This all changed for the better in 1944 when the Crown appointed a Racecourse Domain Board to administer the area on behalf of the Crown. This Board was given the authority to lease any part of the Reserve ‘at reasonable rental and under reasonable conditions for a term not exceeding 33 years at any one time.’ This 33 year lease

term was a big step towards permanence for the Club as the original lease term of just seven years did not allow the members to outlay capital in the confidence that they would always be able to lease the area and thus continue to derive benefit from the investment they had made in developing the land and associated buildings.

In 1956 an additional area of a little more than two hectares to the side of the third fairway was purchased by the Crown and incorporated into the area leased to the Golf Club. As a result the Reserve now contains a fraction over 83 hectares of which 34 hectares is leased to Racing Tauranga, 45 hectares to the Golf Club, the remainder being Reserve land adjoining the Golf Club boundaries. A further area adjacent to the third fairway was incorporated into the Club’s lease in 2010 and the land developed as a turf nursery. In the present era 45 hectares would not be considered sufficient by some margin for the establishment of an 18-hole golf course.

The Domain Board remained our Landlord until 1989 when the Local Government Commission proposed that the function, duties and powers of the Domain Board as an administrative body be transferred to the Tauranga District Council. The Board unsuccessfully objected but did manage to get lease terms amended to provide that the rental shall, in the event of dispute, be assessed on the current market
