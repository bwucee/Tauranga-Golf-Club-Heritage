Veteran men’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

THE LADIES

Our association with the Ladies’ Club goes back as many years as we have existed, as far back in fact as to our earliest Tournaments when Margaret Mowat volunteered to handle the catering before any sort of regular canteen existed. The Ladies always helped at the tournaments with afternoon teas and Mrs Wardrop will be remembered for her kindness in providing baskets of goodies for our midweek games, and also for the wonderful floral arrangements she set up in the clubhouse.

The first organised matches between the ladies and ourselves were played for a shield that still resides somewhere in the clubhouse. The ladies could not find a way to register a win for the shield and the contest gradually faded away. In those early times the two groups alternated the supply of afternoon tea and the results were impressive. As a fuller catering service became available at the Club it became inappropriate to continue the self catering and those marvellous afternoon teas became a distant memory.

More recently we have established ‘Melbourne Cup Day’ when the Vets entertain the Ladies at a golf match held on Cup Day, the first Tuesday in November. The Ladies compete for a Cup presented by Mr and Mrs Tom Dick. The Ladies have christened it the ‘Fillies Cup’, while the men play for a tankard engraved ‘Co-operation 1972’. This was given to the Vets by the Ladies in appreciation of the

assistance given to them during the staging of the Russell Grace Inter Provincial Tournament held in Tauranga that year. The day is an important one in our calendar and involves sweepstakes on the Cup and a social afternoon culminating in a live showing of the big race. As a measure of appreciation for the co-operation shown the Vets by the Ladies Club we presented them with a tray for competition amongst themselves. Each year we send a cheque to provide a personal prize for the winner. This spirit of co-operation engendered so many years ago is still as vibrant today. As an example we relinquish a Tuesday so the Ladies can stage their Championships and, a few weeks later, they give up one of their days so that we can stage the Mark Trophy fixture with the Mount Maunganui Vets.

May such happy associations long continue.

Editor’s Note: This arrangement no longer applies as circumstances changed in 2013 so as to make it untenable.

THE PRESENT

From 1970, when Ken McIntyre was President, the Society began to grow dramatically and this growth was indisputably tied to the general influx of retirees to the Tauranga area and to the reputation and popularity of Veterans’ golf at the Tauranga Golf Club. In three years our numbers increased from 50 to 100 and the old starting board disc system, designed for just 48 people, became taxed to its limit. Eventually our members became frustrated with starting delays and waiting times of up to two hours before they were able to tee off. When membership reached 120 there was no choice but to institute an advance weekly starting sheet. Many members disliked this system feeling it might destroy the friendly social atmosphere that arose when everyone arrived and left together.

Fortunately this did not prove to be the case.
