Women’s golf

LYN GOODHEW began her golfing career in 1982 at the Springfield Club in Rotorua before moving back to Tauranga a year later. Within two years she was elected Ladies Vice-Captain, a position she held for two years before moving up to Captain for the following two years. In the established tradition that had ensured continuity of policy over many years Lyn went on to serve as Ladies President for the two years ending in November 2001. Ever willing to serve she substituted for President Robyn Anderson when the latter was absent for a part of the following year.

In 2003 Lyn became actively involved with Nine-hole golf by assisting new players to understand the Rules and etiquette of the game following on from the clinics provided to them by the Club professionals. In 2008 she began serving as assistant to Alison Holdem in the administration of the Nine-hole section of Women’s Golf.

ALISON HOLDEM learned to play the game in 1983 at the Ngawaro Club on Hugh and Biddy McCarroll’s sheep and cattle farm at Pyes Pa. Alison held the position of Club Captain at Ngawaro and won the senior championship there in 1991. The Ngawaro Club was established by Hugh and existed for a considerable number of years before the property was sold and the new owners were not prepared to continue the course. Many members moved to the new Renner Park course that had then become established in Oropi Road. Alison joined Tauranga in 1985 while still retaining membership at Ngawaro and soon moved onto the committee, being elected Vice-captain in November 1993, Captain in 1995, and President in 1997.

Almost immediately upon completing these six years of service to the Women’s committee Alison was elected to represent the Central Zone on the BOP/TV Association. She completed a six-year term in that role in 2006. Alison became involved in the Nine-hole program with Phyll Sandlant from 1998 to 2008 before taking over as Nine-hole Convenor when Phyll retired from that position. In addition to her work for Women’s golf Alison served a term as a member of the Board of Management.

ANNA BRABYN grew up in a farming district east of Whanganui called Mangamahu where there was a country course of just twelve holes and featuring the usual small fenced greens. These were a bit trickier than those usually encountered on such courses as they were electrified and hence extra caution was

required near them. The course was sometimes closed when necessary farming operations were being performed as the course was laid out on a private farm. Both Anna’s father and his sister represented New Zealand and won National titles so it was inevitable that Anna should follow them in playing the game. She was a member at Mangamahu from 1971 until 1994 winning the Senior championship on 18 occasions. While still a member there she joined Whanganui’s Belmont Golf Club in 1977 in order to further her game. There she won the championship on a number of occasions and also furthered her administrative experience through Club Captaincy and a term of four years on the Board of Management.
