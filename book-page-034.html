The original Gate Pa links

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

NUMBER 7 - THE PAVILION - PAR 4

This was played from a tee near the present tenth green to where the practice putting green now lies.

NUMBER 8 - THE PA - PAR 4

‘The Pa’ was not unlike the present first hole except that the tee was further west towards the second green. The trundler shed now covers that old tee.

NUMBER 9 - THE RIDGE - PAR 4

The ninth was the present second hole now called The Summit though these names, with exceptions such as The Knoll and The Gully, are now seldom used. The only significant changes to the hole have been to the tee, once just a narrow shelf cut into the bank, and the establishment of the alternative green to the right of the original. The ladies played from a forward tee between the first and second fairways.

NUMBER 10 - THE DROP - PAR 3

To get to this tee the players walked around the top of the slope to a tee situated west of the present seventh tee. They were then faced with a shot across a field of turnips to the present third green. It was very difficult finding a ball falling short amongst the turnips as all turnips look very much alike at close quarters and your carefully noted reference point was easily lost. Eventually one season, when the stock had eaten this fodder crop, the hole was eliminated and pine trees planted on the slope.

NUMBER 11 - THE HUMMOCKS - PAR 4

This was a tricky dogleg that was essentially the present third in reverse. The second shot was to a small green tucked into the slope below the third tee. The shape of that green can still be discerned today. It is just as well present day players do not have to drag their trundlers up the hill while playing

that hole!

NUMBER 12 - MOUNT VIEW - PAR 5

The present seventh hole played to the right hand green. As the name suggests the hole played straight towards The Mount. This landmark could be seen much more clearly before the growth of trees eliminated much of the view.

NUMBER 13 - LONE PINE - PAR 4

This hole ran towards the access road and was essentially the present eighth hole. A line of power poles was about 20 metres in from the fence line on the boundary and frequently came into play to the consternation of many a player. On one occasion after this became the eighth hole following course re-organisation a tee shot hit the first of these poles only to fly back over the player’s head and settle in the bunker beside the seventh green. The player faced a daunting second shot as no local rule allowed a replay in such situations. No one seems to recall what happened to the ‘lone pine’ after which the hole was named.
