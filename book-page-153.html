GLEANINGS FROM THE CLUB MINUTE BOOKS

As noted earlier no Minute Books from the first 40 years or so exist to this day. Perhaps this is not the serious problem that it might seem to be at first glance. I say this because, having pored over dusty minutes from as far back as possible, the overwhelming impression has been one of the enormous amount of trivia that has occupied the time of committee members throughout the years. While this is not said to disparage the work successive committees have done it would be obvious to anyone going through the books that they do not truly reflect the value of that work. Much of the important work of a committee is done at sub-committee level and is not recorded in the full committee minutes.

The following excerpts have been selected to provide a timeline of events that have had some significance in the history of our Club. 1954: The new Clubhouse was completed in its present position at a cost of £5750. The furnishing committee raised another £1034 to purchase tables and chairs. The AGM reaffirmed that the required dress for men in the Clubhouse during the season was to include a jacket or Club blazer. The committee were to decide on the colour and style of the blazer to be adopted as the official Club blazer. 1956: £25 was allocated to assist the Greenkeeper to attend the NZ Greenkeepers’ Conference in Dunedin. Imported sand was tried in our bunkers for the first time.

The Labour Weekend tournament fee was set at £1:10:0, meals not included. 1957: Noel Hockin replaced Col Evitt as Club Secretary. It was resolved that schoolboys could play in the school holidays for 2/6d per week but only if accompanied by a member. The price of the traditional meal of ‘pea, pie, and potatoes’ was to increase from 1/6d to 2/3d. 1960: Tauranga invited Te Puke and Mt. Maunganui Clubs to send two representatives each to play in our fixture with the Waikato Eagles. 1961: Subscriptions were set at £12 for men, £10 for women, and £20 for a married couple. Trials were conducted with the 1.68-inch American ball in New Zealand.

It was found acceptable. 1962: USGA Handicapping system adopted. Membership was closed for the first time but reopened a month later. 1963: The causeway across the gully at the 4th hole was raised to provide a level crossing. Trials were conducted with Papamoa sea sand in the bunkers. Research was conducted on the feasibility of using water from the Judea stream. It was found to be much cheaper to remain on Borough supply. No positive results had been gained from trial drilling for water. 1964: Entry fee for Labour Weekend Tournament set at £2:10. This included three lunches at 5/- each. A profit of £70 was made. 1965: The committee approved Professional charges of £1:10 pa for club storage, £2:10 for storage and cleaning, and £3:10 if you wished to include shoe cleaning.

Greens committee reported fairways had been mowed in August for the first time in 6 weeks. The BOPDGA had been advised we were concerned that some local clubs were not applying the handicapping rules properly. C G Wilson Ltd. were the successful tenderers for Clubhouse alterations, their tender being for £15 586.
