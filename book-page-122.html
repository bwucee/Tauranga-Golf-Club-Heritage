At the Society’s 25th Jubilee Tournament Prize Giving the 1977 President, Bill Sanders, presented Miss Mark with a Silver Coffee Pot in recognition of her 25 years of wonderful service as Tournament Secretary. All living Past Presidents subscribed to this handsome gift. This thoughtful and generous act is worthy of record and in Chronological order those who contributed were Past Presidents Ken McIntyre, Kevin Thomson, Eric Blomfield, Ossie Simmonds, Cliff Colson, Len Woods, and Ken Golden. During the presentation Club President Alex Gallagher also paid tribute to Miss Mark and she replied to all those present in her usual charming and eloquent manner.

Regular Old Timers attending our tournaments have been Barney Barnett (Waikato), Eddie Edwards (Christchurch), Jim Forbes (Mirimar), and Alf Sharpe (Auckland). The latter played his last Tauranga tournament at the age of ninety. Over the years many a character has played in our Tournament and some very amusing incidents have arisen but time and space prevent us delving too deeply into these. However one Old Timer who wished to remain anonymous sent in the following anecdote that we felt may prove useful to present day veterans. We Quote: “From the third tee this player drove his ball straight into the scrub which then extended across much of the hill and was very difficult to extricate a ball from.

He then played a provisional ball that was a screamer right between the two pines at the bottom, one of which is now defunct. This left a short Iron to the green. A search for the original was made and his ball located. The player did some quick thinking. He would require one stroke, or even two, to get in the clear, then another towards the gap with no guarantee of success. His silent conclusion: I can’t better my provisional and might do worse. His response followed in seconds. “That is not my ball. Can’t hold up the field. Let’s press on.” So saying he played to the green, sunk the putt and halved the hole in fives.

His opponent, of course, was up one good Dunlop 65. Little wonder so many of us found it difficult to make the grade in Veteran’s Tournaments!

THE OLD TIMERS

In our earlier text we made mention of those men who founded our Society and, as there were so many interesting characters amongst them, no history would be complete without some short biographical details of them, and of those who followed in their footsteps.

JOHN McKENZIE Our first President, John was a recent arrival from Scotland. He was liable to suddenly appear quite

unexpectedly in full Highland Dress. He was President from 1953-56, again in 1960 and 1964, and yet again in 1967, seven times in all. A golfing stylist John hit a good, straight ball with a minimum of effort. As a raconteur of no mean talent he loved the telling of a tale. His mellifluous Scottish accent had undoubted appeal and his training as a Minister in the Church of Scotland ensured his delivery commanded attention. John was one of the first Vets Ken McIntyre played with upon first joining the Society and he recalls John addressing him all day as “young Man”. Ken was then 58 years old! In later years John lived alone and when asked how he fared in the kitchen his response was: “My boy, I have many friends upon whom I call not long before dinner, but early enough to allow them time to put another potato in the pot.
