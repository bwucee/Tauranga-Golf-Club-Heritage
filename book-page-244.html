1970 - THE EMPEROR RULES

Otago Professional Alistair Palmer led with a 65 on the first day but a crowd of Australians, led by then five times British Open Champion Peter Thomson, loomed in his wake. This threat soon became reality as Thomson added a 66 to his opening 68. Another seven Australians were within two strokes of his lead. Three strokes further back Stuart Jones and Aucklander Bob Gillespie, who had aced the gully during his opening round, headed the Amateurs. During his round American Jesse Vaughn scored what must rank as one of the more spectacular eagles to have been recorded on the course. Playing the sixth hole Vaughn’s tee shot found the fairway bunker that used to feature some twenty metres short of the tenth green.

From there a magnificent stroke sent the ball to the heart of the sixth green where a five-metre putt completed an unlikely eagle. Many years later local man Josh Geary, runner up to Gavin Smillie in our Senior Championship final when just a schoolboy, went one better by holing out with a three iron from another now defunct fairway bunker for an albatross two on our 13th hole during the annual under-23 age group tournament held on the course.

Surprisingly Peter Thomson, after his strong start, dropped away in the third round leaving three other Australians, Vic Bennets, Paul Morgan, and Barry Coxon, as the main contenders moving into the afternoon’s final round. No one was picking Stuart Jones to pose a threat to the leaders despite his steady rounds of 68, 71 and 70. While it is now unheard of for Professionals to be required to play two rounds on the final day of a tournament it was common practice in earlier years and the fourth round would fully test the mental and physical resources of all the contestants during a hot Tauranga day in January.

Jones, playing in a threesome with Coxon and Wolstenholme, birdied the two long holes on the front nine to be out in 33 making up just one of the three strokes by which he trailed Coxon. Meanwhile Wolstenholme overtook them both only to fall away on the more difficult second nine holes as many another has done before and since. It was not to be until the 16th that Jones was able to draw level with Coxon and, with Bennets also fading over the closing holes, it was down to these two to fight out the finish. To the delight of the gallery it was the Amateur who prevailed, his closing 66 being too good for Coxon’s even par 70.

By the end of this round some players were suffering quite markedly from heat exhaustion or possible sunstroke and it was considered unlikely that competitors would ever again be scheduled to play 36 holes in one day under these conditions. The difficult conditions seemingly did not adversely trouble Australian David Graham who climbed many places up the leader board with a closing 64. Graham went on to join the PGA Tour in the USA where he won the USPGA Championship in 1979 and their Open Championship two years later. He later became known as “the club maker to the pros” designing and fitting clubs to suit each individual.
