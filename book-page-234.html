AMATEURS SHOW THE WAY IN 1960

Alf Relph was again successful in the tournament of 1960. This event began in atrocious conditions under which most courses would have been virtually unplayable. Despite persistent heavy rain and gusty northerly winds Relph returned a remarkable score of just one over par. Bay of Plenty team mate D K (Don) Boone of Springfield was only one stroke behind. Sunday brought much more favourable conditions and a good sized gallery saw Relph drop only one further stroke over the two rounds to finish on 215. Boone also maintained form to finish a clear second on 218. Professional winner John Kelly of Auckland finished some distance behind these two but he did improve markedly on the 77 returned under the trying conditions of the previous day.

PROFESSIONAL PRIDE RESTORED IN 1961

Auckland professional, and regular participant, Bob Jackson led a quartet of professionals who relegated the amateurs to the lower spots on the Leader Board in the 1961 renewal of the event. The tournament attracted a strong field of 16 professionals and 56 amateurs. Jackson highlighted the weekend with a hole in one at the fourth hole, “The Gully”, on the Sunday afternoon. It has long been said that if you play the par threes well at Tauranga you will have a good round. This may be self-evident from the very fact that there are five of them here while most courses have only the standard four. The adage was surely true of Jackson on that afternoon for his aggregate of 12, or three under par, on the par threes led to a three under round of 68.

With earlier rounds of 72 and 73 Bob won by four strokes from Noel Hayden, a good friend of Tauranga golf and a man who did a great deal for Junior Golf, first from his base in Rotorua and later in Auckland. It was good to see Bob Jackson succeeding as he had been a strong supporter of the tournament from the first staging.

Eldon Carswell of Rotorua who had almost certainly been one to benefit from Noel Hayden’s interest in juniors, led the amateurs with a rather uninspired total of 13 over par for the 54 holes. Carswell himself was heard to say he would have been much happier had he shot three seventies and finished second. Another prominent BOP representative in Ivan Deadman of Rotorua finished one stroke behind Carswell with Don Boone making it a Rotorua trifecta a further stroke back.

1962 - THE WOMEN ARE INVITED

Always willing to be innovative the organising committee decided to invite women (though they were

still called lady golfers at the time) to the tournament. This was believed to be the first time women had been invited to participate in an open tournament of this nature in New Zealand. The initiative was very warmly received by the low handicap women invited. In the event itself local champion Miss Una Wickham had to bow to Mrs Jean Mangan of Palmerston North. The two were separated by a single stroke, Miss Wickham uncharacteristically missing a short putt at the last hole that would have tied the pair. Both women were New Zealand representatives so the aim of the promoters to provide competition for these women prior to the Tasman Cup series proved to be successful.
