Continuing the story of early tribulations suffered by Junior Boys we recall an incident from the Sixties. Two juniors, Wayne Holden and Brent Sutherland, both still at College, were playing the quarter final of the Club Senior Championship. All square after 18 holes they set off to the 19th tee. When the Captain informed the men waiting to tee off at the first hole for their afternoon round that the boys would need to play through the

comments of some of those men waiting to tee off were to the effect that “we are not standing aside for a couple of juniors”. The Captain made it very clear that that was exactly what they were required to do. After this distressing confrontation, the boys were clearly uncomfortable playing the hole, and from memory, scored a five and six to complete the match.

In Michael Nicholson’s junior days the Clubhouse was divided into two distinct areas,

the linoleum covered dining area and the carpeted lounge. The rule was that juniors were not permitted on the carpeted area. This was presumably to protect them from the evils of the Bar or perhaps the risqué stories and language of the senior members. By dint of his ability and behaviour Michael had been invited to join the playing category termed Boys’ A, thus enabling him to play on Saturday afternoon with the men. As Michael was still not permitted on the carpeted area the three men playing with him each week would position a table such that they could sit on the carpeted area while Michael’s chair sat on the linoleum.

Thus the rule was complied with.

How times have changed. Juniors today are given every encouragement to develop their game and to participate fully in Club activities. They qualify for our representative Pennant teams, including at senior level where they often excel. It is to be hoped they will appreciate that the privileges they now enjoy were hard earned by their predecessors and by those adult members who recognised the need to change the culture that had long treated junior members as deserving of little in the way of encouragement

This was achieved by softening the ball so that drives in excess of 250m by professionals and top amateurs became the exception rather than the rule. Few players could reach a hole of 450m or more in two strokes. This regulation was introduced in 2020 though the manufacturers challenged it all the way through the courts, ultimately without success. Golf ball manufacturers were understandably reluctant to see any restriction on ball design as they had so much invested in research and development. The Callaway Company alone spent 170 million $US in developing their first ball termed the Rule 35 ball.

To compete with one another to produce a ball that is fast and low spinning off the drive, yet has a high spin rate when struck with a short iron, the competing companies employed people who previously worked as aerospace engineers or polymer and rubber chemists.
