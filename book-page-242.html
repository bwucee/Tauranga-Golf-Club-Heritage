On that first day in 1968 six under par rounds of 65 were scored by Billy Dunk and Denis Sullivan (North Shore), 66s from Bob Stanton (NSW), Walter Godfrey (Auckland) and John Croskery (Remuera), and 67s from Len Thomas (Western Australia) and Terry Kendall (Manor Park). Sullivan’s round included an Out of Bounds penalty at the thirteenth so a 63 had certainly been a possibility. The long hitting Dutchman, Martin Roesink, who had attracted very large galleries during the first two rounds was well down the field with rounds of 72 and 71. As a result the fans had deserted him when he embarked on his third and final round.

That was unfortunate as they would have been treated to some wonderful entertainment. Roesink showed his true ability with a faultless 63, four under on each nine, to wrest the record from John Lister. It was claimed that Roesink drove the third green during this round but only his playing companions and their caddies were witness to the feat. Driving the third green was unheard of at that time yet today a number of club players drive across the trees in the direction of the green and occasionally one will reach it. Martin Roesink was later to try his luck in the United States where at least one Golf magazine claimed he was the longest hitter on the circuit.

Unfortunately the rest of his game seldom reached the standard of his driving, perhaps because in his own words he “kept looking around to see who was on the course”. The handsome Dutchman was certainly popular with female spectators and it may be understandable that his concentration was sometimes less than 100% on his game. A further example of his hitting ability was in evidence one day when he drove into the seventh fairway when playing the sixth. From there he lofted a nine iron over the pines and into the heart of the green. This was before the advent of the one-piece ball and metal drivers and, no, the pines were no less lofty than they are today.

In spite of missing a ‘tiddler’ on the 54th and final green Billy Dunk, a man renowned for setting course records in Australia, completed immaculate rounds of 65, 66, and 67 to defeat Tasmanian Bob Shaw whose closing 64 left him two strokes in arrears. Ted McDougall was an impressive winner of the Amateur division with rounds of 69, 66, and 66. In all 24 competitors aggregated par or better through the 54 holes.

MORE FIRSTS IN THE 1969 SPALDING MASTERS

The organising committee were delighted to accept the offer of the Spalding Company to have the great American Professional Miss Marilynn Smith play in the 1969 tournament scheduled to be played over 72 holes for the first time. It was no accident that Miss Smith was drawn to play in a dream grouping with New Zealand’s best Amateur and Professional, Stuart Jones and Bob Charles respectively. This was the first occasion that a woman had played in men’s company in any Open Tournament in New Zealand and proved extremely popular with the crowds that thronged the course. Playing before a gallery estimated at 600 these three all played excellent golf.
