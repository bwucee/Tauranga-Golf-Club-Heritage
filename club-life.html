Early Gate Pa years · dates not specified

Making a club from a tin shed

Working bees, shared lunches and the seasonal rhythm of golf sustained the early club.

The book describes a corrugated iron shed shared with the racing club as the first clubhouse at the reserve. A long table and forms occupied most of the room; primus stoves and kettles supplied tea.

Members brought paper-wrapped lunches, and many cycled to the course with clubs on their backs. A cookhouse and stove came later, although the passage does not give a precise year for the change.

Golf was largely a winter activity. In summer, the fairways produced hay; greens were prepared for a new season in March. Grazing animals and an occasional horse-drawn mower helped maintain the playing areas.

The women’s committee catered for visiting players under difficult conditions. Mowat records meals prepared at home and brought to the club, alongside the work of cleaning and preparing the shed.

These passages describe the early Gate Pa years broadly. They are included as context, rather than assigned to a particular year between 1914 and 1923. The accompanying golfing-party photograph is also undated and is not identified as a view of this shed.
