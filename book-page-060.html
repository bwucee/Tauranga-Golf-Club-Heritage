Men’s golf

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

← Previous pageBook p. 60 / 305Next page →
From the book: original views, recollections and terminology are retained. References to “today”, rules, staff or programmes describe the time of writing. Text extraction is being reviewed; use the page reference when suggesting a correction.

From the earliest days, when the Club based itself at the Domain, Saturday afternoon was competition time for those men who wished to exercise their competitive instincts. So it has remained although the growth in membership in the second half of the Twentieth Century resulted in competition play soon occupying Saturday mornings as well as afternoons throughout the playing season. The season has gradually been extended from an original eight or nine months until competition golf is now held almost throughout the year.

At first competition was rather informal with the Secretary or Captain deciding on the day what form the competition would take. The choice lay between Medal, Par, and Match Play, with some variation by way of playing singly or in pairs. Foursomes, including mixed foursomes, were popular. From the beginning match play, including the ubiquitous haggle, was the most popular form of competition. The Stableford scoring system had not then been invented. It was not until 1931 that Dr Frank Stableford of the Wallasley Golf Club near Liverpool developed the system, perhaps in response to his habit of destroying a good Stroke Play round with one or two bad holes.

In doing so he gave new hope to generations of players afflicted with the same unfortunate habit.

As the Club membership grew a variety of trophies were donated. This was often to introduce a new competition or to encourage its adoption. Consequently the Club program grew larger as the years went by and it became increasingly difficult to fit everything in. At one time in the 1960s and 70s some competitions were designated as Sunday competitions to alleviate that problem. While this proved popular with some there were many who found it difficult, or impossible, to play both days of the weekend because of family or work commitments and the change was eventually reversed.

While competition within the Club flourished Tauranga men made little impact at Provincial or National level. Perhaps N P (Norman) Green who won the Tauranga Open Championship against all comers in 1938, 39, and 40 was the first Club member to achieve significant victories against outside opposition.

For those who find the Knoll difficult, bear in mind that Joan Mirrielees and Maurice Bradmore each aced the hole on two separate occasions. Some claim Brownie Carnachan did even better by scoring three ones on that hole in the one day. During the Labour Weekend Tournament one year he holed in one at the Knoll in the morning round then amassed an 11 in the afternoon!

On occasion even the very best can make a real mess of The Knoll. In one Spalding Masters Tournament Kel Nagle moved from winning contention after scoring no less than seven at the hole. I believe a sand fly got into his eye at the precise moment when he was attempting to recover from down the side of the green though Kel was never one to seek

an excuse for such a lapse.
