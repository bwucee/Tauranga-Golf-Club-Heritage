1952–1959

The beginnings of the Veterans’ Society

Friendly rounds grew into an organised society and an ambitious annual tournament.

The Veterans’ history traces the group’s origins to informal games after the Second World War. In 1952 it became an organised association, with John McKenzie as president and Dick Phelan as secretary.

In November 1953, a group of just 23 members staged a five-day tournament. Anne Mark and Rennie Gordon assisted with the organisation, and the event established a pattern that would continue for many years.

The group adopted the name Veterans’ Society in the mid-1950s to distinguish itself from the national association. In 1955 its summer programme included the Angus Plate, donated by Gavin Wallace. Veterans’ day changed to Thursday in 1957.

Tauranga hosted the national veterans’ championship in 1959. E. S. Toogood of Poverty Bay won, with Tauranga’s Frank Macklow runner-up. These events give the Senior Men collection an early foundation; later service projects and present-day activities can be added as separate records.

Much of this section of the book reproduces a 1977 history compiled by Bill Sanders and Ken McIntyre. The main narrative dates the first five-day tournament to 1953; a later passage mentions a 1951 tournament. This preview follows the explicit 1953 account and leaves the inconsistent reference noted for review.
