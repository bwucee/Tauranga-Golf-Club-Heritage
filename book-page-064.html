Keith had his moments as a golfer as well as an administrator. In 1979 Keith contested the final of the Junior Championship and, to the delight of all, he was successful against Kevin Moynahan who was generally well ahead of Keith on the drive. Kevin was almost as pleased as everyone else to have Keith enjoy this success on the course where his Match Committee work had enabled so many others to enjoy their competitions over the years. In 1968 Keith won the Nicoll Cup. On the first Saturday of this 36-hole par competition Keith shot 84- 21-63 to be 7 up on par. On the second Saturday Keith’s daughter was to be married and Keith had not intended playing golf.

His friend and wedding guest Vince O’Connor persuaded him they should play at dawn on the Saturday and still have ample time to prepare for the wedding. And so they did with Keith this time scoring an 86 to be 5 up on par. Is there anyone else who has bettered 12 up against par over 36 holes? When the horror of Parkinson’s disease caught up with him Keith had to give up playing the game he so enjoyed but it did not end his association with the Club. For many years he continued to arrive prior to midday on Saturday to start the afternoon field. His extensive knowledge of the organisation of Saturday competition play was frequently called upon, and greatly appreciated, by successive match

committees. Keith’s unobtrusive manner, his wisdom and experience, and his unfailing loyalty to his Club and to his colleagues made him an exceptional member.

Alex Gallagher joined the Club in 1938 and remained a member for more than 60 years. In 1972 he was elected Vice President, progressing to the Chair in 1974, filling that position for three years. In the days of manual score boards Alex’ special skills were much in demand. A surveyor by profession, Alex produced scoreboards that were works of art. His calligraphic skills became legend in the Club. As co-ordinator of scoring information from around the course during the Spalding Masters, the PGA Championship, and the Russell Grace Inter Provincial Tournament, Alex was unsurpassed. It never crossed anyone’s mind that anyone other than Alex would fill that role when important tournaments were held.

Alex won the Junior Championship in 1989. There was a certain irony in the victory. The previous season Mark Mangos joined the Club shortly before the championship qualifying rounds were due to be held. Mark had been on a 7 handicap previously but had been out of the game for a few years and his handicap had lapsed. Keen to play in the championships Mark got in three quick rounds. These qualified him for a 17 handicap and thus a place in the Junior Championship field. Mark, knowing he was better than that, requested a handicap that would allow him to play in the Intermediate division. His pleas fell on the deaf ears of handicapper Alex Gallagher and so Mark qualified for the Junior Championship.

Making easy work of the early rounds he met none other than Alex in the final and prevailed with little trouble. He was possibly the most able winner of that title in its history. Perhaps it was poetic justice when Alex gained his success the following year. Earlier in 1988 Alex won the Junior Stroke Play Championship. Alex was elected to Life Membership in 1988.
