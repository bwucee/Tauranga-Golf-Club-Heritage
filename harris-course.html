1967 onwards

Subtle changes, a lasting influence

John Harris’s advice helped shape the course without turning it into something its members would no longer recognise.

In 1967 the club invited British golf architect Commander John Harris to inspect the course. His report described changes intended to improve a club course rather than force it into the mould of a long championship layout.

Greens chairman Alan Davidson and superintendent Grant Silvester walked the course with Harris. The book describes Davidson preparing models from the advice and Silvester and his team putting the proposals into practice, with help from Garth Renner.

The changes concentrated on lines of play, bunkering and mounding. Dave’s account values their subtlety and the variety offered by the existing ground.

The original chapter also carries a hole-by-hole history and later reflections on design. Those passages remain available in the book collection, rather than being compressed into a single date on the timeline.
