The Ward dynasty

David G. S. Mowat, editor · Published 2014

Few families, if any, have had such a long and fruitful association with the Tauranga Golf club as has the Ward family. It began in 1947 when F W (Fred) Ward was appointed manager of the Tauranga Branch of Mackenzie’s and he and his wife, Olwyn, moved to the Town. At that time Mackenzie’s and Woolworth’s were intense chain store rivals located in just about every New Zealand town of any size. They sold general merchandise at reasonable prices and perhaps were equivalent to the “Five and Ten Cent” stores of the USA. Woolworth’s was an American company while Mackenzie’s was an all New Zealand enterprise established by Mr R A McKenzie.

Fred began caddying at the Hokowhitu course of the Manawatu Golf Club at the tender age of seven. Fortunately bags were not the heavier bags of today as trundlers had not then been introduced. Fred must have been well regarded by his employers as he rose rapidly through the caddy ranks to earn the badge awarded to the number one caddy, a badge he was very proud to have been presented with. Like a lot of top professional and amateur players of those earlier times Fred learnt the skills and etiquette of the game by observing the actions of the best players he caddied for. Throughout his long career Fred never had a lesson, his smooth, rhythmical swing being developed as the result of emulating the best of what he observed.

That remains a great way to develop a sound swing and young players should take every opportunity to watch the best players, amateur or professional, male or female, whenever they get the chance.

Fred was successful in winning the Senior Championship at Hokowhitu before moving to Tauranga where he immediately demonstrated his class by winning the Senior Championship in that first year of 1947. He was to win it on another six occasions between 1950 and 1956, his seven victories being second only to those of Michael Nicholson who won the Championship twelve times between 1970 and 1991. In addition to his Club Championship successes Fred won the Tauranga Open Championship four years in a row from 1947, and again in 1953 and 1958. These six victories place him ahead of D K (Don) Boone, of Springfield, who won the title on five occasions between 1956 and 1963.

The Tauranga Open Championship was the premier event at the annual Labour Weekend Tournament that was first played in 1934. Apart from the War years of 1941 to 1945 when no tournament was held this extremely popular event attracted top players from Auckland, the Waikato, together with those from the Bay of Plenty and further afield. Sadly it fell victim to a new era where players are reluctant to travel away from their home clubs for tournaments such as this and it was discontinued following the 1999 event. At its peak the tournament attracted full fields of 112 divided into seven groups of sixteen for match play.

At Inter-Provincial level Fred represented the Bay of Plenty with distinction in four Freyberg Rose Bowl Tournaments. This tournament has gone by a number of names since, depending on the company sponsoring it. The Freyberg Rose Bowl has now taken a new role as the trophy attached to the “Masters” Inter-Provincial Tournament for the stars of an earlier age. Fred’s lowest handicap was +1, though he did feel the handicapper was a bit harsh in making him give the course that one stroke.

In 1949 Fred encouraged Olwyn to join the Club and they were active and popular members until 1962 when Fred was promoted to Hastings where he joined the Hastings Golf Club at Bridge Pa. This was the home of Stuart Jones who left no room for any pretender to his throne thus ending Fred’s championship winning run.
