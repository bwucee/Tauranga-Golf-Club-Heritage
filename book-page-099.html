She was rather proud of the fact that when she first played the Poverty Bay Golf Club’s Awapuni Links she gained her official handicap, thus proving that the play at home had enabled her to develop a swing that stood up to the standards of the Town players.

Soon after arriving in Tauranga Margaret was asked to take on the Secretarial role with the Ladies’ Committee which she did. She recalled that they met at Violet Maltby’s home in 13th. Avenue. Claire Mayfield was the Captain and “dear old Elsie Hanna” was President. Margaret recalled the era as a happy time with the Ladies working together to put on wonderful spreads when hosting visiting teams. Mrs. Hanna was famed for the wonderful Potato salads she produced and no one could make and add the dressing quite like her. Myrtle Ripley was especially instrumental in the drive to establish a new clubhouse in 1938. Margaret was still playing regularly two or three times a week, and to a very creditable level, until well into her eighties.

Throughout she pulled her own clubs and she and her regular Monday group of similarly aged ladies played at a pace that would outstrip many younger players of today. In 1978 Anne Mark and Rennie Wallis nominated Margaret for Life Membership. The committee sought further information and nothing eventually came of the nomination.

RENNIE DAINES will best be remembered as Mrs Rennie Gordon while much later in life she became Mrs Wallis. As Miss Daines Rennie won the Ladies’ Championship three times in succession from 1920 to 1922. She served three years as Captain in the Nineteen Forties at which time she also became Mrs Gordon. Some ten years later she served a three-year term as Ladies’ President. A strong supporter of the Club from its early years at Gate Pa Rennie continued her association with, and support of, the Club throughout her life. She was the second Woman to be elected to Life Membership following the election of Elsie Hanna.

NELL SILSON joined the Club in the thirties. She lived at Katikati and drove to golf and home again after the day’s play. Club competitions were then held on Saturday afternoons. The road to Katikati was rough and particularly treacherous in winter. Driving home on dark winter evenings must have been a real challenge yet Nell seldom missed her Club Day.

JOYCE CARNACHAN joined in 1931 and served on committee for many years during which she

organised the catering. She served the Women’s committee as Captain and later as President. Joyce was a selector for the Russell Grace Teams and in 1963 was elected to the executive committee of the TVBOP Ladies Golf Association, serving as Vice President until 1967. In 1972 the Tauranga Golf Club elected Joyce to Life Membership in recognition of her service to the Club over many years. Those who played with Joyce remembered her with affection and with not a little envy for her special touch with the putter.
