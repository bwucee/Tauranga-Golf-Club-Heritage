1904–1913

Golf between the Domain and the sea

Rugby players, tidal water and grazing animals were part of the challenge on Tauranga’s first course.

The early course began at Wharepai Domain and extended below The Elms towards Sulphur Point. The book describes a route through shared recreation grounds and low-lying coastal land, with a tin shed serving as a clubhouse.

One hole crossed the rugby ground. Another led to a green surrounded by water at high tide, reached by stepping-stones. The hazards were inseparable from the landscape and its other uses.

A surviving course card reproduced in the book records nine holes totalling 1,860 yards, with a bogey rating of 38. The longest hole, The Camp, measured 420 yards. Bogey was then used in the sense now associated with par.

The card is useful evidence, but its exact date is unresolved. Mowat notes that one hole is named Anzac, which raises a question about how that name fits the card’s presumed early date. It should not be treated as an independently dated 1904 document.

Conditions also changed over time: the 1908 meeting report describes a competition requiring two rounds of an eight-hole course. The book speculates about the missing hole; this preview leaves the reason open.
